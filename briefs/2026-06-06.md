## What Happened

### The worst day of 2026: rates and chips broke together.

The May employment report landed Friday at +172,000 jobs, more than double the roughly 80,000 the Street expected, with unemployment steady at 4.3 percent. Good news became bad news: futures swung from pricing rate cuts to about 70 percent odds of a hike by year-end, the ten-year Treasury yield rose to 4.54 percent, and the dollar firmed. The shock landed on the third day of the Broadcom-led sell-off in AI-spending names and pulled out the cushion that had let Thursday's damage rotate rather than liquidate. The S&P 500 fell 2.64 percent, the NASDAQ 4.18 percent, and the semiconductor index 9.22 percent — its sharpest day in over six years. Treasuries sold off with stocks and gold fell to a 2026 low; only cash and the dollar held.

### Memory took the brunt on a rumor that misses its core.

The portfolio's memory names led the fall. Micron Technology (MU), the largest American maker of memory chips, dropped 13.25 percent — the worst move in the book and steeper than the routed chip tape itself; SanDisk (SNDK) fell 11.39 percent and Seagate Technology (STX) 8.48 percent. The proximate cause was a SemiAnalysis report that NVIDIA had halved a memory configuration on its next-generation Rubin racks — but the report was single-sourced, partly walked back, and explicitly does not touch the high-bandwidth memory that is the sold-out, contracted core of Micron's thesis. At about eight times forward earnings these names are not expensive; this was the most-crowded corner of the AI trade unwinding into a rate shock, not a demand crack. The 24 June print is where positioning meets fundamentals.

### Meta slid into the zone the principal flagged.

Meta Platforms (META) fell 5.51 percent after the Financial Times reported the company is weighing a new equity sale to fund an AI build-out it has guided to $125–145 billion this year. The report is single-sourced; the dilution it describes is thesis-negative whatever the tape does. The slide carried the shares to $593 — back below the $616 the principal had flagged as a starter-buy level, and the deepest drawdown in the mega-cap group at nearly 25 percent off its high. The cheapest large-cap platform got cheaper on a day the market sold the companies paying for AI and spared those already monetising it. The drop is the tape; the level is the trigger.

## Cascade Watch

### A memory rumor sold the whole storage shelf

- **What happened:** Micron, SanDisk, Seagate and Western Digital all fell 8 to 13 percent on a single research firm's report that NVIDIA had halved a memory configuration on its next Rubin racks.
- **Why it happened:** A narrow DRAM-configuration rumor was read as a memory-demand verdict, even though it explicitly leaves untouched the high-bandwidth memory that is contracted and sold out through 2026.
- **How it matters:** The core of the thesis is intact — treat the move as positioning, and watch Micron's 24 June print for the only fundamental read that settles it.

### The AI-power cohort de-rated as one body

- **What happened:** Bloom Energy fell 9.5 percent, Cameco 9.3 percent, Vertiv 7.2 percent, Constellation 3.7 percent, with CoreWeave and IREN off 7 to 12 percent.
- **Why it happened:** The spending-payer side of the AI trade repriced on the same rate-plus-Broadcom shock, and no sector fund cleanly captures this group.
- **How it matters:** Bloom's move is cohort beta, not company news — the cohort, not the single name, is the unit to watch for a deployment signal.

### Insiders sold the beat-rejected names

- **What happened:** Discretionary executive sales clustered at TJX (about $16.6 million across the chief executive, finance chief and a group president), Diamondback (chief executive plus legal chief) and CrowdStrike (chief executive, about $3.6 million) — the first such cluster in weeks.
- **Why it happened:** Executives took liquidity near highs, and CrowdStrike's filing also stretched commission amortisation from four to five years, flattering its reported margin gain.
- **How it matters:** Where a beat was rejected and insiders are selling at once, the earnings-quality question is worth carrying into any future look at the name.

## Sector Pulse

The day's organising axis was duration, not sector. The rate shock punished long-dated cash flows — semiconductors, software, biotech and gold — and rewarded short-dated ones, with insurers the clean destination: Progressive rose 4.4 percent and the insurance fund 3 percent as rising rates lift the income on their float. Two thematic cohorts that no single fund captures moved as one. The AI-power group — Bloom Energy, Cameco, Constellation, Vertiv — fell 4 to 12 percent on the spending-payer de-rate. The memory-and-storage group — Micron, SanDisk, Seagate, Western Digital — fell 8 to 13 percent, the highest-gain corner giving back hardest. Neither move was a demand revision; both were the most-crowded winners unwinding into a rate shock.
