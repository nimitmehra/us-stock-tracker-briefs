## What Happened

### The AI trade split. It did not break.

A quiet index masked a violent rotation. Long-duration, AI-disruptable software was dumped — ServiceNow (NOW) fell 6.5%, Workday (WDAY) 6.2%, Palantir (PLTR) 6.1%, PTC (PTC) 6.3%, Tyler Technologies (TYL) 5.1% — in a session the tape nicknamed the "SaaSpocalypse." At the same time AI hardware and power were bid: Super Micro (SMCI) up 19.8%, Dell (DELL) 9.3%, NVIDIA and Broadcom higher, utilities and gas producers leading the board. The mechanism is not equity fear; it is duration. The oil-war premium is repricing through rising real yields, which crush far-future software multiples and rotate money into real assets and power. The demand question is settled; the swing factor is now who funds the capex.

### Alphabet answered demand and reopened funding

Alphabet (GOOGL), a portfolio holding, reported second-quarter results after Wednesday's close — a next-session event, not a cause of the daytime tape. Revenue was $119.8bn, up 24%; Google Cloud grew 82% to $24.8bn with backlog at $514bn. Demand is plainly real. The stock still fell about 5% after hours on the funding question: the full-year capital-spending guide was raised to roughly $205bn, free cash flow turned negative at −$5.9bn, and the headline $9.11 earnings figure was flattered by a roughly $98bn unrealised mark on Alphabet's Anthropic stake. Read the operating line, not the net-income line. As Goldman Sachs flagged $489bn of AI-related debt issued this year, the AI question has pivoted from "is demand real" to "who pays for it."

### Super Micro re-rated; the accounting question stayed open

Super Micro Computer (SMCI), a watch-list name, rose 19.8% after a preliminary fourth-quarter update guided gross margin to 15–17%, up from the 8.2–8.4% it had guided, on more than $60bn of new orders and a gigawatt AI data centre co-built for SpaceX and xAI. High short interest amplified the squeeze. The margin re-rate is real and independently corroborates hyperscaler AI-capex durability. But a roughly two-times margin swing pinned on "favourable mix" does not resolve why the original guide was so far off — that forensic question stays open until the full report on 11 August. Two truths hold at once: the demand is booking in hard dollars, and the accounting is not yet closed.

### ServiceNow beat, raised, and fell anyway

ServiceNow (NOW), a watch-list software name, fell 6.5% in the regular session — before it reported. After the close it delivered a clean beat-and-raise: subscription revenue up 24.5%, enterprise-AI bookings past $1bn in annual contract value, and a raised full-year guide. A great print could not outrun the de-rate. Rising real yields and a growth-convergence read — forward bookings growing slower than revenue — compressed the multiple regardless. This is the clearest single proof of the day's software repricing, and it validates the stress-test review completed a day earlier that cut the name to a weakened hold. The fear is half-overpriced; the multiple re-rates anyway.
