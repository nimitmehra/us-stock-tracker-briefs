## What Happened

### NVIDIA printed history. The shares did not extend.

NVIDIA Corporation (NVDA) posted Q1 FY27 revenue of $81.6 billion on Wednesday after the close, up 85 percent on the year and ahead of the $79 billion consensus. Management guided Q2 to $91 billion, excluding any China datacenter compute. The dividend was raised twenty-five-fold and an additional $80 billion was added to the buyback authorisation. On Thursday the shares closed at $219.51, down 1.77 percent — under-performing the semiconductor ETF by 2.34 percentage points on the company's own beat day. The periphery told the real story. Sandisk (SNDK) rose 10.75 percent, Lumentum (LITE) 11.11, Bloom Energy (BE) 9.06, Seagate (STX) 7.91 and Corning (GLW) 6.20 — five portfolio holdings caught the leg up. The consensus long has no marginal buyer; the marginal AI dollar bought the beneficiaries instead.

### Software broke. AI agents are the bear.

Intuit Inc. (INTU) beat and raised on Wednesday — and then announced cuts of seventeen percent of its workforce. The shares closed Thursday at $307.07, down 20.02 percent, the deepest single-stock derate of the day. The bear thesis crystallised in one print: AI agents will replace the workflows TurboTax and QuickBooks have charged for, and the company itself is restructuring around the claim. The cohort caught it. Workday (WDAY) fell 3.76 percent, Adobe (ADBE) 3.66, ServiceNow (NOW) 3.49, Salesforce (CRM) 2.10, Veeva Systems (VEEV) 4.06. Cybersecurity went the other way — Palo Alto Networks (PANW) up 2.54 percent, Cloudflare (NET) up 1.20, Fortinet (FTNT) lagging mildly at minus 0.42. The market's verdict: agents replace workflows, not network-edge enforcement.

### Off-price crushed. Walmart flagged fuel.

The TJX Companies (TJX) reported Q1 FY27 EPS of $1.19 against guidance of $0.97 to $0.99 — comparable sales ran three to four times the guided midpoint. Ross Stores (ROST) followed with Q1 EPS of $2.02 against $1.60 to $1.67, comparable sales of plus seventeen percent against seven to eight. Both raised the full-year guide. Walmart (WMT) printed in line on Q1 but guided Q2 EPS below the consensus, and explicitly absorbed $175 million of higher fuel and logistics expense in the quarter. The shares closed Thursday down 7.27 percent — the first real-economy fuel attribution from a Tier-1 retailer this cycle. The Consumer Staples ETF (XLP) fell 1.01 percent on the drag. Off-price is the trade; mass-merchant absorbs the fuel.

### Iran flipped the script. Warsh held the hawk.

Iran's Supreme Leader Mojtaba Khamenei directed on Wednesday that enriched uranium must not leave Iran — a reversal of the precondition under which President Donald Trump postponed the planned strike six days earlier. Brent rose 2.64 percent to $105.29; gold held above $4,500. The same evening, the Federal Reserve released April's FOMC minutes — the first under Kevin Warsh, chair since 15 May. Four officials dissented, the first four-vote dissent at a Fed meeting since October 1992; markets priced a forty-percent probability of a December hike. The ten-year Treasury yield closed at 4.586 percent, a fifteen-month high. Energy equities refused the bid even as oil rallied — the oil and gas E&P ETF (XOP) fell 2.34 percent. The diplomatic track flipped escalatory; the rate path lost the cut.
