## Rates & Macro

The ten-year Treasury closed at **4.74%, up five basis points**, and every part of that move was real. The inflation-stripped ten-year rose five basis points to **2.40%** while the inflation part of the yield — what the market expects prices to do over ten years — sat unchanged at **2.34%**. Two sessions moved the ten-year nine basis points through two opposite channels.

For the artificial-intelligence book this was a headwind, and the clean kind. ## What Happened

### Two verdicts. Two documents. Opposite answers.

HEICO Corporation (HEI) and Axon Enterprise (AXON) each sit on our watch list rated buy, and each has a second review on file from the same day saying the opposite. HEICO's adversarial stress test upholds a pass. Timestamped ten hours before the review we cite, it says so plainly: *"the business is a BUY; the stock at $342 is a PASS."* The favourable review reaches buy by changing the yardstick, and by buying the cheaper non-voting share class instead. Axon's stress test confirms a hold and records that the return bar was missed at $475. The favourable review clears its gate at about $433 and says that fall *"improves the entry."* HEICO closed **$355.04** and Axon **$627.75**. Both buys were priced conditions, and both prices are gone.

### One survey moved everything. Utilities proved it.

S&P Global's flash composite business survey — where fifty divides growth from contraction — printed **56.0** for August, the strongest reading since April 2022. Services hit 56.8, a twenty-month high; factory output fell to 51.9, a thirteen-month low. Shares took the growth and the bond market took the bill. The proof is in the cross-section, not the index: every one of twenty-five regulated utilities fell, **mean −2.63%**, while homebuilders **rose 1.13%** on the identical five-basis-point move. A simple rate model treats those two cohorts as one trade. They are not. One owns duration, the other owns demand, and Friday's print raised the discount rate for the same reason it raised the cash flows. Thursday ran the test backwards and homebuilders fell.

### Steel bought a deal that then failed.

Steel Dynamics (STLD) rose **4.42%** on Friday and Nucor (NUE) **1.31%**, reversing Thursday's falls, while negotiators worked toward a midnight deadline. Bloomberg reported the talks running with "signs of optimism" on a framework halving metals duties to 25%. After the close — and this explains nothing about Friday — the talks collapsed. The United States imposed 50% tariffs on about $20 billion of Canadian goods; Ottawa dated dollar-for-dollar retaliation to **8 September**, naming steel, dairy, appliances, farm equipment, pulp and electronics. Mark Carney, Canada's prime minister, called the American terms "uneconomic, unfair." Our 20 August brief carried that deal on one wire and refused to upgrade it. The refusal paid — the deal died within hours.

### A fixed ratio lost a third of itself.

ON Semiconductor (ON) filed its merger prospectus for Synaptics (SYNA) on Friday. The exchange ratio is fixed at 1.350 onsemi shares per Synaptics share — no collar, no adjustment for either stock's price. The day before the deal was announced, that ratio was worth **$156.25** a share. At Friday's close it is worth **$100.18**, or 35.9% less, entirely because onsemi's own stock fell 35.9%. The fairness opinion blessing the ratio was rendered on **25 June** and has never been refreshed. The document quotes a 4 August reference price, so it was 8.1% stale on the day it was filed. Synaptics owes $235m to walk; onsemi owes $320m if regulators block it. Shareholders will vote on a ratio blessed at a price that no longer exists.

## Cascade Watch

### The consumer is relocating, not retrenching

- **What happened:** Flowers Foods (FLO) cut its full-year guidance on volumes down 5.8 percent, named shoppers trading down as the cause, and fell 5.7 percent — while the two deep-discount retailers rose 2.35 and 2.36 percent and the off-price chain that had just posted a traffic-driven 10 percent same-store gain rose 4.39 percent.
- **Why it happened:** Household budgets are moving spending between formats rather than out of the economy — the branded packaged-food maker loses the dollar that the discounter and the off-price chain gain.
- **How it matters:** The wires ran this as consumer weakness, and the difference decides whether you sell the discretionary complex or rotate inside it.

### Nuclear fuel led the tape the day utilities were sold

- **What happened:** Regulated utilities fell 2.63 percent on average with zero advancers out of twenty-five, while the independent power producers fell only 1.59 percent and the nuclear-fuel names rose hard — Cameco (CCJ) 7.24 percent, Energy Fuels (UUUU) 8.92 percent, the uranium fund 5.09 percent.
- **Why it happened:** If the data-centre power story were cracking, the power producers and fuel names would lead the fall; they did the opposite, so what sold was the bond proxy, not the artificial-intelligence trade.
- **How it matters:** Do not read Friday's utility selloff as a power-demand signal — the cohort ordering refutes it, and the group we actually own inside that theme fell half a percent.
