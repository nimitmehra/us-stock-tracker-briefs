## Rates & Macro

The rate that prices long-dated stocks, the ten-year Treasury, closed at 4.57 percent, up two basis points. The number that actually moves the AI trade — the inflation-stripped ten-year yield — rose three basis points to 2.35 percent. The two-year, the market's read on the Federal Reserve, rose three basis points to 4.16 percent.

Today was a headwind for the AI and long-duration book, and the mechanism matters. The real yield did not rise on an inflation scare. It rose because inflation is falling: cool June inflation pulled market inflation expectations down to 2.22 percent while the nominal yield held near 4.57 percent, so the real yield rose by arithmetic. A rising real discount rate marks down far-future profit, and the AI trade is far-future profit.

The exit line is a real ten-year holding above roughly 2.25 to 2.30 percent, or the two-year pushing toward 4.4 to 4.5 percent. Today sits decisively above the first line and well short of the second. The backdrop is a paradox: June consumer prices came in cool, down 0.4 percent on the month — the biggest monthly drop since April 2020 — and producer prices were soft, yet Chair Kevin Warsh pushed back on the dovish read, and nine of eighteen Fed officials still pencil a hike this year. Layered on top, oil spiked roughly 11 percent on the week to a Brent price of $85 on Iran-Hormuz escalation — forward-inflationary, and the second jaw of the squeeze.

## What Happened

### The exit line broke to the surface

The S&P 500 fell 0.51 percent to 7,533.77 and the Nasdaq 1.47 percent, a mild tape that hid the day. Beneath it, the memory and AI-hardware complex fell 9 to 13 percent in a single session. The driver was the inflation-stripped ten-year yield rising to 2.35 percent, through the 2.25-to-2.30-percent zone the system marks as danger for long-duration stocks. The rise came from falling inflation, not rising: cool June prices pulled expectations down while the nominal yield held, so the real yield rose by arithmetic. A higher discount rate marks down far-future profit. The AI trade is that profit — and today it got marked.

### Memory sold off. Demand did not.

The storage and memory names led the fall — Sandisk (SNDK) down 12.6 percent, Seagate (STX) 10 percent, Western Digital (WDC) 9.2 percent, Astera Labs (ALAB) 8.8 percent, Marvell (MRVL) 8.7 percent. None filed anything; no memory maker reported a miss. The trigger was a peer's warning: South Korea's SK Hynix guided its June quarter roughly 8 percent below consensus and fell 15 percent in Seoul, its worst day on record, atop an $8.6 billion Chinese memory listing that spells future supply. The market read record fundamentals as "as good as it gets." Gold fell with stocks and bond-market volatility stayed calm — the signature of a repricing, not a panic. This was a positioning unwind, not a demand break.

### They beat. They raised. They fell.

The two chipmakers the desk rates a buy reported blowout quarters and dropped anyway. Taiwan Semiconductor (TSM), the foundry that makes the most advanced chips, posted second-quarter revenue up 33.7 percent to $40.2 billion, earnings up 77 percent, and lifted its full-year growth guide above 40 percent. ASML Holding (ASML), which builds the machines that make those chips, beat and held its €43-to-45 billion year. Both shares fell — TSM down 2.3 percent, ASML down 1.7 percent — though each outperformed the memory names it dragged down. The prints refute the "AI has plateaued" fear outright. In a tape marking down the discount rate, even proof of demand gets sold.

### Alphabet's model slipped, not just its multiple

Alphabet (GOOGL) fell 4.4 percent, and only part of that was the market. Bloomberg reported that Gemini 3.5 Pro, the company's most powerful model, slipped its schedule for a third time; Google scrapped the base model and restarted training, pushing the launch to July 17 after failures on complex layout and tool-use tasks. The delay landed alongside a talent exodus from its DeepMind lab — Gemini co-lead Noam Shazeer left for OpenAI, and Nobel laureate John Jumper for Anthropic. Reports peg roughly $225 billion of market value erased. This is an execution-and-retention signal on Google's frontier-model standing, separate from the sector's discount-rate reset. The stock lagged its own sector by nearly four points.

### Where the money went: earnings, not AI

The cash leaving AI hardware bought defensives with real prints. Abbott Laboratories (ABT) rose 10.7 percent, a top gainer, after second-quarter earnings of $1.31 beat and it raised full-year guidance on medical-device strength. Cintas (CTAS), the uniform-services group, gained 7.2 percent on a fiscal-fourth-quarter beat and record margins; DexCom (DXCM), the glucose-monitor maker, rose 7.2 percent on a Canadian approval and a price-target lift. Staples, health care, transports, and regional banks all caught the bid while semiconductors bled. Gold fell too — no fear trade, just rotation. The rotation is the tell: this was a discount-rate event that spared the cash flows you can bank today.

## Cascade Watch

### China supply fear links Micron to the tariff regime

- **What happened:** Micron's connection to the tariff-and-trade macro strengthened, its weight rising from 3.0 to 4.5, on the $8.6 billion listing of China's ChangXin Memory and continued US export controls on advanced Chinese memory equipment.
- **Why it happened:** A well-funded new Chinese entrant is the classic supply-glut signal in cyclical memory, even as export controls cap its high-end threat — a supply story and a trade-policy story at once.
- **How it matters:** It reframes Micron's cycle risk as partly policy-driven; the buy-back-in band that went active today should be weighed against a China-supply overhang that will not clear quickly.

### Oil's spike fanned out to the energy producers

- **What happened:** The Hormuz oil spike propagated through the graph to the energy producers — ExxonMobil, Chevron, ConocoPhillips, Diamondback, and Cheniere all saw their link to the oil macro refresh as Brent reached $85.
- **Why it happened:** US strikes on Iran and a reinstated naval blockade near the strait that carries a fifth of the world's oil tightened supply and lifted crude roughly 11 percent on the week.
- **How it matters:** The same oil move that feeds producer margins is the inflationary second jaw of the squeeze on the AI trade — a cross-current the principal's energy exposure benefits from while his long-duration book pays for it.

### The de-rate split the chip sector in two

- **What happened:** Within semiconductors, the fabricators held — NVIDIA, TSM, ASML, and KLA all outperformed — while memory, storage, and connectivity were destroyed, a roughly 10-point intra-sector spread.
- **Why it happened:** A discount-rate repricing hits the highest-multiple, most-levered names hardest, and spares the ones with proven, banked cash flows.
- **How it matters:** This is a selective de-rate, not a sector capitulation; the split says where to add if the rout extends — quality fabricators over speculative memory.
