## What Happened

### The AI-server cohort is no longer a Dell story.

Hewlett Packard Enterprise Co. (HPE) printed an April-quarter blowout on Monday after the close — revenue $10.7 billion (up 40 percent), Networking up 148 percent, non-GAAP earnings $0.79 against $0.54 consensus — and raised its full-year revenue-growth guide to 29–33 percent from 17–22 percent and earnings to $3.35–$3.45 from $2.30–$2.50. The stock was up about 26 percent in Tuesday's pre-market. CoreWeave (CRWV) rose 14.0 percent on Monday to $124.82 on the industry's first delivery of NVIDIA's Vera Rubin NVL72 rack, built by Dell. Marvell Technology (MRVL) jumped roughly 25 percent in Tuesday pre-market after Jensen Huang, NVIDIA's chief executive and founder since 1993, called it "the next trillion-dollar company" on stage at Computex. Friday's 33 percent move on Dell, in other words, was not a lonely repricing. The cohort move now spans compute, memory, storage, networking and servers — and Broadcom's print on Wednesday is the next read-through.

### Bonds bid; oil faded; the tape made fresh records anyway.

Monday's session opened on a 7–8 percent oil spike after Iran threatened to close the Strait of Hormuz; by the close the move had been pared to plus-5.85 percent on West Texas Intermediate as Trump announced a partial Israel–Hezbollah ceasefire in Lebanon and said talks with Iran were continuing. Tuesday the US suspended new Iran sanctions; Brent settled at $94.58, down 0.42 percent net. The bond complex, separately, took the duration bid overnight — the 10-year Treasury closed Tuesday at 4.439 percent, four basis points lower than Monday — and the indices closed at fresh records across the S&P 500, NASDAQ and Dow. The Russell 2000 fell 0.47 percent. The cohort lifted on its own catalyst stack; the geopolitics ran alongside it as ambiguous overlay, not as the day's author.

### SK Hynix put a number on the memory bear case.

SK Group Chairman Chey Tae-won used Computex on Tuesday to commit SK Hynix to doubling wafer capacity over five years, with 2026 capital spending "well above" the won-30.2 trillion ($20 billion-plus) it spent in 2025 — and named NVIDIA's Vera Rubin high-bandwidth-memory socket as the target. This is the first credible Korean supply-response cite for the memory-pricing-power bear case on a 2027-plus view. It does not invalidate the near-term tightness Micron Technology (MU) and SanDisk (SNDK) are riding — the lead time on a greenfield fab is five years — but it is the long-cycle counter-narrative the bull case has not had to answer until now. The graph carries both. The MU print on roughly 24 June is the next test.

## Cascade Watch

### AI-infrastructure cascade confirmed at every layer

- **What happened:** Dell up 10.7 percent, CoreWeave up 14.0 percent on the first NVIDIA Vera Rubin NVL72 rack delivery, NetApp up 3.1 percent, Hewlett Packard Enterprise up 9.2 percent before its 26 percent pre-market move on a 40-percent revenue-growth print, and Marvell up 25 percent pre-market on Huang's stage endorsement — all in a 48-hour window.
- **Why it happened:** Each name monetises a different vertical of the same artificial-intelligence build — compute, networking, storage, servers, cloud — and each posted a discrete confirmation in the same window. The market is no longer pricing the build as concentrated in NVIDIA alone.
- **How it matters:** Broadcom's print Wednesday after the close is the read-through validator — the Street is looking for $5 billion or better on artificial-intelligence revenue against $4.1 billion of prior guidance. If the cohort holds the bid into Thursday, the watching list now includes Marvell.

### Korea put a number on the memory bear case

- **What happened:** SK Hynix's chairman publicly committed to doubling wafer capacity over five years, with 2026 capital spending "well above" $20 billion, and named NVIDIA's Vera Rubin high-bandwidth-memory socket as the target.
- **Why it happened:** The 2024–26 memory tightness has been the most-profitable cycle for the Korean producers in a decade. A capacity-doubling commitment is what a rational major does in response — and the Computex stage is the highest-conviction venue to signal it.
- **How it matters:** Near-term Micron and SanDisk are still on the bid (supply lead times are five-plus years and the cohort thesis is intact through 2027), but the long-cycle pricing-power bear case now has its first credible Korean cite. The Micron print in late June will be the first earnings test of how the bull case answers it.

### Computex split the chip cohort in two

- **What happened:** NVIDIA up 6.3 percent, Micron up 6.6 percent, SanDisk up 3.9 percent and Seagate up 4.7 percent — all to records — while Qualcomm fell 8.8 percent, Intel 4.7 percent and Texas Instruments 4.1 percent on the same Huang keynote.
- **Why it happened:** NVIDIA's N1X / RTX Spark personal-computer chip threatens Qualcomm's Snapdragon X Elite in artificial-intelligence personal computers, and Microsoft easing Windows-on-Arm hardware exclusivity erodes the Qualcomm moat. The legacy/non-artificial-intelligence chip names sold as cohort funding.
- **How it matters:** The market has stopped pricing semiconductors as one block. Same event, opposite signs, single session — the pattern is the cleanest within-sector winners-versus-losers print of the cycle.
