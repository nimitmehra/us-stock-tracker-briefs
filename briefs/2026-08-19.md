## Rates & Macro

The ten-year Treasury yield, the rate that prices long-dated stocks, closed Wednesday at **4.65 percent, down six basis points**. The inflation-stripped ten-year — the number that marks down far-future artificial-intelligence profits — also fell **six basis points, to 2.35 percent**. The two-year, the market's read on the Federal Reserve, was **unchanged at 4.19 percent** for a third session. Inflation expectations did not move: the ten-year breakeven sat at 2.30 percent. The whole rally was real rate.

On the arithmetic that is a **tailwind** for the artificial-intelligence book: far-future profit got cheaper to discount. The chip and storage complex fell two to eight percent anyway, for the second day running. That gap is the day's most useful fact and it is told below.

The exit line is unchanged and restated every day: the inflation-stripped ten-year holding above roughly 2.25 to 2.30 percent, or the two-year pushing toward 4.4 to 4.5. Today sits **above the first at 2.35**, where it has now sat for over a week, and **21 to 31 basis points below the second**. Treat both as a calibration, not a promise.

The move had one cause. At 09:00 ET the Treasury said it will at least **double the size of its long-end buyback operations — from $2 billion to at least $4 billion each** — in the ten-to-twenty and twenty-to-thirty-year sectors, effective 9 September through 4 November. The thirty-year fell nine basis points to 5.19 and the twenty-year eleven, to 5.17; the front end did not move at all. That is the debt-management office capping the long end days after the thirty-year printed its highest yield since 2007. Five hours later the Federal Reserve published the minutes of its July meeting, in which **three regional presidents dissented in favour of a rate rise** and Chair Kevin Warsh floated cutting the committee from eight meetings a year to six. A curve-managing Treasury and a hawkish Fed in the same afternoon is not a tidy story, and gold priced the first one.

## What Happened

### Moderna tripled. The trim did not fire.

Moderna (MRNA), a portfolio holding, closed at **$174.38 from $62.96 — up 176.97 percent** on 195.3 million shares, about forty times normal. Merck & Co. Intismeran plus KEYTRUDA beat KEYTRUDA alone on recurrence-free and distant-metastasis-free survival in resected melanoma, with no new safety signals. The instruction on our books read: **trim above $78 absent a positive melanoma interim.** The interim was positive, so the clause is defeated and the order does not fire — even with the price 123.6 percent through the level. ### Neither the number nor a filing arrived

Read it as evidence and you fire the $78.

### Yields fell. The chips fell anyway.

Yesterday's brief argued the long bond did not cause the storage selloff: chips rose when yields spiked and fell when they eased. Wednesday tested that out of sample and it held. The Treasury buyback took **six basis points off the ten-year and nine off the thirty**. ### Our insider read has now been wrong twice in two runs

- **What happened:** The 18 August brief said *"Chevron's chief executive sold $63.6 million outside any plan."* **It was an exercise-and-sell** — Michael Wirth, Chevron's chairman and chief executive, exercised 317,100 options at an $88.20 strike and sold the same 317,100 shares the same day, with expiring options forcing the hand.
- **Why it happened:** A naive read of an insider filing counts gross proceeds and misses the exercise leg — the same class of error as 18 August, when routine tax withholding was counted as selling and reversed the readings on Bloom Energy, Lumentum and Cloudflare.
