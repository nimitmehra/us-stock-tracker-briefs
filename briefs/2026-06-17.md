## What Happened

### The Fed held rates but penciled in a hike.

The Federal Reserve's interest-rate committee held its main rate at 3.50 to 3.75 percent on Wednesday, twelve votes to none — but the projections flipped from an easing bias to a hike. The median official now sees a year-end 2026 rate of 3.8 percent, up from 3.4 percent in March; nine of eighteen project at least one hike. Kevin Warsh, the new chair confirmed last month, cut the statement to 130 words, dropped the rate-cut outlook, and refused all forward guidance at his first press conference. A hot retail-sales print that morning — up 0.9 percent against a 0.5 percent forecast — removed the soft-landing cover. The S&P 500 fell 1.21 percent to 7,420.10, all eleven sectors lower; the two-year yield jumped about eleven basis points intraday before fading, and the dollar hit a 52-week high. The bearish pairing landed.

### Meta fell to the edge of its add level on an unfiled raise.

Meta Platforms (META), the social-media and AI group, fell 5.44 percent to $567.58 — the deepest drawdown in the big-tech complex, worse than its own falling sector. The slide had a company-specific layer. Press reports said Meta may raise as much as $62 billion in equity to fund AI infrastructure, a dilution fear atop an existing spending overhang. The raise has not appeared on the SEC's filing system; it is a report, not a filed transaction. The day's mechanics matter more. At $567.58 the stock sits only 1.4 percent above the $560 level where the firm would add — the closest it has come — yet it did not cross. The cheapest big-tech name is now the closest to a buy and still just out of reach.

### Moderna hit a record the day before its vote.

Moderna (MRNA), the messenger-RNA vaccine maker, rose 11.55 percent to a record $61.80 — a 13-point move against both a falling healthcare sector and a falling market, the third up-day running. The lift is the run-in to Thursday's advisory-committee vote on mFlusiva, its seasonal-flu shot, where regulators' staff documents found no major deficiencies and no heart-inflammation signal. This is the only near-term binary across the whole book, and it lands in roughly twenty-four hours. Favourable staff documents skew the odds, but the older-adult comparator question — the one that sent the application back in February — is exactly what the committee can still re-open. The vote is event-risk, not a foregone approval. Tomorrow is the test, not today's record.

## Cascade Watch

### The value-over-growth rotation snapped back into a broad de-rate

- **What happened:** Yesterday's split tape — Dow at a record while the Nasdaq fell — collapsed into an all-down session; the AI and semiconductor names that unwound earlier in the week bounced (the semiconductor ETF up 1.29 percent, the only green group besides defense and biotech), while the financials, real-estate and homebuilder names that caught yesterday's rotation gave it all back.
- **Why it happened:** The hawkish projections lifted the discount rate through the two-year yield's intraday jump, which both hit long-duration growth and unwound the rate-sensitive value bid that had been positioned for cuts — so the rotation reversed rather than extended.
- **How it matters:** The day was a higher-for-longer reset, not a panic — volatility rose but tail-hedge pricing stayed flat; the question is whether the front-end repricing sticks, since the partial fade into the close suggests the market is not yet convinced.

### Two structural themes stayed bid through the selloff

- **What happened:** Gold held flat against a 0.78-percent dollar surge, and the AI-power-infrastructure cohort — GE Vernova up 6.77 percent, Vertiv up 6.00 percent, Bloom Energy up 1.46 percent — was bid through a rate-driven down tape for a second straight session.
- **Why it happened:** The gold bid is a structural central-bank reserve-diversification trade rather than a real-rate trade, so it survived a hawkish Fed; the power-infrastructure demand is a datacenter-buildout story that decoupled from the rate move.
- **How it matters:** Both cross-currents argue the structural-demand themes are trading independently of the rate tape — a quiet tell that the held Bloom Energy thesis and the watched gold-miner cohort are insulated from the higher-for-longer reset.

### No trigger changed status; Meta sits at the edge

- **What happened:** Despite the de-rate, no Tier 1 trigger transitioned — Meta's $560 add level tightened to its closest-ever 1.4 percent above without crossing, and Micron's trim and Sandisk's legacy-share exit stayed where they were.
- **Why it happened:** Meta's fall shortened the distance but crossed no line, and its reassess-sell level is set to fire only on fundamental deterioration, not a market-wide selloff — so today's macro drop left it untouched.
- **How it matters:** Meta is the single closest-to-fire holding in the book; one further down-session arms the most actionable add we have.
