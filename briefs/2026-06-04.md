## What Happened

### Broadcom's non-raise repriced the buildout cohort.

Broadcom (AVGO), the custom-AI-chip and networking supplier whose after-hours print closed yesterday's brief on a bullish note, fell about 15 percent — its worst day since January 2025. The second-quarter beat was clean, but chief executive Hock Tan held the 2027 AI target at $100 billion rather than raising it, and the current-quarter AI-chip guide of $16 billion came in below the $17.2 billion the Street wanted. Into a stock that had set five straight records, "good but not better" was a sell. The damage ran across the complex — Ciena off nearly 19 percent, Marvell, Qualcomm and the optical names with it. The cohort now trades on a single rule: beat and raise, or get repriced.

### Memory's leadership island took its first crack.

Micron Technology (MU), the largest American maker of memory chips and the one corner of the AI trade the rate shock left alone yesterday, fell 7.54 percent to $998.20 — the portfolio's worst showing and worse than the routed chip tape itself. There was no Micron-specific news; the move is Broadcom read-through plus a Raymond James warning that memory-chip prices could peak by mid-2026. That caution maps onto the company's own sell-trigger for falling prices, which makes the 24 June quarterly print the real test. Storage held while memory fell — Seagate and SanDisk both shed under two percent. The island stood alone yesterday; today it has a hairline.

### Capital rotated into banks and healthcare, not into cash.

The day's tell was the Dow up 1.52 percent while the Nasdaq fell — and the stock-market fear gauge (VIX) eased to 15.93, the signature of a rotation rather than a retreat. Money leaving the AI-capex payers landed in two cheap, rate-relieved sectors: financials rose 2.45 percent on a steeper yield curve that fattens bank lending margins, and healthcare rose 3.26 percent, the day's strongest, led by insurers on Centene's stabilising medical-cost read. Oil fell 3.83 percent as progress on an Iran ceasefire framework pulled the war premium back out; gold rose, the dollar softened, and ten-year yields eased — a clean disinflation print, the mirror of yesterday's rate shock. The proceeds did not sell the market; they crossed it.
