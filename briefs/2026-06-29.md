## Rates & Macro

The ten-year Treasury yield — the rate that prices long-dated stocks — sat flat at 4.38 percent on Monday, unchanged on the day. The number that matters most for the AI book, the inflation-stripped (real) ten-year, eased two basis points to 2.16 percent; the two-year, the market's read on the Fed, firmed three to 4.10 percent. A falling real yield is mechanically a tailwind for the AI and long-duration trade, because a lower discount rate marks up far-future profit. But today's move was small and was not what drove the tape — the rally was equity-specific, not a rates event.

On the exit line, the real ten-year at 2.16 percent now sits comfortably below the calibrated danger zone of roughly 2.25 to 2.30 percent — and below where it sat last week. The AI book retains its rate cushion; nothing today moved it toward the line. The quiet tell sits at the front end: three- and six-month bills firmed four to six basis points while the ten-year held, the market nudging out hopes of second-half rate cuts without disturbing the long end. That is consistent with the hawkish June projections — year-end core inflation lifted toward 3.6 percent, and the Fed's own rate-path projections (the "dot plot") tilted to a hike — under the new chair, Kevin Warsh, who succeeded Jerome Powell on 15 May and speaks in Portugal on Wednesday. The June jobs report lands a day early on Thursday; oil's rebound off a four-month low partly reverses the disinflation tailwind but stays far below the war peak.

## What Happened

### Alphabet entered the Dow. Verizon left.

Alphabet (GOOGL), the parent of Google and DeepMind, joined the Dow Jones Industrial Average on Monday, taking the slot vacated by Verizon (VZ), and closed up 4.82 percent at $353.65 — outpacing its sector by three points. At roughly $350 a share in a price-weighted index, it immediately commands about 4 percent of the Dow and enters the top ten, the fifth of the Mag-7 in the average. The Dow closed above 52,000 for the first time, up 0.59 percent. The move is forced index buying plus an oversold-relief bounce, not a fundamental re-rating — membership does nothing for the DeepMind retention worry flagged last week, which the celebration simply buried. Verizon was the mirror: down 5.24 percent on the removal, on merger charges, and on a Starlink-mobile threat, dragging the wider telecom cohort down with it. The index, not the franchise, did the work.

### Micron's memory boom reached the toolmakers.

The read-through from last week's record Micron Technology (MU) quarter finally hit the companies that sell the equipment, climbing the supply chain a rung. The chip-equipment makers ripped — KLA (KLAC) up 11.97 percent, Applied Materials (AMAT) 10.82 percent and Lam Research (LRCX) 8.39 percent — on a wave of price-target raises and management's claim of "the industry's strongest period ever," with the equipment market's addressable size upgraded to $154 billion and bookings now stretching into 2028. The storage duopoly ran alongside: Seagate Technology (STX), a holding, gained 7.63 percent and Western Digital (WDC) 11.16 percent on sold-out capacity locked through 2028. Tellingly, the names that already ran on the print itself — Micron up just 1.14 percent, Sandisk down 1.93 percent — lagged or fell. The tape now pays the picks-and-shovels, not the prospectors.

### A relief rally the options market distrusts.

The oversold Mag-7 bounced on the US-Iran stand-down, with the fear gauge (VIX) easing 4.13 percent to 17.65 as both sides agreed to "stand down for now" ahead of Doha talks on Tuesday. Amazon (AMZN) rose 3.20 percent and Meta Platforms (META) 2.24 percent on no company news beyond the broad bid; Microsoft (MSFT), down 1.18 percent, was the lone Mag-7 holdout, its capex de-rate unbroken. Yet beneath the green, defensives, materials and off-price retail all closed red, and the options market's tail-hedge gauge firmed even as spot volatility fell — participants paying for the rally while hedging it. A relief rally on a fragile, conflicting de-escalation is the kind the tape distrusts even as it buys.
