## What Happened

### Dell repriced the AI-server stack in one session

Dell Technologies (DELL) reported first-quarter fiscal-2027 revenue of $43.8 billion, up 88 percent. Artificial-intelligence-server orders booked in-quarter ran to $24.4 billion — more than the $16.1 billion it shipped — and the backlog rose to $51.3 billion. Adjusted earnings of $4.86 a share landed against $2.94 expected. The full-year artificial-intelligence-server outlook was raised to $60 billion. Dell rose 32.76 percent, its best day on record. The entire stack repriced in sympathy: NetApp (NTAP) +22.39 percent, ServiceNow (NOW) +14.38 percent, International Business Machines (IBM) +12.71 percent, Hewlett-Packard Enterprise (HPE) +12.64 percent, Workday (WDAY) +12.45 percent, Oracle (ORCL) +10.84 percent. The cleanest single-print confirmation of the artificial-intelligence-capital-expenditure cycle so far is now a maker's quarterly result, not a sell-side forecast.

### The consumer survey hit a record low. The index hit a record high.

The University of Michigan consumer-sentiment final for May printed 44.8 — a record low for the index — against 48.2 expected. Fifty-seven percent of consumers spontaneously cited high prices, and long-run inflation expectations rose to 3.9 percent. The S&P 500 closed at a record 7,580.06 on the same tape, with a ninth straight weekly gain and seventh straight winning day, the longest streak since 2023. Hard data — Dell's blowout, Sandisk's seventeen-month surge, the Iran-driven oil reversal — is doing all the work; the soft consumer channel is dissenting. The split is the regime question heading into next week's May consumer-price index print on the eleventh.

### Bonds were calm. Oil kept falling. The truce is reported, not signed.

The April core personal-consumption-expenditures price index ran in-line at 3.3 percent over the year and a softer-than-expected 0.2 percent on the month. Ten-year Treasury yields drifted two basis points lower to 4.453 percent; the bond-market volatility gauge (MOVE) fell almost twelve percent on the week — bond vol is capitulating. The US and Iran are reported to have mostly agreed a sixty-day pause-and-Hormuz-reopening memorandum of understanding, but Trump has not yet approved and Iranian state media said it is not finalized. Brent fell to $91.70, down 2.14 percent on the day and roughly nineteen percent in May — the biggest monthly oil decline since 2020. A diplomatic stumble would partly reverse the crude crash.

### Compute is lagging memory inside the artificial-intelligence trade

NVIDIA (NVDA), the consensus accelerator long, closed at $211.14, down 1.45 percent on a day the rest of the cohort ripped. That is a third straight week of relative-laggard behaviour, now down nine percent from its May peak of $235.74. Micron Technology (MU) rose 5.14 percent to a fresh record $971.00 on a Susquehanna price-target raise to $1,750 from $600, the most aggressive sell-side anchor of the cycle. Sandisk (SNDK) added 3.25 percent to a fresh record after the Q3 print showed 97 percent sequential revenue growth. Capital is moving down the artificial-intelligence stack from compute into memory; the custom-silicon, Dell-system and software cohort is the non-Nvidia leg the dollars now want.
