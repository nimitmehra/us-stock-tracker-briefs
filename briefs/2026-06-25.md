## Rates & Macro

The ten-year Treasury yield barely moved, easing one basis point to 4.40 percent on Thursday. The number that matters most for the AI book — the inflation-stripped (real) ten-year — fell four basis points to 2.19 percent; the two-year, the market's read on the Fed, eased two to 4.09 percent. A falling real yield is mechanically a tailwind for the AI and long-duration trade, because a lower discount rate marks up far-future profit. On the exit line, the real ten-year at 2.19 percent now sits clearly below the calibrated danger zone of roughly 2.25 to 2.30 percent — further below than yesterday. The AI book has more rate cushion today, not less.

That cushion is the day's paradox. The Fed's preferred inflation gauge, May core PCE, printed +3.4 percent — the hottest reading since 2023 — alongside firmer growth and a tighter jobs market. Normally that pushes yields up and hurts the AI trade. Yet yields fell, because the Iran-driven oil collapse is disinflating faster than hot services prices can pressure the curve, and the rates market is not yet pricing the hot print as a hike. So rates were not today's threat to the AI complex. The threat was idiosyncratic — a memory-cost shock hitting the device makers — not a rate shock.

## What Happened

### The Micron print split the AI-hardware trade in two.

Micron Technology (MU), the largest American memory-chip maker, closed up 15.74 percent at a record $1,213.56 — the day's largest Tier 1 gain — as Wednesday's after-hours quarter played out in cash. Revenue hit a record $41.46 billion, and management disclosed roughly $100 billion of revenue contracted at floor prices across sixteen multi-year agreements — the durability evidence the bear case had demanded. The supply side ripped with it: Sandisk (SNDK) jumped 21.97 percent to a record, and the chip-equipment makers Applied Materials, KLA and Lam Research carved fresh all-time highs on the capex read-through. The same shortage cut the other way for the device makers. Apple and Dell are now paying for the boom; Micron is being paid for it.

### Apple raised prices. The market read demand destruction.

Apple (AAPL) fell 6.12 percent to $275.15 — the cohort's largest loss, against a tech sector that rose — after pre-announcing 15 to 25 percent price hikes across Macs, iPads and home devices. Chief executive Tim Cook blamed the AI-driven memory shortage, calling the cost backdrop a "hundred-year flood"; an iPhone hike is deferred but under consideration. Dell Technologies (DELL), the AI-server maker, fell 5.67 percent on the same squeeze, its gross margin compressed to 18 percent from 21 percent on rising memory costs. The market took the second read of a price hike — not margin defence, but volume risk into a hot-inflation consumer. The deferred iPhone hike is the far larger line, and the real overhang.

### Expensive software sold; the Dow hit a record.

High-multiple software de-rated as capital rotated out of crowded growth, with Palantir (PLTR) falling 5.49 percent to a fresh 52-week low despite record revenue and a raised guide — the cleanest valuation tell of the day. The dollars went to value and cyclicals: Caterpillar rose 6.29 percent on a wave of price-target raises, and the Dow Jones Industrial Average printed a fresh intraday all-time high even as the Nasdaq logged a fourth straight loss, its longest streak since February. The index level — the S&P 500 dead flat at 7,357.49 — disguised the whole session. Nine of the top ten names advanced while the cap-weighted Nasdaq fell. A flat tape can hide a violent rotation underneath.

## Cascade Watch

### The memory shortage became a consumer-inflation vector

- **What happened:** Apple and Microsoft raised device prices 15 to 25 percent on the same day core PCE printed its hottest reading since 2023, with DRAM contract prices up 90 to 95 percent last quarter and another 58 to 63 percent projected this one.
- **Why it happened:** AI data-center demand for memory has diverted supply from consumer hardware, so the chip shortage that hands Micron a record quarter now lands directly in consumer-goods prices.
- **How it matters:** The memory link between the chip sector, the Fed-rates picture and consumer prices is now two-way and inflationary — a fresh goods-inflation source that feeds the very PCE problem the Fed is fighting, and a new reason the hot-inflation case could eventually drag yields back up.

### The Micron read-through ran to the supply chain, not the compute leader

- **What happened:** Micron's contracted-demand print lifted the chip-equipment makers (Applied Materials +13.4 percent, Lam Research, KLA) and the optical-interconnect names (Corning +10.8 percent) to records, while NVIDIA fell 1.64 percent and lagged the rising chip sector.
- **Why it happened:** The market paid for demonstrated, contracted memory demand and for the equipment that builds the capacity — not for the AI-compute front-runner, which is itself a memory-cost consumer.
- **How it matters:** The semiconductor-equipment cohort is now strongly eligible to become its own tracked theme (Applied Materials as anchor); the day reads as a surgical memory-supply trade, not a broad chip re-rating.
