## Rates & Macro

The ten-year Treasury yield, the rate that prices long-dated stocks, closed at **4.69 percent, up four basis points**. The five-year rose four, the thirty-year four, the twenty-year three. The two-year — the market's read on the Federal Reserve — **did not move at all**, at 4.19 percent. The inflation-stripped ten-year, the number that marks down far-future artificial-intelligence profit, **did not move either: 2.35 percent**. That is a direct reading from our price file — 2.35 today against 2.35 the day before, a change of zero basis points — not an inference. Expected inflation over ten years accounts for the entire four-basis-point move, rising from 2.30 to 2.34 percent.

On that arithmetic today was **neither a tailwind nor a headwind** for the artificial-intelligence book. Nothing happened to the discount rate. What rose was the price of oil, and oil is not in the denominator of a technology valuation.

The exit line, restated every day: the inflation-stripped ten-year holding above roughly 2.25 to 2.30 percent, or the two-year pushing toward 4.4 to 4.5. Today sits **above the first at 2.35**, where it has now sat for more than a week, and **21 to 31 basis points below the second**. Both are a calibration, not a promise.

The backdrop got worse in one place that matters. The Treasury sold thirty-year inflation-protected debt at a real yield of **2.973 percent — the highest since October 2001**, a quarter of a century. It cleared 1.8 basis points through the level buyers had indicated, so demand was fine at the price; the price is the point. The same week the national debt crossed **$40 trillion**, five months after $39 trillion, and Wednesday's long-end buyback relief was fully given back in one session. There was no inflation or jobs print in the window: weekly claims came in at 206,000 against 210,000 expected, while people already out of work rose to 1.799 million. Nobody is being fired and nobody is being hired, which is the exact shape that lets a hawkish committee stay hawkish.

## What Happened

### One sentence from Washington sorted the market

The Treasury Secretary said he will lay out a plan to wage "economic warfare" on Iran, and the President threatened the "most crushing economic operation ever taken against any country." The sixty-day agreement that kept the Strait of Hormuz open expired on 16 August and is not being renewed; the United Arab Emirates halted all trade with Iran after two ballistic missiles were fired at it. Crude rose **2.14 percent** on 232,492 contracts, a fifth straight advancing session, and Brent went above **$94**, peaking at $94.67 in the morning. Higher oil raised expected inflation four basis points; nominal yields followed by the same four with real rates flat. The market then sorted by inflation sensitivity, not by industry — hard assets and real-economy names bid, everything long-dated or consumer-dependent sold.

### A court ruling from February arrived as August profit

Five retailers booked refunds of tariffs the Supreme Court struck down in February, all into the same quarter. Walmart (WMT) took about **$2.9 billion**, Home Depot $730 million, TJX Companies (TJX) $331 million, Ross Stores (ROST) $253 million, Lowe's $80 million. Only Ross said how much: $253 million is **$0.60 of $2.66 in earnings per share, and 405 of a 610 basis-point gain in operating margin** — strip it and the margin story is 205 basis points, not 610. Walmart's own material-event filing mentions tariff refunds six times and **never states a dollar figure**; the $2.9 billion reached the market through same-day press coverage, not the filing. The money is repayment of duties already paid, so it cannot repeat. Three of the three tracked retailers that booked it sold off.

### Walmart raised the guide. The filing sold it.

Walmart fell **9.15 percent to $103.84** on a quarter in which it raised every number it guides. Its filing explains that better than the sales story the wires led. Reported earnings per share fell **9.1 percent**. Six-month free cash flow fell **20.4 percent** while operating cash flow rose. And the capital-spending guide went from about 3.5 to about **4.0 percent of sales — roughly $3.7 billion a year** — in the same table that raised the earnings guide by three and a half cents. The next quarter is guided between flat and up 3.2 percent. On store sales the release gives **two drug-price headwinds, 80 basis points in the summary and 125 in the segment, and reconciles neither**. Against a consensus near 3.7 percent, one clears and one does not. Walmart did not say which.

### Moderna fell 23 percent. We read the wrong trial.

Moderna (MRNA), a portfolio holding, closed at **$133.32, down 23.55 percent**, giving back part of Wednesday's 176.97 percent. **Our record owes a correction.** Yesterday's working file attributed a 49 and a 59 percent risk reduction to the late-stage trial. **Those are earlier mid-stage figures.** The late-stage release disclosed no hazard ratio, no event counts, no confidence intervals, no survival data. Yesterday's brief printed that warning; the file behind it used the numbers anyway. Our re-underwrite condition gates on a ratio at or below 0.65, so it is **unscoreable — not met, not failed — and it did not fire**. Moderna has filed nothing since 12 August, and that was a third party's notice. On the figure we can source most directly, market value fell **$18 billion, from $69.6 billion to $51.5 billion**; our own graph file states the same round trip more roundly, as a $53 billion company giving back $16 billion. **The two do not reconcile and we have not made them.** Call the loss $16 to $18 billion. Either number describes a company that told nobody anything.

### Korea bought its own shares. American memory followed.

SK hynix's board approved a **40 trillion won buyback and cancellation — about $29 billion, 3.3 percent of the company** — and raised its payout target above half of free cash flow. The Korean market rose 5.89 percent, SK Hynix 12.73, Samsung Electronics 9.49, Kioxia 6.01. A memory maker retiring its own equity rather than building the next plant, mid-cycle, is a statement about supply. American memory followed: Micron Technology (MU) rose **3.97 percent**, Seagate Technology (STX) 2.12, SanDisk (SNDK) 2.02. That is the second out-of-sample test of the Asian read, and it passed on direction both times. But the pass-through is roughly **three to one** — about 1.0 down against 0.26 up. American memory tracks Asia down and merely nods at it going up.
