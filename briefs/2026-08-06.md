## Rates & Macro

The ten-year Treasury yield, the rate that prices long-dated stocks, closed at 4.69 percent, up six basis points, after two sessions of refusing to move at all. The two-year, the market's read on the Federal Reserve, rose seven to 4.25 percent, and so did the three- and five-year — the middle of the curve led, and the gap between the two- and ten-year narrowed a point to 44 basis points. The inflation-stripped ten-year, the number that marks down far-future artificial-intelligence profits, rose two to 2.43 percent against ten-year inflation expectations of 2.26.

That composition matters more than the size. Almost the whole move was real yield and the extra pay lenders want for going long, not expected inflation, and only the real part reaches valuations. So this was a headwind for the AI book — the first proper one this week — and it showed up exactly where it should have, in homebuilders and property, not in credit.

The exit line is unchanged: the inflation-stripped ten-year holding above roughly 2.25 to 2.30 percent, or the two-year pushing toward 4.4 to 4.5 percent. Today sits well above the first, at 2.43, and has never dipped into the band in any session our file holds. The two-year at 4.25 is closer to the second than it has been. Treat both as a calibration, not a promise.

## What Happened

### Cloudflare's instruction died twenty minutes early

Cloudflare (NET), the internet-infrastructure company we hold, was under a standing instruction to sell half the position before Thursday's regular-session close. It closed at $284.43, 13.77 percent above the price that instruction named, and the instruction lapsed unexecuted after sixty-seven days. It is now marked expired in the record rather than left to fade. The second-quarter report was accepted at 16:20:52 Eastern — twenty minutes after the deadline passed. Revenue was $696.1 million, up 36 percent and accelerating from 34; renewal reached 120 percent from 114; the full-year guide rose on all three published lines. The shares reportedly traded up 16.2 percent after hours on two independent quote sources. All three arms of the standing exit condition fail to fire.

### The twenty-percent cut sits in Note 15

The charge that tripled Cloudflare's statutory operating loss — $150.7 million — is a workforce reduction of about 20 percent. It was announced on 7 May and lands in the profit and loss for the first time this quarter. The figure appears in Note 15 of the quarterly report and in the forward-looking boilerplate. It appears nowhere in the release's own narrative. Beneath it, gross margin fell 310 basis points and the non-statutory operating margin fell 30 basis points on 36 percent growth — zero operating leverage on the fastest growth in two years. The artificial-intelligence agent products launched on 4 and 5 August carry no revenue, no recurring revenue and no customer count anywhere in the SEC record. The company chose what the headline would be.

### Axon fell 14 percent on a raise

Axon Enterprise (AXON), the public-safety company we rate a buy at conviction seven but do not own, fell 14.28 percent on Thursday. It was the first tradable session after Wednesday's post-close report, and the report raised the full-year revenue guide to 32–34 percent from 30–32. Software and services gross margin fell 430 basis points to 71.3 percent, the segment the multiple is paid for. Operating income was $47 million and tariff refunds received were $47 million; the profit benefit is never separated. Free cash flow was minus $1 million on $904 million of revenue, and earnings a share fell on both bases. The margin guide was held at 25.5 percent after delivering 26.8 — a second-half cut written as a hold.

### Motorola bought the drone company Axon named

One second after Axon's filing hit the wire on Wednesday evening, Motorola Solutions (MSI) filed its own. It raised full-year revenue and earnings guidance for the second time this year and agreed to buy D-Fend Solutions, a counter-drone company, for $1.5 billion. It rose 8.20 percent on Thursday against Axon's fall — a spread of 22.48 points in one session between two companies in the same business. Axon's release names Dedrone, its own counter-drone unit, as a growth engine. Motorola was not in our graph until today, which means the system has been watching one side of a duopoly. Its margin expansion carries a $60 million tariff refund of its own, so roughly 140 basis points of the 330 is underlying. It is still up.

### Yesterday's rule broke on Western Digital

Yesterday's brief published a rule: margin direction, not revenue, decided who got sold. Western Digital (WDC) broke it. It guided revenue up **and** gross margin up — 54.4 percent delivered against a 55 to 56 percent guide — and fell 13.03 percent, harder than Sandisk (SNDK), which guided margin down and fell 6.81. Seagate (STX), which did not report at all, rose 1.83. The rule survives in software and industrials, where Axon, Datadog (DDOG) and AppLovin (APP) were destroyed on flat or lower margin while Parker-Hannifin (PH), ATI and Motorola were bought on margin raises. In memory and storage it fails outright. There the tape is not marking guide quality. It is marking disclosure itself.

### Micron's condition is met. The tape disagrees.

Micron Technology (MU) closed at $881.47, its third consecutive close inside the $850–950 range the system watches for re-engagement. The dwell condition this brief has asked for on three separate runs is now satisfied twice over, and the condition says add. The day's strongest evidence from the rest of the tape says the opposite: both memory names that reported were sold regardless of what they guided, and the one that stayed silent was bought. Micron trades at 5.7 times forward earnings on a quarter the market appears unwilling to capitalise — which is precisely what its own research warned of, peak earnings times a multiple that still embeds permanence. This is the third good-print-bad-tape instance in memory and storage. The contradiction is recorded, not resolved.

## Graph Signal

The cleanest divergence today sits inside Axon, and it is not the price. The company has changed five measurement definitions across two layers in four quarters — three in the non-statutory accounting measures, two in the operating metrics. The count alone is not the finding; the asymmetry is. The two changes **not** restated to prior periods are the two that flatter adjusted earnings before interest, tax, depreciation and amortisation and adjusted gross margin — the exact two measures management guides on and the market capitalises. The one change that **was** restated feeds the earnings-per-share line, and that is the line which, honestly compared, shows a decline: $1.88 against $2.18. So "adjusted profit rose over 40 percent" and "adjusted gross margin fell only 40 basis points" are not like-for-like, because the numerator uses a definition adopted this quarter and the comparator does not. Bookings moved to a five-year normalised basis with no bridge to the old one. Every change is disclosed, and every one is certified immaterial; the immaterial amount is never quantified. Set that beside an operating income line numerically identical to a one-off tariff refund, and the raised guide reads differently than the headline invites. The flag is the cadence and its direction, not any single change. A sixth would escalate it.
