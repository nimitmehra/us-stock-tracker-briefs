## Rates & Macro

The ten-year Treasury — the rate that prices long-dated stocks — closed at 4.63%, up three basis points, its highest since mid-May. The inflation-stripped ten-year rose two points to 2.37%, its sixth straight day above the danger zone. The two-year, the market's read on the Fed, added five points to 4.26%. So even as chips soared, the rate backdrop got worse, not better — and the move was not benign disinflation. The ten-year breakeven, the market's inflation bet, held at 2.26% and is pushed by oil, not pulled down by cooling prices. Today was a headwind for the AI book: real rates, inflation expectations and the crude cost line all point the same way.

The exit line for the AI trade is unchanged — the inflation-stripped ten-year holding above roughly 2.25–2.30%, or the two-year pushing toward 4.4–4.5%. Real yields sit inside that zone for a sixth day and are still rising, not backing away. The macro removes the cushion. The Fed is in its pre-meeting quiet period before the 28th–29th, a hold is near-certain, but futures now put roughly two-in-three odds on a rate *hike* by September as oil reinflation resurfaces. Brent is $92.29, up almost a fifth on the month, the US–Iran war in its tenth day — though a mediation offer and a possible ten-day truce gave the crude tape its first two-sided session in two weeks. Alphabet reports Wednesday after the close: the first mega-cap, and the market's referee on whether AI capital spending turns into profit.

## What Happened

### Memory priced power, not a bounce

The memory chip cohort ripped on hard evidence, not hope. Micron Technology (MU), a portfolio holding, rose 12.2% to $970.82; Sandisk (SNDK) 14.3%, Seagate Technology (STX) 11.1% — all three held by the principal. Three catalysts stacked in one session. Morgan Stanley raised its third-quarter memory-price forecasts — contract DRAM up 13–18%, NAND up 10–15%. Taiwan Semiconductor (TSM) confirmed a roughly 10% price hike for 2027 and a $100bn Arizona expansion. And South Korea's chip exports printed a record +180.6% year-on-year. Bank of America lifted its Micron target to $1,550 and flagged high-bandwidth memory sold out through 2027. Morgan Stanley's own note called this peak rate-of-change, not peak cycle — the caution that got no airtime on an up-14% day.

### The index bounced; the hedges didn't buy it

The broad tape snapped a three-day losing streak, but the shape was a squeeze, not a bottom. The most-shorted, most-beaten memory and optics names led — each still down 15% to 33% on the month — while the mega-cap AI leaders lagged: NVIDIA (NVDA) trailed the chip index, Microsoft (MSFT) fell 1.1%. Eight of eleven sectors were flat-to-red; the whole gain was semiconductors. The tell sat in the hedges: the stock-market fear gauge (VIX) fell 8.6%, but its options-tail cousin (SKEW) and the bond-market volatility gauge (MOVE) both rose. Buyers reached for junk while quietly paying up for protection. A real bottom broadens and comes with falling real yields; this one narrowed and came with rising ones. Both facts hold — the memory names have a genuine leg the index lacks.

### Bloom Energy financed the fall

Bloom Energy (BE), the fuel-cell maker and a portfolio holding, rose 14.8% to $226.26 on a real catalyst — closing the thread from its unexplained break below $200 last week. Industrial Development Funding and Oaktree committed $1.7bn to deploy Bloom fuel cells behind the meter across Nebius's US data centres, with Morgan Stanley as tax-equity investor and MUFG on senior debt. JPMorgan raised its price target to $346. The formal SEC filing has not yet posted; a material-agreement disclosure typically follows a press release by one to four business days. The drawdown was positioning ahead of a financeable demand catalyst, not fundamental damage. The macro that pressures Bloom — oil and real yields — got worse today even as the stock rose, so the case to trim into strength stands rather than softens.

## Cascade Watch

### Rotation within AI, not out of it

- **What happened:** Money left the mega-cap AI leaders — NVIDIA underperformed the chip index by 2.6 points, Microsoft trailed tech by 4 points — and chased beaten-down memory, optics and chip-tool names up 5% to 14%.
- **Why it happened:** Dip-buyers reached down the AI quality curve after a steep prior-week selloff, funding the trade by selling the consensus longs and high-multiple software.
- **How it matters:** The AI thesis is broadening in leadership, not breaking; but low-quality-led leadership is the signature of a reflex rally, so treat the index move as a squeeze even where the memory sub-cohort's fundamentals are real.

### Memory pricing feeds the same two edges again

- **What happened:** Micron's pricing-power signal refreshed its two graph links — to the semiconductor sector and to the AI-disruption theme — both already at maximum strength.
- **Why it happened:** Foundry price hikes, analyst memory upgrades and record Korean exports all confirm the same up-the-stack demand story, so the links reinforce rather than expand.
- **How it matters:** The AI-demand edge is now saturated on hard-dollar evidence, not sentiment — the memory names carry a real leg into a macro that still caps the multiple.
