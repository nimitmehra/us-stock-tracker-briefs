## What Happened

### Cloudflare beat. Cloudflare crashed.

Cloudflare (NET), the edge-platform leader, reported Q1 revenue of $639.8m (+34% YoY), beat consensus EPS by 47 percent and lifted FY26 guidance — then closed -22 percent. The same release announced a 1,100-job cut, roughly a fifth of headcount, framed as an "agentic AI-first operating model" pivot. Block and Oracle ran similar plays earlier and were rewarded; Cloudflare's reaction is the inverse. The cleanest read is that a +34 percent revenue line is too strong to support a "we cut to enable AI-first" framing — the market read the cut as a forward demand admission, not a productivity win. The trim/add line resets to the after-hours close.

### Memory kept running.

Micron Technology (MU) closed at $706.51, up 9.3 percent and at a fresh all-time high — Day-2 above the $600 super-trim sub-threshold and 41 percent above the original $500 trim line. Sandisk (SNDK) followed at +8.5 percent, also a new high. Beyond the portfolio, the broader compute and memory cohort confirmed the read — half a dozen mid-to-high single-digit gains across the chip stack — as Digitimes reported (single-source) that hyperscaler-side memory buyers are offering to pre-fund SK Hynix capacity. Moderna (MRNA) ran 10.6 percent on no fresh catalyst; Agent B's price-attribution failed and the move stands un-explained on the desk's record. The trim instruction was written for this exact tape.

### Capex shows up at the contract level. Again.

NVIDIA (NVDA) disclosed a strategic partnership with IREN Limited covering up to $2.1bn of equity — a five-year option on 30 million shares at $70 — plus a $3.4bn AI cloud services contract for Blackwell GPUs; $5.5bn combined, up to 5GW of DSX-aligned data-center capacity. Akamai separately announced the largest deal in its history: a seven-year, $1.8bn cloud-infrastructure contract with a leading frontier-model provider, sending its shares up 20 percent. Including Tuesday's $3.2bn NVIDIA-Corning fibre partnership, three multi-billion-dollar AI-infrastructure contracts have been disclosed inside a single trading week. The signal is capex-cycle persistence at the contract-disclosure level, not capex-cycle peaking.

### Oil rebid. The tariff toolkit narrowed.

US Navy destroyers exchanged fire with Iranian forces in the Strait of Hormuz overnight; US strikes hit three Iranian ports and Iran's missile, drone and small-boat counterattacks were intercepted with no US damage. Brent rose 1.0 percent to $101 and WTI 0.2 percent to $95 on the news, though both remain down about 6 percent on the week. President Trump called the strikes "just a love tap" and said the ceasefire holds — operationally meaningful framing, since the deal pathway depends on it. Separately, the US Court of International Trade struck down the Section 122 global-tariff authority 2-1 — the second executive tariff authority struck down at the trial level inside ninety days. The toolkit is at its narrowest since 2018.
