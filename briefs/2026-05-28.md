## What Happened

### The memory supercycle is now a contract, not a forecast

Micron Technology (MU), the largest American maker of memory chips, traded at $945.67 intraday on Thursday, up 1.9 percent and at a fresh all-time high above a trillion dollars in value. The thesis stopped being a forecast. Second-quarter contract prices are set up 58 to 63 percent for dynamic memory and 70 to 75 percent for flash — the widest deficit since 2011, with AI servers now two-thirds of demand. Seagate Technology (STX) and Sandisk (SNDK), the other storage holdings, both touched records, up 2.8 and 6.1 percent. The same shortage cuts the other way for the buyers: Apple raised MacBook Pro prices by up to $400 and warned on device margins. Micron's 25 June print is where a sold-out order book meets a guide.

### Discount retail beat, and beat again

Dollar Tree (DLTR) and Best Buy (BBY) both cleared the quarter and lifted the year, up 17.8 and 18.8 percent intraday. Dollar Tree raised its full-year earnings outlook to $6.70–7.10 after a 38 percent jump in quarterly earnings — the margin coming from ticket and the multi-price conversion, not footfall, since April traffic ran negative. Best Buy turned comparable sales positive at +2.0 percent, reaffirmed its year, and named Jason Bonfig to succeed Corie Barry, chief executive since 2019. The clean Best Buy read is the +11 percent adjusted line, not the +38 percent headline a restructuring credit flattered. Dollar General (DG) rose 6.4 percent into its own 2 June print. The trade-down is real, against a federal food-aid cut still hanging over the group.

### The ceasefire broke. The disinflation bet unwound.

The US–Iran truce took fire overnight. Overnight US strikes hit drone sites near the Strait of Hormuz and a control station at Bandar Abbas; Iran answered with a ballistic missile at a US air base in Kuwait, intercepted, no casualties. Both sides now accuse the other of breaking it, and the Hormuz-reopening framework is imperiled. The bet the 27 May brief flagged as a forming regime — oil down, yields down, peace on — reversed in a single night. The morning data added a stagflation tint: the Fed's preferred inflation gauge ran at a three-year-high 3.8 percent over the year, the monthly core cooled, and first-quarter growth was marked down to 1.6 percent. Yesterday's rotation rested on an unsigned deal, and the deal just took fire.

### Microsoft won the Pentagon's enterprise book

Microsoft Corporation (MSFT) rose 2.8 percent intraday, its cleanest single-name gain in weeks, on a $9.69 billion five-year enterprise-technology agreement with the Department of Defense. The deal consolidates software across the military, intelligence services and Coast Guard. A Morgan Stanley note the prior day added that Azure data-center capacity is set to scale from roughly five gigawatts to twenty by fiscal 2028. The stock sits well below its 52-week high, the rare mega-cap with room rather than a record to defend. A government anchor tenant is the kind of revenue the AI-spend bears cannot wave away.

### Cloudflare ran on an Anthropic tie-up — reportedly

Cloudflare (NET), the principal's edge-network holding, rose 7.8 percent intraday, the day's largest gain among portfolio names and a sharp reversal of the prior session's software-derate sympathy. Reports — aggregated, with no primary release located — tie the move to an expanded artificial-intelligence-infrastructure partnership with Anthropic, layered on a stronger-than-expected first quarter and the company's AI-first restructuring. The deal would put Cloudflare in the AI-data-consumption winner camp the market rewarded in Snowflake after hours, narrowing the open question of whether last session's fall was a blanket derate or soft-guide risk. Until a primary source surfaces, both the partnership and the move are reports, not fact.
