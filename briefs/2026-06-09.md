## What Happened

### The market rotated out of tech, not down

Tuesday looked like a slide and was a rotation. The S&P 500 closed at 7,386.65, down 0.26 percent, but the Dow rose 0.17 percent, the Russell 2000 of small caps rose 0.41 percent, and more than 350 of the 500 index members closed higher. The Nasdaq, down 0.97 percent, was off more than 3.6 percent intraday before a buy-the-dip clawed most of it back. The driver was last Friday's strong May jobs report — 172,000 jobs against an 85,000 forecast — which pushed traders to price a Federal Reserve that holds rates higher for longer, even a hike by year-end. That punishes long-duration growth and pays the cheap side of the market: homebuilders, real-estate trusts, airlines and staples all caught a bid while cloud software, optics and chips were sold. The bid moved to the parts of the market the AI trade had starved.

### Apple's Siri fade deepened; Cloudflare sold its own investor day

Two single names told the cleanest stories, and both were the price already knowing. Apple Inc. (AAPL) fell 3.64 percent to $290.55, a second down-leg after Monday's developer-conference reveal of a rebuilt "Siri AI" carried no shipping date for a feature already slipped for two years. Morgan Stanley nudged its target up to $360 into the fade. But the skeptics at Rosenblatt and KeyBanc read a defensive catch-up with no monetization clock, and the target bump did not arrest the slide. Cloudflare, Inc. (NET), the edge-network and security firm, fell 4.71 percent to $236.13 after its investor day reaffirmed — rather than raised — its goal of $5 billion in annual revenue by the end of 2028; brokers had lifted targets into the event expecting an acceleration, and the reaffirmation unwound the bet. Neither name had bad news. The market had simply pre-paid for more.

### The AI trade split — periphery sold, core held

The de-rating was surgical, not wholesale. The AI periphery took the damage — optical names Coherent (−11.4 percent), Lumentum (−8.2 percent) and Corning (−7.3 percent), custom-silicon maker Marvell (−7.6 percent, giving back Monday's pop), AI-server builders, and the highest-multiple software. The AI core held its ground: NVIDIA Corporation (NVDA) closed −0.22 percent after being down more than 4 percent intraday, the memory makers Micron (MU) and Sandisk (SNDK) were flat-to-green, and the fuel-cell power name Bloom Energy (BE) rose 2.38 percent. A Broadcom-led reset of how much hyperscalers will spend on AI gear was the proximate trigger, with a single-source rumor that NVIDIA may halve the commodity memory on its next-generation Rubin racks adding an intraday needle — a rumor that explicitly spares the high-bandwidth memory that anchors the Micron thesis. The market is discriminating within the AI trade, not abandoning it.

### Oil fell on deal talk, even as a fresh Hormuz incident flared

The Middle East cut the other way from the morning's fears. Crude fell — West Texas Intermediate down 2.15 percent to $89.34 — after President Trump said an Iran deal was "two or three days" away and the Strait of Hormuz would reopen "immediately" on a deal. Pulling against that, Mr Trump accused Iran of shooting down a US Apache helicopter patrolling the strait, which kept a risk-off bid under equities even as oil sold. Energy producers and oil-services names tracked crude lower (the exploration-and-production fund XOP −2.58 percent), and a precious-metals washout ran alongside — gold −2.40 percent, silver −5.31 percent — on the same dollar-and-yields-up regime and fading odds of an Iran peace. The market faded the geopolitical premium and judged the helicopter as noise; the two-sided tail now lives in the overnight hours.
