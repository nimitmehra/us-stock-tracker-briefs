## What Happened

### HEICO beat. Electronics did it.

HEICO Corporation (HEI), the aircraft-parts and defence-electronics maker on our watch list, filed third-quarter results **seventy-three minutes after Tuesday's close**. Net sales rose **23.1% to $1,413.1m**, diluted earnings **$1.67 against $1.26**, operating margin **25.14% from 23.10%**, cash from operations up **49%**. Consolidated organic growth was 14%. Underneath it the two halves moved apart. Flight Support organic **decelerated to 12% against 15% for the nine months**. Electronic Technologies **accelerated to 18% from 14%** and lifted its margin 321 basis points to **25.97%** — above Flight Support's 25.88% for the first time in these figures. HEICO gives no numeric guidance and never has; the absence is not a signal. The multiple was underwritten on the aftermarket, and the aftermarket is no longer the engine.

### Memory round-tripped on nothing.

The whole of Monday's memory selloff came back on Tuesday, and Korea proved it before New York opened. Samsung Electronics had fallen 8.70% on Monday after promising 90–110tn won of shareholder returns against roughly 150tn expected, with no share cancellation. On Tuesday the Korean market index fell more than 4% intraday and **closed up 0.68%**; Samsung closed up 0.19%. Our own names followed — Seagate Technology (STX) +3.40%, Micron Technology (MU) +2.48%, the memory and storage cohort +3.08%. **Not one issuer in either country disclosed a memory, chip or storage datapoint inside the window.** Monday was a payout decision in a demand costume — or positioning noise round-tripping into an Nvidia print. Both fit; neither is upgraded. Sandisk (SNDK) fell 0.83% through both sessions and nobody can say why.

### Canada's list now taxes phones.

Prime Minister Mark Carney announced matching counter-tariffs on Tuesday: **C$27.6bn of American goods across more than 700 items, effective 8 September**. Steel and aluminium duties double to 50%, with new 50% duties on milk, furniture and clothing. The item that changes our exposure map sits further down the schedule — **video-game consoles, smartphones and other consumer electronics**. This dispute has been a steel-and-autos story for a month. **No US–Canada retaliation schedule has named finished consumer electronics before.** Apple (AAPL) is not named; the duty is on the category, not the company. **Nobody published a dollar figure inside this window, so we do not have one either.** American tariffs on Canadian goods have been in force since 22 August. The category is on the list, and it was not on Monday.

### The index rose. The average stock fell.

The S&P 500 closed up 0.32% while the **equal-weighted average constituent fell 0.079%**, and only 207 of 501 names advanced. The gap between the two is **+0.399 percentage points — the largest positive reading in the ten-session series**. Monday produced the mirror image at −0.370. Those are the two strongest forms this measure can take, back to back in opposite directions. That is a round trip in one factor, not a turn. **The funding leg is not ambiguous.** Consumer staples fell 1.21% on average, with only three of twenty-six names higher, on a session the real discount rate fell six basis points. A defensive sector that ignores its own best input is not responding to rates. It is paying for something else, and what it bought reports Wednesday night.

### Dick's fell 30% on what it bought.

DICK'S Sporting Goods (DKS) had **the worst day in its listed history**, cutting full-year earnings guidance to **$11.00–12.00 against a $14.20 consensus** — a 19% shortfall at the midpoint. The split inside the quarter runs the opposite way to the headline. **Dick's own stores comped up 4.9%. Foot Locker (FL), which it bought last year, comped down 3.6%**, and Foot Locker's full-year guide was cut to flat-to-minus-two from plus one-and-a-half-to-three. **This is not a datapoint about the consumer and must not be filed as one** — a +4.9% comp at the acquirer is a good comp. It is a datapoint about capital allocation: a healthy retailer bought a broken one, and the broken one now sets the consolidated number. Our own price file has never contained DKS at all.
