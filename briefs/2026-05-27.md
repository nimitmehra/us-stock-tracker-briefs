## What Happened

### Micron crossed a trillion. Chips sold off.

Micron Technology (MU), the largest American maker of memory chips, touched a $1 trillion market value for the first time on Wednesday — an intraday high of $963.90 before easing to $901.46. It rose at a fresh record while the broad semiconductor complex fell hard, the sector index down 2.3 percent and its most-extended names off 4 to 9 percent. Seagate Technology (STX), the principal's storage holding, did the same — a fresh record, three points ahead of the sector. For a second day the makers of memory climbed as the chips built around them sold off. The market is paying only for capacity already sold out — three-year supply contracts, server-memory up over 100 percent off the lows — and trimming what ran ahead of demand. Yesterday's leaders funded the rotation; today they lead it alone.

### Software's cash quality cracked. Cloudflare fell with it.

Zscaler (ZS), a cloud-security firm, beat sales estimates for the quarter but cut its free-cash-flow margin guidance to about 23 percent from 27 percent. The shares fell 16 to 17 percent after hours, dragging the cybersecurity group with them. Cloudflare (NET), the principal's holding in the cohort, fell 2.8 percent — about two points worse than the tech tape, on the sector move, not anything company-specific. What the market punished was not growth but the quality of the cash behind it: sales beat, the cash-conversion guide did not. Stacked with Microsoft's (MSFT) retreat from agentic-coding tools and a survey showing few chief executives can yet show a return on AI spend, the doubt is no longer about one company. It is about what the software actually earns. The question the brief flagged on Tuesday now has its answer.

### The relief rally ran ahead of the relief.

For a third session, capital rotated out of the war trade and into its relief — airlines, cruise lines, casinos and homebuilders bid on cheaper fuel and lower rates. Energy services and the rate-sensitive brokers fell, and ten-year yields are down nineteen basis points on the week. But the relief is running ahead of its cause. The US–Iran framework that drives it is still unsigned. The day's oil print is itself disputed — the price feed shows crude down a third day near $91; the news desk reads it holding near $99 on tight inventories. Underneath, the Federal Reserve is leaning the other way. Governor Christopher Waller said Friday a rate rise is "just as likely" as a cut, and Thursday's inflation report is forecast near 3.9 percent — the hottest since 2023. The one number that would justify the rotation is going the wrong way.

## Cascade Watch

### Memory decoupled up from the chip selloff

- **What happened:** Micron and Seagate rose to fresh records — three and four points ahead of the semiconductor index — while that index fell 2.3 percent and its weakest names dropped 4 to 9 percent; Micron's links to the chip sector and the AI-buildout theme are pinned at their ceiling and refreshed again.
- **Why it happened:** The market is paying only for memory capacity already locked into multi-year supply contracts and trimming the chips that ran ahead of demand — memory is sold out, the rest is not.
- **How it matters:** The portfolio sits on both sides of this — it owns the winning makers (Micron, Seagate, Sandisk) and the paying buyers (Microsoft, Alphabet, Nvidia). The split is now the single most thesis-relevant signal in the book; Micron's 25 June print is its test.

### Cheaper oil fired the travel cascade a third day

- **What happened:** Falling fuel lifted airlines, cruise lines and casinos; the graph's oil links to Delta, Carnival and FedEx fired again, and FedEx's link to crude strengthened past the half-point threshold for a second day.
- **Why it happened:** A roughly 15-percent weekly drop in crude on the unsigned Iran framework feeds straight through to the fuel-cost-sensitive transport and leisure margins.
- **How it matters:** The rotation is real on the week's oil move regardless of the disputed day-print, but it rests on a deal that has not been signed — treat the travel cohort as a momentum trade, not a confirmed regime.

### Oral weight-loss drugs tightened their drag on packaged food

- **What happened:** New approvals and opinions for oral weight-loss drugs (Novo Nordisk's oral Wegovy in Europe, Eli Lilly's Foundayo) doubled the graph's link from weight-loss demand to General Mills and lifted the one to McCormick.
- **Why it happened:** An easier-to-take pill widens the treatable population, which the engine reads as a structural volume headwind for packaged-food makers.
- **How it matters:** Note the cross-current — General Mills and McCormick both rose about 2 percent today on the defensive rotation, the opposite of the structural drag; the rotation is a trade, the weight-loss read is the thesis.

## Graph Signal

The portfolio is long both sides of the memory supercycle, and today the graph drew the line between them. It owns the makers — Micron, Seagate, Sandisk — now winning outright, decoupling upward from a chip complex that sold off 2 to 9 percent. It also owns the buyers — Microsoft, Alphabet and Nvidia — who pay for that memory and lagged again. The new wrinkle is valuation. Micron's crossing of a trillion dollars means the maker side is now priced for the supercycle, while the payer side is not; the winning trade has quietly become the expensive one. That is the asymmetry to watch: the same names that have carried the book are the ones with the least margin for a disappointment. Micron's 25 June print, defending an all-time revenue guide, is where the priced-for-perfection side gets tested.

## Sector Pulse

Two cohorts moved as one today, and neither maps to a single sector fund. Travel and leisure — United, Delta, Carnival, Royal Caribbean, Norwegian and MGM all up 4 to 10 percent — ran on cheaper fuel and a lower cost of money; it was the cleanest single-factor move of the day. Its mirror image is the rate-sensitive financials: brokers and asset-gatherers (Raymond James, Schwab, Ameriprise, Interactive Brokers) fell 3 to 6 percent as falling yields squeeze the income they earn on client cash — a move no single headline explains, which the desk has flagged for corroboration. The same disinflation impulse that feeds the consumer starves the financials. Both are trades on the oil move; both unwind if the Iran deal slips.

The bear case stacks underneath: a hot inflation print Thursday, a Fed leaning toward a hike, a stalled oil crash, and the AI-software return doubts that Zscaler's cash warning just sharpened. The clearest tell is in oil itself — US outlets carried the Iran deal as nearly done, yet crude refused to extend its crash and the deal stayed unsigned. The one asset that has to confirm the disinflation story is the one declining to.
