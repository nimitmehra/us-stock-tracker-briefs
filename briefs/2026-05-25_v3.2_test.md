## What Happened

### Iran framework disputed; oil traded the press conference

Washington Post, Axios and CNN reported a Trump-Iran framework "ninety-five percent there" over the weekend — sixty-day ceasefire, the Strait of Hormuz reopening toll-free, Iran clearing mines and returning shipping to pre-war levels over thirty days, sanctions waivers, a US commitment that Iran "can never possess a nuclear weapon." Brent fell 4.8 percent to $98.52 over the global session, the first sub-hundred print this month. The trade is the press conference, not the strait. Three Iranian state outlets contradicted the US framing — Fars insists Hormuz stays under Iranian control, ISNA says no nuclear or enriched-uranium commitment has been made, and Tasnim warns the agreement "may be canceled" amid alleged US obstruction. The Abu Dhabi national oil company's chief executive said physical Hormuz flows may not normalise before 2027 even if conflict ended now. This is the textbook single-interested-source pattern; the premium can re-price violently if any state-media contradiction sticks.

### Huawei sharpens the China concession

NVIDIA (NVDA), back in Tier 1 from the run-start graph snapshot, watched competitor Huawei present its LogicFolding chip roadmap in Shanghai on Monday — targeting Kirin smartphone chips this autumn and claiming a path to 1.4-nanometre-equivalent capability by 2031 against the TSMC 2-nanometre node in volume now. Layered onto Jensen Huang's 21 May statement to CNBC that NVIDIA has "largely conceded" the China artificial-intelligence chip market to Huawei — "expect nothing" on near-term licence approvals — the two together harden the read that China is now a Huawei-local market. The Q2 ninety-one-billion-dollar guide ex-China is the upside case, not the base case. NVDA's deep-research refresh fires at Tuesday's reopen.

### Microsoft cancels Claude Code; agent economics surface inside the hyperscaler

Fortune's 22 May reporting on Microsoft's internal cost data — agentic-artificial-intelligence per-task token consumption exceeding the equivalent human-labour cost in 92 percent of agentic adopters — was sharpened over the holiday by a Verge report that Microsoft is cancelling most direct Claude Code licences roughly six months after rollout. The story cuts two ways. It is bearish for the Copilot-monetisation and artificial-intelligence-margin narrative on hyperscaler platforms; it is a near-term reprieve for incumbent seat-based subscription software — Adobe, ServiceNow, Workday, Cloudflare — because the agentic-disruption case requires agent unit economics to clear human costs, which they conspicuously do not yet do. Same evidence, two implications, deployed across a two-versus-three-year horizon.
