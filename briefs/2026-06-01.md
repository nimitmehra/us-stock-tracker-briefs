## What Happened

### Alphabet raised $80 billion. Berkshire bought $10 billion of it.

Alphabet Inc. (GOOGL) disclosed a roughly $80 billion equity raise to fund artificial-intelligence compute, in a preliminary offering document filed late Monday — after the tape had closed. The structure is three-part: $30 billion of underwritten stock, half of it mandatory-convertible preferred that softens near-term dilution; a $40 billion at-the-market sale program beginning in the third quarter; and a $10 billion private placement to Berkshire Hathaway at fixed prices of $351.81 and $348.20. Shares closed −1.0 percent, before any of this was public. The most cash-generative company in the world is funding its AI build with dilution rather than its own cash flow — a tell about the size of the spend. Berkshire writing part of the floor is the counter-tell.

### The market split AI in two.

Jensen Huang, NVIDIA Corporation's (NVDA) chief executive and founder since 1993, used his Computex keynote to frame agentic AI as a tailwind for software rather than a threat — and the tape rotated hard on it. Capital poured into the software and AI-chip beneficiaries and fled the companies paying the capital bill. Cloudflare (NET) led the portfolio up 12.0 percent to a record; Micron Technology (MU) rose 6.6 percent and NVIDIA 6.3 percent, both to all-time highs. On the other side, the capex-spenders were sold to fund the move: Meta Platforms (META) −5.1 percent, Amazon.com (AMZN) −3.5 percent, Apple (AAPL) −1.8 percent. Indices held green only on mega-cap weight. The market has stopped trading AI as one block.

### Oil reversed. Defense did not bid.

Crude ripped on Monday. West Texas Intermediate rose 5.85 percent to $92.47 and Brent 3.53 percent to $95.30, as the US-Iran understanding stayed unsigned and Iran signaled it could close the Strait of Hormuz. The move reverses a brutal May — Brent's worst month since March 2020. The cross-current is what the energy bid did not buy: with a war premium back in oil, the aerospace-and-defense group sold rather than bid as a haven, its sector fund down 2.4 percent. Defense usually rallies on a Middle-East premium; today it was a funding source instead. The rotation was strong enough to override even a war.

## Sector Pulse

The day's defining move was a thematic cohort no single sector fund captures cleanly: the AI-software re-rating. More than twenty names — Salesforce, Oracle, ServiceNow, Snowflake, Datadog, CoreWeave and the portfolio's Cloudflare among them — rose 7 to 14 percent on one keynote, with the cloud-software fund up 7.3 percent its largest print of the year. The cause was Huang's reframing of agentic AI as something that *consumes* more software, not something that replaces it — flipping a six-week-old disruption fear. Its mirror image was the AI-power unwind: utilities (the worst sector, −3.0 percent), the nuclear-power names and Bloom Energy all sold as money left the "AI needs electricity" leg for the "AI is software" leg. The deployment read: the market is now pricing AI winners against AI losers within the theme, not the theme as a block — a regime the portfolio's software and memory names sit on the right side of.
