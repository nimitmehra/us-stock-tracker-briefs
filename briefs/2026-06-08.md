## What Happened

### Chips snapped back. Bonds did not confirm.

The hardest-hit names led the rebound. Micron Technology (MU), the largest American maker of memory chips, rose 9.87 percent to $949.28 after Susquehanna lifted its price target to $1,750 from $600 and the company reiterated its 2026 high-bandwidth memory is sold out under binding contracts. Intel (INTC) jumped 11.19 percent on a Google order for more than three million of its own AI chips to be built at Intel's foundry, and Marvell (MRVL) gained 9.63 percent ahead of its 22 June entry to the S&P 500. The semiconductor exchange-traded fund (SMH) rose 5.0 percent and the volatility gauge (VIX) fell below 20. But the long bond kept selling — the ten-year yield rose to 4.552 percent and the long-Treasury fund (TLT) fell — so Friday's rate shock has not cleared. This was a dip-buy of the oversold, not an all-clear.

### Apple unveiled Siri AI. The market sold it.

Apple Inc. (AAPL) used its developer conference to reveal "Siri AI" — a rebuilt, conversational assistant running on Apple's own models "in collaboration with Google and its Gemini." The stock ran to about $317 intraday, then reversed to close $301.54, down 1.89 percent, the day's worst Tier 1 showing on a tech-up tape. Two things did the damage: Apple gave no ship date for a feature already slipped repeatedly, and the Gemini reliance reframes Apple as an integrator of someone else's intelligence rather than a leader in its own. Gene Munster and others pinned the fade on the missing timeline. The read-through ran the other way for Alphabet Inc. (GOOGL), whose models now sit inside Apple's assistant. What was not said carried the day, not what was.

### AI split in two: chips up, power down.

The bounce was narrow on purpose. The market re-bought demonstrated AI demand — memory pricing, foundry orders, optical components — and left the speculative power-infrastructure leg behind. Bloom Energy (BE), the fuel-cell maker that supplies data-centre power, fell 3.81 percent to $253.57 with no company news, the lone Tier 1 decliner, while the uranium and independent-power cohort also failed to join. Utilities (XLU −1.87 percent) and real estate (XLRE −1.50 percent), Friday's safe-haven winners, were sold to fund the chip rebound. The dividing line is whether the AI cash is already flowing or still merely promised. Monday paid only for the cash that has arrived.

### Iran and Israel traded fire, then stepped back.

The most serious Middle East confrontation since April's ceasefire flared over the weekend — Iran fired missiles at Israel on Sunday, Israel struck central Iran early Monday — before Iran's foreign ministry told CNBC it had ceased fire, conditioned on Israel halting its Lebanon operations. Crude spiked, with Brent above $98 intraday, then handed almost all of it back to close $93.50, up 0.44 percent. Oil-services names (OIH +3.64 percent) held a thin Gulf-risk premium and defence carried a quiet re-escalation bid. The tape treated it as a contained flare, not a regime break. The contingent risk now lives in the overnight hours, where a Lebanon spillover would reopen it.

## Cascade Watch

- **What happened:** Micron led a 5 percent semiconductor-fund rebound, and the graph's links from both the semiconductor sector and the AI-disruption theme back to Micron fired on confirmed evidence — its reiterated "high-bandwidth memory sold out for 2026."
- **Why it happened:** the sector bounce was concentrated in exactly the demonstrated-demand names, and Micron's sold-out reiteration is the cleanest read that AI buildout is still paying up for memory.
- **How it matters:** these links are now at their maximum weight — the memory-to-AI connection is as tight as the graph allows, so the 24 June Micron print is the next event that can either confirm or break the most heavily-wired thesis in the book.

### The AI complex split into two cohorts

- **What happened:** chips, optics and foundry recovered — Micron, Intel, Marvell, the equipment names, the optical cohort — while the power-infrastructure names (Bloom Energy −3.81 percent, plus uranium and independent-power plays) kept falling.
- **Why it happened:** capital re-bought AI demand that is already converting to cash and left behind the leg whose revenue is still a multi-year promise.
- **How it matters:** the split tells you where the market's confidence sits today — it strengthens the demonstrated-winner holdings and counsels patience on the power names until their cash arrives.

### Risk recovered but the bond market stayed put

- **What happened:** the volatility gauge fell 12 percent and the chip fund rose 5 percent, but the ten-year yield rose, the long-Treasury fund fell, and the bond-volatility gauge ticked up.
- **Why it happened:** Friday's shock was a rate shock fused with an AI-spending scare; Monday undid the equity scare but not the rate one.
- **How it matters:** a relief rally the bond market refuses to confirm is fragile — Wednesday's inflation print decides whether the bounce holds or Friday's regime reasserts.

## Sector Pulse

The AI trade bifurcated cleanly on Monday, and the split is the week's most informative cohort signal. On one side, the demonstrated-demand names ran together — Intel up 11 percent on the Google foundry win, Marvell up 10 percent on its index inclusion, Micron up 10 percent on sold-out memory, the equipment cohort (KLAC, AMAT, LRCX) up 7 to 9 percent, and the optical names (Coherent, Corning, Lumentum) bid on capacity commentary. On the other, the power-infrastructure cohort — fuel cells, uranium, independent power — did not participate at all. The driver is a flight to AI revenue that has already converted to cash and away from the leg still underwritten by future contracts. For deployment, it favours the chips-and-optics holdings and argues for patience on Bloom Energy until its data-centre revenue is demonstrated rather than promised; Intel's foundry inflection is now worth its own research track.
