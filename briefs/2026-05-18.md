## What Happened

### Bonds and oil tightened. Equity hedges did not.

The ten-year Treasury opened Monday at 4.617 percent and touched 4.63 intraday — a fresh 15-month high above Friday's 4.595 close. Moody's stripped the last AAA from US sovereign credit late Friday, citing federal deficits rising from seven to nine percent of GDP by 2034. Standard & Poor's removed the AAA in 2011; Fitch in 2023. CME FedWatch now prices zero cuts in 2026 and a roughly fifty-percent chance of a December hike. Brent traded to about $111 in Monday pre-market after three drones struck the United Arab Emirates' Barakah nuclear plant on Sunday — the first kinetic event at civilian nuclear infrastructure in this conflict. Defense primes did not bid on the news for a seventh consecutive session. Wednesday's Nvidia print decides the week.

### Abel built Alphabet. Berkshire exited Amazon.

Berkshire Hathaway's first 13F under Greg Abel — Warren Buffett's successor as chief executive earlier this year — lifted the Alphabet (GOOGL) stake by 204 percent. The new position spans 36.4 million Class A shares plus a fresh 3.59 million Class C piece, aggregating roughly $23 billion and a top-five Berkshire holding. The same filing opened a new 40 million-share position in Delta Air Lines (DAL), Berkshire's first airline holding since the 2020 mass exits. Sixteen names were fully sold — Amazon (AMZN) among them, alongside Visa, Mastercard, UnitedHealth and Domino's. Bill Ackman's Pershing Square exited Alphabet in the same quarter to fund a new Microsoft position. Two of the most-followed value books in US equities took opposite-direction Alphabet bets in the same window. Google I/O on Tuesday at 10am Pacific is the near-term arbiter.

### Microsoft cleared its structural seller.

Pershing Square's 13F detailed a new $2.1 billion Microsoft (MSFT) position, accumulated since February. Bill Ackman framed the buy at 21 times forward earnings as "broadly in line with the market multiple, and well below Microsoft's trading average," and gave the OpenAI economic interest as the latent value. The Bill & Melinda Gates Foundation Trust closed its final 7.7 million-share Microsoft holding the same week — the last trade of the foundation's 25-year position. TCI was also reducing. Microsoft closed Friday up 3.05 percent, alpha of 4.86 points against the technology sector, on an otherwise tech-down day. The marginal buyer arrived in the same window the structural seller left.

### Sandisk's rally meets a $3 billion supply shock.

Western Digital announced a $3.086 billion secondary offering of Sandisk (SNDK) stock on Sunday — the largest selling-stockholder placement in the company's history. Sandisk closed Friday at $1,407.61, up 411 percent year-to-date, and was the only major memory name to outperform the semiconductor ETF on its worst day of 2026. The offering reverses that setup at Monday's open. Secondary placements at parabolic prices function as discrete supply shocks regardless of the underlying business read. The Friday alpha and the Monday supply setup pull in opposite directions across the same 48-hour window. Sandisk's first real test of float is the Monday open.
