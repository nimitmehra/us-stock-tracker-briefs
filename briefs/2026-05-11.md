## What Happened

### Bloom landed. Constellation slipped to 2031.

Bloom Energy (BE), the fuel-cell maker added to the portfolio on 6 May, closed up 11.58 percent — the largest single-day Tier 1 gain of the cycle. Oracle named Bloom sole power supplier for 2.45 gigawatts at Project Jupiter, which will host OpenAI infrastructure; Q1 revenue grew 130 percent and beat consensus by 42 percent. Constellation Energy (CEG), paired with Bloom on the same thesis, reported adjusted operating earnings up 28 percent that morning and fell 3.86 percent — its Three Mile Island restart has slipped to 2031. Mary Bush, a Bloom director, sold 6.67 million dollars of stock on 7 May with no 10b5-1 footnote. The thesis split into a now-cycle leg and a 2031 leg.

### Memory sold out. Micron ran.

Micron Technology (MU) closed at 786 dollars, up 5.25 percent — a third session above 700 and 57 percent above the original 500-dollar trim line. SK Hynix, the Korean memory peer, ran 15 percent in its own session to a fresh all-time high. The chief executive told the call that DRAM, NAND and HBM are completely sold out, and disclosed multi-year hyperscaler contracts replacing the quarterly pricing convention. UBS lifted its target on the peer from 1.55 million to 1.70 million won and raised FY26 and FY27 earnings forecasts by 22 and 29 percent. Sandisk (SNDK) and Seagate Technology (STX) — the NAND and HDD legs of the cohort — held their all-time highs. The trim-to-very-small action written for 600 dollars is well past should-already-have-done.

### The AI buildout dollar broadened down the chain.

Nvidia (NVDA), the GPU leader, closed up 2.94 percent — modest against the cohort it leads. The harder gains went down the supply chain: optical-photonics names ran 5 to 16 percent; storage and data-center-power names 5 to 11 percent. The mega-cap chip names outside Nvidia sat out or sold. Anthropic disclosed at the Code with Claude conference that revenue and usage are growing 80 times year-on-year; Akamai's seven-year cloud commitment was identified as the Anthropic deal. The graph promoted seven new Tier 2 names — Akamai, Palantir, IREN, Coherent, Corning, Qualcomm and Vertiv — the cohort the wires named with the move. The dollar paying for the AI buildout is no longer paying the GPU king; it is paying the picks and shovels behind him.

### Mosaic withdrew. Nitrogen won.

Mosaic Company (MOS) reported Q1 before Monday's open. Net sales rose 14 percent, but GAAP earnings flipped to an 81-cent loss, adjusted earnings missed at five cents against a 20-cent consensus, and adjusted EBITDA fell 24 percent. The company withdrew its FY26 phosphate-production guide, announced partial curtailments at Louisiana, Bartow and Brazil, and cut 2026 capex to 1.25 billion dollars. Sulfur prices hit a record 1,200 dollars a tonne and remain the structural raw-material chokepoint for phosphate. Shares fell 4.19 percent. Nitrogen-fertiliser peers ran the other way — CF Industries (CF) up 5.76 percent and Nutrien (NTR) up 3.76 percent on the parallel scarcity case. The fertiliser cohort is no longer a single trade; it is two opposite ones.

### Trump rejects. Hormuz love-tap is dead.

President Donald Trump called Iran's counter-offer on the Strait of Hormuz "totally unacceptable" on Monday and said the ten-week-old ceasefire was "on life support". Tehran's terms — sovereignty over the strait, sanctions lifting, frozen-asset release and war-damage compensation — were rejected verbatim. Brent crude rose 2.36 percent to 103.68 dollars a barrel; WTI 2.18 percent to 97.50. A single Qatari LNG tanker crossed the strait on Sunday — the first transit since the war began — and remains the only thread cutting the other way. The cruise leg of the oil cascade priced the news: cruise names fell 4 to 5 percent on the jet-fuel-cost reset. The "love tap" framing is dead.

### Risk sits in vol, not yields.

The S&P closed up 0.24 percent at a fresh all-time high. Underneath, the equity-volatility index (VIX) rose 5.06 percent — a third consecutive green-tape session with vol up — while the bond-volatility gauge (MOVE) crushed 6.91 percent to a multi-month low. Options dealers are paying to hedge into the highs; the rates desk is paying for the calendar. April CPI lands Tuesday morning. The full-Senate vote on Kevin Warsh, the Federal Reserve chair-designate, falls this week; Jerome Powell, the sitting chair, ends his term on Friday. The first IEEPA-tariff refunds began flowing through US Customs and Border Protection on or about Monday. Stocks held the high; the hedge is in the wing.
