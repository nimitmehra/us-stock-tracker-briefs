## What Happened

### Amazon's Supply Chain Services launch reshapes the logistics landscape

Amazon (AMZN), the United States' largest e-commerce platform, launched Amazon Supply Chain Services (ASCS) on Monday, opening its warehousing, freight, customs, last-mile and parcel network to enterprise customers — with Procter & Gamble, 3M, Lands' End and American Eagle Outfitters named as inaugural clients. FedEx (FDX), the parcel-delivery group whose largest single historical customer is Amazon, closed -9.11% — the largest negative alpha in our Tier 1 cohort and the worst possible competitive configuration: yesterday's customer is today's competitor. The cohort confirmed the read: United Parcel Service (UPS) -10.47%, C.H. Robinson (CHRW) -9.06%, Old Dominion (ODFL) -6.62%, Expeditors (EXPD) -5.11%. FedEx's June-1 listing of Freight as FDXF — long the value-unlock thesis — now operates inside an Amazon-disruption overhang the prospectus did not contemplate. Watch the next three trading days for an FDX Reg FD response statement; absent one, the spinoff prices into a structurally-disrupted cohort. [CONFIRMED — Amazon press release; Bloomberg, 4 May]

### Bond market repriced to a high-rates regime as Hormuz turned kinetic

The Strait of Hormuz saw its first kinetic exchange under Project Freedom on Monday, with US Navy forces sinking six Iranian small boats and the United Arab Emirates intercepting Iranian missiles for the first time since the 8 April ceasefire. Brent crude spiked +5.8% intraday to $114.44 before fading. The bond market did not fade. The ten-year Treasury yield closed at 4.446%, +6.7 basis points and a fresh 52-week high; the thirty-year at 5.025% and the five-year at 4.093% likewise broke to 52-week highs; the long-bond ETF (TLT) set a 52-week low; the MOVE index of bond volatility rose +10.58% to 77.86 even as the VIX fell -3.23%. Layered onto the geopolitics is the institutional input the equity wires keep under-selling: Kevin Warsh's full-Senate confirmation vote falls in the week of 11 May after a 13-11 party-line committee advance — the first fully partisan Fed-chair committee vote in history. The pre-vote tape is the asymmetric tail-risk window. [CONFIRMED — CENTCOM; Senate Banking Committee record; CNBC; Bloomberg]

### Micron prints another high as the Memflation cascade matures

Micron Technology (MU), the largest American memory-chip manufacturer, closed Monday at $576.45, +6.31% and a fresh all-time high — the largest single-name alpha in our 611-ticker universe and the sixth consecutive session above the company's $500 trim line. The catalysts compounded: Bernstein lifted its price target on Sandisk (SNDK) to $1,700 from $1,250 (+36%), Citigroup and Wedbush followed, and Tim Cook of Apple invoked a "prolonged memory crisis" alongside Microsoft chief financial officer Amy Hood's $25bn-of-$190bn-2026-capex memory-component disclosure and Andy Jassy of Amazon's "skyrocketing" framing. Sandisk closed +5.80% to a fresh all-time high, corroborating the AI-buildout's willingness-to-pay through the storage stack. Best Buy (BBY) is the cleanest counter-print: -4.01% to a fresh 52-week low as memory-cost pass-through compresses consumer-electronics margins. The Memflation cascade is now graph-mature — MU bull, BBY bear, AMD -5.27% and Qualcomm (QCOM) -4.88% on the same memory-cost mechanism — and is a candidate for organic macro-node creation. Advanced Micro Devices reports tonight after-market and is the load-bearing read-across for tomorrow's tape. [CONFIRMED — Micron, Sandisk, sell-side notes]

### Norwegian's guide cut and Spirit's bankruptcy expose the Hormuz cost

Norwegian Cruise Line (NCLH), one of America's three large cruise operators, cut its 2026 adjusted earnings-per-share guide on Monday to $1.45–1.79 from $2.38 — a 30-39% midpoint reduction — citing Iran-war-driven jet and marine fuel costs and tepid European demand. Carnival Corporation (CCL), which carries the cohort's highest fuel sensitivity and no hedging, fell -3.71%; Royal Caribbean (RCL), best-positioned with roughly half of FY26 fuel hedged at lower prices, fell only -2.29%. The cruise tape is now reading Hormuz as a structural input to FY26 economics, not a transient headline. The capacity-destruction wave is broader: Spirit Airlines filed for liquidation on 2 May citing fuel costs as the final straw — the first US airline directly attributable to the Iran-war energy spike — while Turkish Airlines suspended 18 international routes and Lufthansa, Ryanair, British Airways and ANA cut schedules. Jet fuel is reportedly trading near a $200/barrel-equivalent. The medium-term read for Delta Air Lines (DAL) is bullish on pricing if Delta survives the cycle; the near-term read for the three cruise majors is that the lowest-hedged operator carries the bear-case option. [CONFIRMED — NCLH guidance release; Time magazine; Reuters]
