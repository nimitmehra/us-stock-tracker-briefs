### Fed splits 8-4; structural oil regime deepens

The Federal Open Market Committee voted 8-4 on Tuesday to hold the funds rate at 3.50-3.75%, the most dissents since October 1992. Stephen Miran dissented for a 25 basis point cut; Beth Hammack, Neel Kashkari and Lorie Logan dissented against the inclusion of an easing-bias statement — a fragmented committee against both directions simultaneously. Jerome Powell, chair since 2018, announced at his "last press conference as chair" that he would remain on the Board of Governors after his chair term expires, citing what he called "unprecedented" legal attacks by the Trump administration. It is the first time a Fed chair has stayed on as governor since 1948. The Senate Banking Committee separately advanced Kevin Warsh as the next chair on a party-line vote. The 5-year Treasury yield rose roughly 8 basis points to 4.065% as the curve repriced no-easing-path; the 30-year touched a 52-week high of 4.987%. Concurrently, Brent crude printed $118.03 on Tuesday close — its highest since June 2022 — as the United Arab Emirates exited OPEC and Strait of Hormuz traffic remained at roughly 5% of pre-war levels into day 62 of the dual blockade. For Carnival Corporation (CCL), the largest US cruise operator and the only major cruise line that does not hedge fuel, this oil-regime persistence drove the largest Tier 1 underperformance on the day: -2.74% to $25.58 on -2.59 percentage points of alpha versus the consumer-discretionary ETF. Management has been silent through three consecutive weeks of Brent over $110; the silence is itself the data point.
