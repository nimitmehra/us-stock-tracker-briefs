## What Happened

### The Fed scare reversed in one session.

Stocks erased yesterday's hawkish-Fed de-rate on Thursday. The S&P 500 rose 1.08 percent to 7,500.58, the Nasdaq 1.91 percent, and the Russell 2000 small-cap index led at 2.12 percent — broad, not a narrow mega-cap bounce. The stock-market volatility gauge (VIX) collapsed 11.06 percent to 16.40, fully unwinding Wednesday's event-day spike. Two catalysts overrode the rate story: the US-Iran framework signed a day early, sending oil down three percent, and a reported Apple-Intel US chip deal that lit the semiconductors. The bond market helped — the long bond rallied seven basis points and the front-end kept fading the rate committee's hike-bias projections. The market is not buying the dots.

### An AI-infrastructure melt-up ran on three legs.

The day's real engine was an AI-buildout rip across three groups. Chips led: Intel (INTC) rose 10.64 percent to a record. The catalyst was a Truth Social post by President Donald Trump saying Apple (AAPL) would design chips with Intel in America. Neither company confirmed it, so it is a report, not a closed deal. The semiconductor ETF (SMH) rose 5.76 percent, broadening into the analog names. Optical followed: Corning (GLW) rose 11.13 percent on an Amazon (AMZN) multibillion-dollar fibre deal for its AI data centres. Power completed it: Bloom Energy (BE) rose 15.41 percent to a record, with GE Vernova and Vertiv alongside. Micron (MU) added 8.70 percent. What survived Wednesday's selloff is now leading it.

### Moderna's vaccine cleared its vote — and barely moved.

Moderna (MRNA), the messenger-RNA vaccine maker, won its advisory-committee vote unanimously on Thursday. The Food and Drug Administration's vaccines panel voted nine to nil that mFlusiva, its seasonal-flu shot, benefits adults 50 to 64. It voted nine to nil again for those 65 and over — the contested older cohort that sent the application back in February. It is the FDA's first vaccine recommendation since 2023, clearing a path to the first US messenger-RNA flu vaccine ahead of the 5 August decision. Yet the shares rose only 3.50 percent to a record. The whole move front-ran into Tuesday's nine-percent briefing-document pop. The book's only near-term binary resolved at the bullish tail — and the tape had already spent it.

- **How it matters:** The debasement bid is more risk-appetite-sensitive than it looked; the gold-miner cohort the graph watches is not the rate-insulated trade Wednesday suggested, and defence weakness is a de-rate, not a fundamentals break.
