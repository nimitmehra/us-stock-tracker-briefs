## What Happened

### A weekend war scare the tape is set to fade

Iran fired missiles at Israel on Sunday — its first bombardment since April's ceasefire — and Israel struck military targets in western and central Iran early Monday, the trigger an Israeli strike on Beirut's southern suburbs against an American request to stand down. By Monday Iran's foreign ministry told CNBC it had ceased fire, while warning it would resume if Israel kept up operations in Lebanon. Crude jumped more than 4 percent toward $97 a barrel, then handed most of it back to near $94 as the walk-back landed; Washington and Tehran are reported to be negotiating a memorandum to reopen the Strait of Hormuz. The escalation reverses last week's sixty-day-ceasefire framing, yet the tape is treating it as a contained flare. The single largest reversal risk sits in the hours before the session opens.

### Four-dollar gasoline is rewiring where America shops

The fuel shock from the Gulf scare is hardening a trade-down that retail executives now describe as structural rather than cyclical. Walmart, McDonald's and Dollar General all flagged over the weekend that better-off shoppers are crossing into deep-value formats: Dollar General's chief executive, Todd Vasos, named $4 gasoline as the tipping point pulling households earning over $100,000 into his stores, while Walmart said members were buying under ten gallons a trip for the first time since 2022. The graph read it as a two-sided coin, strengthening the discount-retail names and routing the same wallet share away from the broader discretionary cohort. A fuel price that empties one cart fills another.

## Portfolio Pulse

**Apple (AAPL).** Its developer conference opens today at 1pm Eastern, after this pipeline ran, with previews pointing to a Gemini-powered "Siri 2.0" and what is reportedly one of Tim Cook's final major keynotes as chief executive. A credible, shipping AI story closes the "Apple is behind" discount; a refinement-only event reopens it.

**Alphabet (GOOGL).** Filed a routine exchange-listing certification — the mechanical tail of last week's record $84.75 billion equity raise — and its raised $0.22 dividend has its record date today. The raise itself is the cleanest example of the market's split between mega-caps still paying for AI and those already earning from it; Alphabet tapped equity, not cash, with Berkshire Hathaway's $10 billion placement a partial offset.

**Amazon (AMZN).** Filed a preliminary Canadian-dollar bond offering — non-dilutive treasury funding, with priced terms due within days. Separately, Pinterest signed a roughly $4 billion cloud agreement through 2031 and Prime Day is set for June 23–26. All modestly thesis-supportive; none of it a fresh catalyst.

**Micron (MU).** Began volume shipments of its HBM4 memory for NVIDIA's next-generation Vera Rubin platform — shipping at scale ahead of Samsung, which directly supports the pricing-power thesis. The Q3 print on June 24 is the next hard test of its $33.5 billion revenue and 81 percent margin guide.

**Cloudflare (NET).** Holds its Investor Day tomorrow at the New York Stock Exchange, with Barclays reiterating Overweight and a $250 target into it. The test is whether management can frame the lower-margin mix of newer AI-inference products as investment rather than erosion of the 75–77 percent gross-margin target. Our own research rates it HOLD, fully valued near $273.

**NVIDIA (NVDA).** Jensen Huang, the co-founder and chief executive, faces a Monday deadline to confirm whether he will testify at Thursday's Senate Banking hearing on whether China-bound chips are reaching the military, which the company denies. How he handles it decides whether China-policy risk becomes a structural discount or settles as background noise.

**Microsoft (MSFT).** Reid Hoffman, the LinkedIn co-founder who has sat on the board for nearly a decade, is departing — governance noise rather than a thesis change. The more relevant weekend datapoint was an in-house AI coding model shown at its Build conference, which trims its dependence on OpenAI.
