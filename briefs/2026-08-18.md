## Rates & Macro

The ten-year Treasury yield, the rate that prices long-dated stocks, closed Tuesday at **4.71 percent, down one basis point** from Monday's 4.72. The inflation-stripped ten-year — the number that marks down far-future artificial-intelligence profits — fell **three basis points to 2.41 percent**. The two-year, the market's read on the Federal Reserve, was **unchanged at 4.19 percent**, where it has sat since Monday. So the whole of Tuesday's move, and more, came out of the real rate rather than out of inflation expectations.

On the arithmetic, Tuesday was a mild **tailwind** for the artificial-intelligence book. Real yields fell; far-future profit got fractionally more valuable. The artificial-intelligence complex fell four to seven percent anyway. That gap is the day's story and it is told below.

The exit line is unchanged and restated every day: the inflation-stripped ten-year holding above roughly 2.25 to 2.30 percent, or the two-year pushing toward 4.4 to 4.5. Today sits **above the first at 2.41**, where it has now sat for over a week, and **21 to 31 basis points below the second**. Treat both as a calibration, not a promise. **Note what the line does not watch: the thirty-year.** It closed Monday at **5.31 percent, a nineteen-year high**, then fell three basis points on Tuesday to 5.28.

## What Happened

### The crowded trade unwound itself

Monday's market fell while its most crowded corner went vertical. The S&P 500 lost 0.52 percent on 132 risers and 366 fallers — yet Sandisk (SNDK) rose 8.88 percent, Micron Technology (MU) 4.13 and Seagate Technology (STX) 2.19. On Tuesday each one gave it back and more: Sandisk −9.01 percent, Micron −7.02, Seagate −9.16, Bloom Energy (BE) −9.97. Across 574 stocks with a full record, Monday's return and Tuesday's correlate at **−0.460**. The artificial-intelligence hardware group went 24 up out of 28 on Monday and **28 down out of 28** on Tuesday. Nothing outside equities moved — no currency pair cleared one percent, no yield ten basis points, credit sat still. Positions were unwound. Nothing was repriced.

### Nvidia guaranteed someone else's rent

NVIDIA (NVDA) told the Securities and Exchange Commission during Monday's session that it has written guarantees on data-centre leases **"cumulatively capped at $105 billion."** The lessor is SB Energy, the site 4.25 gigawatts at Pike County, Ohio, the tenant an affiliate of OpenAI, with 3.8 gigawatts more at Nvidia's option. Two features carry the weight. The only protection against an OpenAI default is an indemnity **from OpenAI** — circular by construction. And the obligation ends when OpenAI "achiev[es] a satisfactory credit rating", a condition with neither definition nor date. The agreements are not on file; they arrive with the quarterly report. The next day the Wall Street Journal counted about $3 trillion of such commitments across nine technology firms. No outlet joined them. They are the same instrument.

### The chips did not fall on the long bond

Every wire named the thirty-year Treasury yield as the cause of Tuesday's chip selloff. Our own numbers refuse it. The thirty-year **fell three basis points on Tuesday** to 5.28 percent, from Monday's nineteen-year high of 5.31; the long-Treasury fund (TLT) rose 0.38 percent; semiconductors fell 4.09. On Monday, the day the thirty-year set that high, semiconductors **rose 1.06 percent**. Across the pair the rate and the chips move opposite ways. A second test fails the same way: utilities, the most rate-sensitive group on the board, moved −0.36 percent on Tuesday while energy rose 1.76. That is a rotation, not a repricing of duration. The record intraday print of 5.33 percent was real, and it faded by the close.

### Micron's buy level was never live

Micron closed Tuesday at $940.76, inside the $850–950 band our own record tells us to buy. **It is not a buy, and Thursday 13 August's brief was wrong about why.** That brief called the gap a leak between two systems. It was not. The band comes from a refresh dated **25 June**. The deep research of **29 June, four days later**, set PASS at conviction four and a single add level of **$700** — one rung, not a range. The older instruction was superseded by the research that superseded the verdict behind it, and nobody marked it. It is marked now. What stands open is worse: a PASS at four out of ten on a stock we own.

### Lumentum filed a footnote it never wrote

Lumentum Holdings (LITE), the optical-components maker, filed its annual report on Monday. Note 19, Subsequent Events, reads in full: **"TBD."** Not the standard line that management looked and found nothing — the placeholder. It was checked against the 3.3-megabyte original document, where the word appears exactly once. It sits beneath the signed certifications in which the chief executive and chief financial officer attest that no material fact is omitted. The same filing carries revenue up 83.2 percent, operating income of $524.8 million against a prior $180.1 million loss, and a **$6,935.1 million net loss** driven by a non-cash $7,756.6 million charge for retiring debt. A subsequent-events note is where a refinancing that size gets disclosed. It is the one note left blank.

## Cascade Watch

### Our exit line watches the wrong tenor

- **What happened:** The thirty-year Treasury yield set a nineteen-year closing high of 5.31 percent on Monday, the wires called it the cause of everything that followed, and the words "thirty-year" appear **zero times** in our 14 August brief.
- **Why it happened:** Our exit calibration was built on the inflation-stripped ten-year and the two-year, because those are the instruments that de-rated the artificial-intelligence trade on 17 June; the two-year went 4.17 to 4.19 to 4.19 across these two sessions, flat and 21 basis points from its trigger.
- **How it matters:** Whether or not the wires' causal claim survives — and it does not — the long end can now make a nineteen-year high without our own instruments registering anything, so the calibration needs a long-end leg before the next one.

### The defence null was not a null

- **What happened:** Our news desk published the defence sector as silent on Monday, naming ten primes; two of them filed that day, and one of the filings removed a chief executive.
- **Why it happened:** L3Harris (LHX) disclosed that Christopher Kubasik, chairman and chief executive, left immediately after the independent directors found conduct inconsistent with the company's code — **with no severance and no acceleration of unvested equity**, which is a for-cause outcome in mutual-agreement language. *(The chairman and chief-executive roles were split at the same time, and Motorola Solutions priced $950 million of notes that day.)*
- **How it matters:** L3Harris fell 4.61 percent on Monday and our market desk left it unexplained; the cause was in a filing all along. A null result is only as good as the sweep behind it, and this one should not have been published.

### Insider selling was half what it looked like

- **What happened:** Across 177 insider filings over the two sessions, tax withholding on vesting shares came to **$420.2 million** and genuine open-market selling to **$411.3 million** — so the blended figure overstates selling by roughly double.
- **Why it happened:** Our filings desk caught its own method mid-run: a text search for the plan reference mis-scored one filing that spells it wrongly, and summing all disposals conflates shares sold with shares withheld by the issuer.
- **How it matters:** Three conclusions reverse. Bloom Energy insiders were **not** dumping into an unexplained spike; Lumentum's selling is $4.1 million, not $56.5 million; Cloudflare's is zero. What survives is that **Chevron's chief executive sold $63.6 million outside any plan**, and that across all 177 filings there was **no insider buying at all**.

### Energy re-coupled to oil while the metals liquidated

- **What happened:** The energy fund made a fresh 52-week high on Tuesday and rose in both sessions as crude gained 3.08 percent, while the entire metals complex fell on Tuesday after rising on Monday — silver −3.30 percent, palladium −3.22, platinum −3.09, copper −1.84, aluminium −2.85.
- **Why it happened:** The energy leg has one dated cause — the United States–Iran understanding expired Monday with no successor. The metals leg has none: **dollar strength is ruled out** (the dollar was flat both sessions) and a margin increase is ruled out (the search results describing one are from December 2025).
- **How it matters:** Energy is the one place a confirmed catalyst met a confirmed price move across both sessions; the metals move should be carried as unexplained rather than folded into the risk-off story it superficially fits.

### Fifteen of our seventeen top names have no links at all

- **What happened:** The link audit found that **fifteen of seventeen Tier 1 records carry zero connections**; only Micron has any number of them (three) and Axon one.
- **Why it happened:** The connections were never authored — every link must have its mechanism verifiable in a specific research report, and that work has not been done for the Tier 1 cohort.
- **How it matters:** Every chain of effects this run had to route through the macro records instead. When this brief says no link fired on a holding, that means **there was no link to fire** — not that there was no signal.
