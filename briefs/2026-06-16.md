## What Happened

### Moderna's flu binary de-risked before the vote.

Moderna (MRNA), the messenger-RNA vaccine maker, rose 6.27 percent to $55.40 on Tuesday — a clean move against a flat healthcare sector, the second up-day running. The Food and Drug Administration released its staff review of mFlusiva, Moderna's seasonal-flu shot, ahead of the 18 June advisory-committee vote. It found no major deficiencies: the shot met every pre-set efficacy bar and beat the standard-dose comparator in adults aged 50 to 64. This is the only near-term binary across the whole book, and it just shifted from coin-flip to favourably skewed. The committee can still re-open the older-adult comparator question on Thursday, so the event stays live, not won. Favourable staff documents reduce binary risk; they have never erased it.

### Monday's risk-on rotated rather than ran.

The rally split in two. The Dow Jones Industrial Average closed at a record 51,999.67, up 0.64 percent, while the Nasdaq fell 1.15 percent and the S&P 500 slipped 0.57 percent to 7,511.35. The move was a turn out of long-duration growth and into value before the Federal Reserve's rate decision. The catalyst did not break: West Texas Intermediate crude fell 6.87 percent to $75.20, a fourth straight down session on the Strait-of-Hormuz reopening. So the disinflation tailwind strengthened, even as the equity expression flipped. The AI-memory melt-up that hit records on Monday unwound on profit-taking, and the freed money rotated into banks and industrials — financials rose 1.47 percent. Bond volatility fell and the Dow set a record: a breather, not a flight.

### Storage split from memory inside the same supercycle.

The day's sharpest signal was the crack inside the AI-storage trade. Seagate Technology Holdings (STX), the largest US maker of hard-disk drives, rose 1.23 percent to a fresh all-time high of $1,031.34 even as the semiconductor index fell 4.81 percent. That six-point outperformance was the best in the book. It rode a read-through from Western Digital's quarterly beat, its closest peer, which reported revenue up 45 percent and committed drive capacity through 2028-29. The memory makers gave it all back: Micron Technology (MU) fell 6.18 percent and Sandisk (SNDK) 5.52 percent, both pure sector beta, both off Monday's records. The tape is starting to pay demonstrated fundamentals over momentum — hard drives held, flash memory did not.

## Cascade Watch

### Storage decoupled from memory inside the AI supercycle

- **What happened:** Seagate set a fresh record (+1.23 percent) and Western Digital beat (+4.22 percent) while Micron (−6.18 percent) and Sandisk (−5.52 percent) gave back Monday's melt-up, all on the same −4.81 percent chip-index day.
- **Why it happened:** Western Digital's quarterly beat — drive capacity committed through 2028-29 — paid the hard-disk names on demonstrated fundamentals, while the flash-and-DRAM names fell as pure profit-taking beta with no fresh catalyst.
- **How it matters:** The supercycle is bifurcating; the held storage thesis strengthens on the hard-drive side, while the memory trims stay overdue on names that just ran and reversed.

### Oil's fourth down session fired the energy-and-travel chain again

- **What happened:** The reopening of the Strait of Hormuz pushed crude down 6.87 percent for a fourth straight session, refreshing nine links from oil prices out to producers (ExxonMobil, Chevron, ConocoPhillips, Diamondback, Petrobras, Cheniere) and fuel-heavy consumers (Carnival, Delta, FedEx).
- **Why it happened:** A sustained crude drop cuts producer revenue and cuts cruise, airline and freight fuel bills at the same time, so one catalyst pushes the two cohorts in opposite directions.
- **How it matters:** No portfolio holding sits on an oil link, so this is a read on where cheaper-fuel money is flowing, not a position alert — and the disinflation it implies feeds tomorrow's Fed calculus.

- **Why it happened:** The memory pullbacks shortened distances but crossed no fresh line, and Meta's $616 starter and $560 add levels both held without a touch.
