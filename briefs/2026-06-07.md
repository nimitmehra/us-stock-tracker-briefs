## What Happened

### OPEC+ added barrels it cannot ship.

Eight producers in the OPEC+ alliance — Saudi Arabia and Russia taking the largest shares — agreed in a Sunday video meeting to raise their July output ceiling by 188,000 barrels a day, the fourth such increase since the Strait of Hormuz closed three months ago and the same step as June. It is a paper increase, not a physical one: with the strait still a ghost route — about seven tanker transits on Friday against a hundred a day normally — the extra Gulf quota cannot reach the market. One delegate-side analyst called it "more a policy signal than a real supply boost." Crude closed Friday near $91 a barrel, down about 3 percent on the day but still higher on the week on the same Gulf-risk premium the quota does nothing to relieve. The market is short of barrels, not announcements.

### The weekend held the line; Wednesday's inflation print is the gate.

Two days after the worst equity session of 2026, the weekend produced nothing to break Friday's fall or confirm it — no fresh data, only the symbolic oil quota and a steady drip of regulatory pressure on the mega-caps. Friday's damage came from a hot jobs report fused onto Broadcom's flat 2027 spending guide, a rate shock and an AI-spending scare arriving together. Whether that was a single bad day or the start of a regime now rests on the May consumer-price report due Wednesday, the cleanest read on whether the rate scare broadens. The wires are calling it a chip rout; the bond market is calling it a repricing. Wednesday tells which one is right.
