## What Happened

### Hormuz turned kinetic. The oil bid came back.

The US and Iran traded strikes over the Strait of Hormuz overnight — the first two-way exchange since the 8 April ceasefire. After an Iranian drone downed a US Army Apache near the strait on Monday, US Central Command struck Iranian air-defense and radar sites after Tuesday's close. Iran counter-struck US bases in Bahrain, Jordan and Kuwait; host-nation defenses reported interceptions. The strait sits effectively closed under a dual blockade. Crude reversed up — West Texas Intermediate plus 1.4 percent to $89.44, Brent plus 1.1 percent — undoing Tuesday's "deal-in-days" sell-off. The two-sided tail flagged on Tuesday resolved to the escalation side, and equity futures opened offered into it. Yesterday's hope is today's risk-off.

### The inflation print is the gate. It is not out yet.

May consumer prices land at 8:30 ET this morning, and Tuesday was explicit de-risking into the number. Consensus is plus 4.2 percent on the year and plus 2.9 percent at the core — what would be the hottest headline since April 2023, with the Iran war premium named as the driver. The print is not released at this writing; one aggregator has already circulated the consensus as if it were the result, which it is not. Underneath sits the rates regime that set the week up. After Friday's strong jobs report, the market prices roughly a 70 percent chance the Federal Reserve hikes by year-end; the ten-year Treasury yield has pushed toward 4.54 percent on the wires. A hot number validates the year-end-hike bet; an in-line one is the circuit-breaker.

### Micron's memory got the carve-out. The chip tape did not.

NVIDIA Corporation (NVDA) confirmed on Tuesday that all three of its high-bandwidth-memory suppliers are qualified and shipping for its next-generation Vera Rubin platform. One of the three is Micron Technology (MU), the largest American memory maker; the platform is now in full production, with deliveries due in the third quarter. That is direct demand validation for the memory thesis and an explicit carve-out from the spending worry Broadcom's miss seeded last week. The worry is real elsewhere: a global chip selloff ran overnight — Samsung, SK Hynix, Tokyo Electron and Advantest all sharply lower — on fear that hyperscaler AI spending is moderating. The supply side cut the other way, with Taiwan Semiconductor's chief executive, C.C. Wei, saying chip supply will fall short of AI needs for years. Micron softened pre-market with the complex into its 24 June print — cohort de-risking, not a thesis crack. The de-rate is surgical, not structural.
