## What Happened

### Rates repriced; Tuesday's rotation reversed.

A single repricing organized Wednesday. Iran's overnight missile-and-drone strikes on Kuwait and Bahrain killed one at Kuwait International Airport. Crude bid a third straight day — West Texas Intermediate $96.20, up 2.6 percent — on a sixth consecutive weekly US inventory draw. Stronger jobs data and a hot services-inflation print did the rest: the Federal Reserve's 17 June meeting now prices as a near-certain hold with a hike bias forming. The long end answered — the ten-year Treasury closed at 4.491 percent. Tuesday's risk-on winners reversed cleanly: uranium, copper, rare earths and regional banks, all duration bets, gave back the lot. Gold sold with stocks, not against them. That is the tell — this was a rate day, not a fear day.

### SanDisk's legacy-exit line fired.

SanDisk (SNDK), the NAND-flash maker spun out of Western Digital, closed at $1,831.50, up 6.71 percent and at a fresh all-time high — the largest sector-relative move in the portfolio. The close carried it above the $1,800 level the system had set to begin trimming the small legacy position into a melt-up; that line is now live, and the decision sits with the principal. Two catalysts drove it: Melius Research raised long-term estimates on memory's sold-out-through-2026 supply, and David Tepper's Appaloosa opened a new SanDisk position last quarter — its only new buy. Memory was the one corner of the AI trade the rate shock could not touch.

### Broadcom's after-close blowout reset the cohort.

Broadcom (AVGO), the custom-AI-chip and networking supplier, reported after Wednesday's close and beat on every line. Second-quarter revenue rose 48 percent to $22.2 billion; the AI-chip line grew 143 percent to $10.8 billion. The guidance was the shock: $29.4 billion for the current quarter, with AI-chip revenue guided to $16 billion — up more than 200 percent — and the 2027 AI target reaffirmed above $100 billion. Chief executive Hock Tan named six custom-silicon customers, Anthropic, Google, Meta and OpenAI among them. The daytime tape had sold the buildout cohort all session on the rate shock. The print that landed after the bell answered the only question that mattered — the buildout pays.

### Moderna re-rated on cancer-vaccine data.

Moderna (MRNA) closed up 7.49 percent at $49.06 — the portfolio's best raw move of the day — on five-year survival data for its personalised melanoma vaccine, run with Merck's Keytruda, showing durable long-term benefit. A second item helped: up to $50 million from the Coalition for Epidemic Preparedness Innovations to advance its Ebola vaccine. The move ran against a falling tape and inside the day's one bid sub-sector, biotechnology. A separate catalyst still sits on the calendar — a Food and Drug Administration advisory hearing on the flu vaccine on 18 June. The cancer-vaccine readout is the re-rate; the regulatory hearing is the next test.

### Microsoft's regulatory week landed with an insider sell.

Microsoft (MSFT) fell 3.17 percent to $427.34, underperforming even a weak technology tape, as the regulatory and capital-spending anchor of mega-cap tech. The company was reported to face two fronts: a new executive order requiring artificial-intelligence firms to submit models for federal review, and a Federal Trade Commission probe into its bundling of cloud, AI and software. Beneath the tape, an insider sale opened. Judson Althoff, the commercial-business chief executive who runs Microsoft's enterprise sales, sold $7.15 million of stock on 1 June with no pre-planned-sale cover. It was the first discretionary sale of size by a top executive in the recent window, and it lands into the stock's worst stretch of the week.
