## What Happened

### Project Freedom escort begins in the Strait of Hormuz

The United States Navy's "Project Freedom" escort operation, intended to police the Strait of Hormuz against Iranian harassment of commercial shipping, began on Monday morning with a Central Command force comprising more than 100 land- and sea-based aircraft, 15,000 service members and a flotilla of guided-missile destroyers. Iran's parliamentary National Security Commission, chaired by Ebrahim Azizi, has called the operation a violation of the 8 April ceasefire; Tehran is still charging tolls north of $1m per non-American transit and, under Supreme Leader Ali Khamenei, refusing nuclear concessions. Brent crude marked +1.30% on the Monday tape and US natural gas closed +11% on the week, both reading as early Project Freedom pricing. Carnival Corporation (CCL) carries the only formally elevated Hormuz catalyst trigger in the Tier 1 cohort; ConocoPhillips (COP) is the only American major to have already taken Qatar volumes out of its second-quarter production guide. The asymmetric tail to watch over the next forty-eight hours is a ship-to-ship engagement that puts Brent through $130 — the operation's "harasses but does not engage" middle scenario is the consensus base case. [CONFIRMED — CENTCOM/White House readouts, 3 May; CNBC, NPR, CBS, Axios, FT, Bloomberg]

### Four FOMC dissents — the highest since 1992 — and Powell exits the chair

The Federal Open Market Committee, the Federal Reserve's rate-setting body, kept the funds target at 3.50–3.75% on 29 April by an 8–4 vote, the largest split since 1992. Stephen Miran, a governor since March 2025, dissented in favour of a 25 basis-point cut; Beth Hammack of Cleveland, Neel Kashkari of Minneapolis and Lorie Logan of Dallas supported the hold but dissented against retaining the easing bias. The committee also dropped the word "somewhat" from its inflation language — a single-word edit that is engineered, not accidental. Jerome Powell, chair of the Federal Reserve since 2018, said on the same day that he will step down as chair effective 15 May while remaining a Governor through 2028, citing "no choice" in the face of Donald Trump's legal pressure on the institution. The bond market read it on Friday: the ten-year yield fell 1.2 basis points despite a +0.29% S&P 500 print at a fresh all-time high — the second consecutive session of rates-down-on-equities-up. The next-chair nomination, expected this week, is the catalyst to watch. [CONFIRMED — Federal Reserve press release, 29 April; CNN Business; CNBC]

### Micron rips to a fresh high on hyperscaler corroboration

Micron Technology (MU), the largest American manufacturer of memory chips, closed Friday at $542.21, +4.84% — a fresh all-time high and a fifth consecutive session above the company's $500 trim line. The catalyst was a Buy initiation from D. A. Davidson with a $1,000 price target — Street-high and roughly 85% above the close — but the load-bearing corroboration came from outside the sell-side. Microsoft's chief financial officer Amy Hood disclosed on her company's earnings call that $25bn of its $190bn 2026 capital-expenditure plan is being driven by component pricing rather than capacity addition; Andy Jassy, Amazon's chief executive, described memory cost as "skyrocketing" and capacity as "not enough."
