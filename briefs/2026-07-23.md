## Rates & Macro

The ten-year Treasury — the rate that prices long-dated stocks — closed at 4.71%, up four basis points and a fresh 52-week high; the five-year set its own high at 4.46%. The inflation-stripped ten-year rose four points to 2.43%, an eighth straight session above the danger zone and the deepest reading of the move. The two-year, the market's read on the Fed, added six points to 4.37%.

Today was a clean headwind for the AI book, and it was real-yield-led: the ten-year breakeven — the market's inflation bet — held at 2.28%, so the whole move marks down far-future profit rather than repricing inflation. Worse, it came on a red equity day. Stocks and bonds fell together, which is the funding-stress signature, not a growth scare.

The exit line is unchanged: the inflation-stripped ten-year holding above roughly 2.25–2.30 percent, or the two-year pushing toward 4.4–4.5 percent on hawkish Fed repricing. Today sits beyond the first marker and closing on the second. Next Wednesday's Fed decision is now a near coin flip — roughly 46.5 percent odds of a quarter-point hike, up from about 34 percent at the week's start — pulled forward by oil. The European Central Bank held at 2.25 percent but said some governors now ask whether a further hike is warranted. Oil is doing the tightening before the Fed does.

## What Happened

### The AI bill went tape-wide. Bonds priced it.

Alphabet (GOOGL), a portfolio holding, fell 7.1 percent on a record quarter — punished not for demand but for the bill. The full-year capital-spending guide rose to $195–205bn and quarterly free cash flow printed negative at −$5.9bn; Thursday's quarterly filing showed first-half capital spending doubled, with $22.1bn moved into private AI stakes. Tesla (TSLA), reporting the same night, fell 14.5 percent for the same sin: capex up 142 percent, cash flow negative. The second message was in bonds — five- and ten-year yields closed at 52-week highs on a risk-off equity day. Stocks and bonds fell together: the market is pricing the buildout as a funding problem, not a growth scare. Money moved from the names paying for the buildout to the names being paid.

### Meta crossed its buy line. The stress test says wait.

Meta Platforms (META), a portfolio holding, closed at $606.10 — 1.6 percent below the $616 level where the system's May research said to start buying — six days before its 29 July report. The collision: this week's eight-persona stress test cut Meta from buy to hold, conviction five, on precisely the mechanism now moving the price — AI capital spending eating free cash flow. Its standing instruction: hold, do not add above $560, wait for the print. Wednesday resolves both sides — management's promised $125–145bn full-year capex is the number the market will price, made heavier by Alphabet's capex doubling. The discipline, not the trigger, governs into the print.

### Intel delivered the quarter the AI trade needed

Intel (INTC), a watch-list name, reported after Thursday's close: revenue up 25 percent to $16.1bn — its fastest growth since 2011 — gross margin up nearly 13 points, a raised third-quarter guide, and the first external cloud-provider order for its 18A process, whose yields improved from 65 to 85 percent. Shares rose 13 percent in late trading, per CNBC — a Friday setup, since the print landed after the close. One reading note: the headline GAAP loss of $2.16 a share is a $12.5bn non-cash mark on US-government-held shares, a liability that grows as the stock rises; the operating line is the story. Money still pays for delivered earnings — it is promised capex it now charges for.

### The war reached equities. Oil reached the Fed.

Brent closed above $100 for the first time since May after Houthi attacks on two Saudi tankers spread the US–Iran war to the Red Sea, stacking on the Caspian pipeline halt that has stopped roughly 80 percent of Kazakh crude exports. The futures market moved next week's Fed decision to a near coin flip. The same day, Washington announced 10–12.5 percent tariffs on imports from dozens of countries — covering roughly 99 percent of US imports — under the 1974 trade act's Section 301, replacing the expiring blanket 10 percent; outlet counts of the country list still diverge (60 versus 86), so the action is confirmed while the specifics are not. Oil raises the discount rate exactly when the AI complex most needs cheap capital.

## Graph Signal

The graph's read of the day is stratification, not selloff. Alphabet and Tesla — the payers — were punished; Micron and KLA — the receivers — were paid on the same red tape; and the two names in between, NVIDIA and Taiwan Semiconductor, got no credit at all for record customer budgets. That middle gap is the signal: the market now discounts hyperscaler capex into supplier revenue at less than one-for-one, which is new. The payer-versus-receiver frame has been accumulating in the graph since spring and today met every rule for formal tracking as its own macro theme — it is the week's organizing lens and is not yet a node.
