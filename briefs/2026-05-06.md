## What Happened

### Memory makers ran on Micron's print

Micron Technology (MU), the largest American memory-chip manufacturer, closed Tuesday at $640.20 — up 11.06 percent and at a fresh all-time high. After Monday's bell the company reported Q2 adjusted earnings of $12.20 per share against a $9.21 consensus, a 33 percent beat, on $23.9bn of revenue (twenty percent above consensus). Market capitalisation crossed seven hundred billion dollars. Sandisk (SNDK) followed at +11.98 percent on $42bn of multi-year contracts and a Susquehanna price-target double. Qualcomm (QCOM) closed +10.79 percent on Q2 net profit up 162 percent year-on-year; Intel (INTC) +12.92 percent on a single-source Bloomberg report of Apple foundry talks. The trim line at $500 trails the tape by twenty-eight percent. The rule has become the question.

### Oil collapsed. Defence did not rally.

President Trump on Tuesday afternoon paused the United States effort to guide stranded vessels out of the Strait of Hormuz, citing room for a deal. West Texas Intermediate fell -8.11 percent to $93.98 and Brent -7.20 percent to $101.96 — the cleanest oil-down-on-de-escalation tape since the war began. The defence cohort did not rally. Northrop Grumman (NOC) closed -1.48 percent against the iShares Aerospace & Defense ETF (ITA) up 0.49 percent. Huntington Ingalls Industries (HII), the Newport News naval contractor, fell -10.25 percent on a Q1 print: top-line beat, operating margin compressed from 5.9 to 5.0 percent, $461m of negative free cash flow. Three sessions of strong orders against weak tape is no longer a coincidence.

### Jazz's quarter cleared three promises

Jazz Pharmaceuticals (JAZZ), the rare-disease and oncology specialty firm whose Xywav narcolepsy franchise faces an authorised generic launched in January, reported Q1 2026 after Monday's bell. Revenue at $1.07bn beat consensus by nine percent on +19 percent year-on-year growth. Non-GAAP earnings of $6.34 per share against $4.62 consensus — a 37 percent beat. Xywav net product sales at $408m grew 18 percent — evidence the Amneal generic has yet to bite the franchise. The company reaffirmed its $4.25–4.50bn full-year revenue guide and its full-year adjusted EPS guide. The FDA accepted the zanidatamab supplemental BLA for HER2-positive first-line gastroesophageal cancer; PDUFA date 25 August. The stock closed at a fresh 52-week high of $212.26, up 2.02 percent. Trim sits at hand.
