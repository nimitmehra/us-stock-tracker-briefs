## Rates & Macro

The ten-year Treasury yield, the rate that prices long-dated stocks, closed at 4.70 percent, down five basis points. The inflation-stripped ten-year — the number that marks down far-future AI profits — fell four to 2.43 percent. The two-year, the market's read on the Fed, fell three to 4.25 percent. Friday's seven-basis-point sell-off is most of the way back.

This was a tailwind for the AI book, and the same clean kind Friday's headwind was. Inflation expectations barely moved — the ten-year breakeven sits at 2.27 percent — so the rally was real yield, which is the part that hits valuations. The exit line is unchanged: the inflation-stripped ten-year holding above roughly 2.25 to 2.30 percent, or the two-year pushing toward 4.4 to 4.5 percent. Today sits above the first and moved away from the second.

The composition is not dovish. July's factory survey printed 55.6 against 54.0 expected, the highest since May 2022. It carried the first month of factory hiring in thirty-three months and a prices index at 71.1 naming tariffs and the Iran war. The three-month bill rose eight basis points to 3.91 percent while everything from five to twenty years fell five. One summary put the market at roughly a 68 percent chance of a rate rise in September. The long end rallied because crude fell 4 to 6 percent, not because the Fed got easier. Friday's jobs report, not Monday's headline, decides whether it holds. The Treasury's refunding statement lands 05 August.

## What Happened

### A record close, and Tehran said no

The Dow Jones Industrial Average closed at a record 53,178.41 on Monday, up 1.32 percent, and Brent crude fell 6.03 percent. Both moved on one thing: the administration called off planned strikes on Iran to open talks. Iran's foreign ministry denied the premise the same day. Its spokesman, Esmaeil Baghaei, said Iran is negotiating with Oman alone, over a temporary route for shipping, and not with Washington. President Donald Trump then called the Iranian leadership "unbelievably duplicitous". The Strait of Hormuz has been impassable since 08 July, and nothing announced on Monday reopens it. A record high and a six percent oil break rest on a negotiation one side says does not exist.

### Cloudflare's best exit, and three days left

Cloudflare (NET), the internet-infrastructure company we hold, closed at $282.74 — within a quarter of one percent of its 52-week high. On 01 August our eight-persona stress test killed the thesis outright: a pass at conviction 8, on price, at 178 times forward earnings. The same review executed an instruction that had sat unexecuted for sixty days — sell half the position at market before Thursday's close. Cloudflare reports second-quarter results after that same Thursday close. The best price the position has ever offered and the event that could take it away fall on the same day. The window is three sessions wide.

### Amazon passed three trillion; Bezos filed to sell

Amazon.com (AMZN) closed at $284.02, up 4.58 percent and at a 52-week closing high, taking its market value through $3 trillion for the first time — the fifth company ever to do it. The same Monday, a planned-insider-sale notice disclosed that Jeff Bezos, founder and executive chairman, intends to sell 15 million shares through Morgan Stanley Smith Barney, about $4.07 billion of 1994 founder stock. That filing states no reportable sales in the prior three months; reporting has him selling roughly 25 million shares for about $5.7 billion between late June and late July. Two facts, one Monday, and only one of them led the wires.

### The market bought AI's customers, not its silicon

Semiconductors lagged on the best artificial-intelligence day in weeks. The semiconductor fund rose 0.91 percent against the S&P 500's 1.48, on a Nasdaq up 2.13 driven entirely by AI spending. Taiwan Semiconductor (TSM) added 0.46 percent, Broadcom (AVGO) 0.76, and KLA (KLAC) fell 0.04. Our own research rates two of those three a buy; Broadcom's latest report reads hold. Cloud software, data-centre power and optical components all ran hard; the companies that make the chips those buyers are buying did not move. This is the third session of the same split. It happened on the day the bull case for silicon got its strongest evidence yet, and we cannot explain it.

### Moderna decides Wednesday; the selling is already scheduled

Moderna (MRNA) has a US decision on its seasonal influenza vaccine on 05 August, two sessions away, with a unanimous advisory-committee recommendation behind it. Approval is the base case, not the tail. One thing is worth settling in advance. Stéphane Bancel, chief executive since 2011, has a pre-arranged share-sale plan that begins trading on the decision date itself. He cancelled a plan to sell 751,715 shares on 04 May and sold none under it. He replaced it the same day with one that exercises expiring options and sells only enough to cover the strike, the tax and the costs. Two option tranches expire 10 August. Insider filings after the decision will be scheduled, not chosen.
