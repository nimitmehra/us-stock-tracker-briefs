## What Happened

### Our thesis says pass. The trigger says buy.

Micron Technology (MU), the memory maker and a holding, closed at $949.83, up 4.23 percent. Our own file on the name carries a **PASS verdict at conviction 4 out of 10**, dated 29 June, and names **$700** as the price at which we would add. A live instruction on the same name describes an **$850–950 buy band**, and the close sits 17 cents inside its top — roughly $250 above the level our research actually names. The thesis says pass, the ladder says $700, the band says buy near $950. Those three cannot all be right. Micron is a holding, bought before the downgrade, and holding it is a legitimate state; presenting a buy signal on a name our research passes on is not. The formal three-agent stress test on Micron has been listed as pending since 29 June.

### Crude fell. Fuel did not.

Gasoline rose 8.80 percent and heating oil 2.71 percent while crude fell 1.20 percent — all computed from price levels. The refiner's gasoline margin went from **$38.51 to $50.13 a barrel, up 30.2 percent**; the broader three-two-one margin rose 17.2 percent. Two forces collided. Crude was marked down on crude-specific news. Demand forecasts were cut, and the largest US crude build in three and a half years stranded barrels at **Cushing, the US contract's own delivery point**. That marks the benchmark down without adding a gallon of fuel. Fuel rose because capacity burned. An explosion at Gunvor Energy's Europoort terminal in Rotterdam killed one man at about 08:01 New York time; power cuts hit Exxon's Rotterdam refinery; drones struck Russian refineries; a Houthi strike hit Saudi refining; and US refiners already run flat out. One terminal fire does not mechanically justify 8.80 percent on a US contract. It is the trigger on a loaded spring.

### Nobody filed, and that is the proof

Two independent checks agree with the futures. The equity tape voted first: **Valero +3.85 percent, Phillips 66 +3.12, Marathon Petroleum +2.33 against ExxonMobil −0.71** — refiners beating the integrated majors by 3.99 percentage points, none of them names we own or track. The second check is a null result, and it confirms rather than undermines. A sweep of the complete listed US refining set found **not one material-event filing. Valero, CVR Energy and ExxonMobil filed nothing at all.** That is exactly what a foreign-capacity cause predicts: no American asset failed, so no American company had anything to disclose. A roll from the September to the October gasoline contract is ruled out too — winter-grade specifications step the price *down*, so a roll would mask this move, not manufacture it. The absence of paperwork is the evidence.

### Cisco beat. AI took the margin.

Cisco Systems (CSCO), the networking-equipment maker and not a holding, fell 8.40 percent on a record quarter. Revenue was $17.3 billion, up 18 percent; reported earnings per share rose 52 percent; the fiscal-2027 guide accelerates; artificial-intelligence orders reached $9.3 billion for the year. The same filing carries **product gross margin at 64.8 percent against 67.5 — 270 basis points lost**. It also shows **operating cash flow flat at $14.2 billion while net income rose 30 percent.** This is the first filed, quantified evidence in our record that hyperscaler AI revenue is margin-dilutive. The filing was accepted at 16:07:06 New York time on 12 August, after the close, so Wednesday's 2.86 percent gain preceded the company's own numbers. We are long this theme through Micron, SanDisk, Seagate and NVIDIA. Selling more to the hyperscalers is not the same as earning more from them.

### Coherent's record hid a cash hole

Coherent (COHR), the optical-components maker and not a holding, fell 7.99 percent. Revenue was $2,045.5 million, up 33.8 percent; adjusted earnings per share came in at $1.74 against $1.00; the coming quarter is guided **above the quarter just delivered**. Underneath it, full-year operating cash flow fell to **$79.5 million from $633.6 million, down 87.5 percent**. Capital spending of $1,102.9 million turned free cash flow to **minus $1.02 billion, a $1.22 billion swing**. Inventory rose 79.5 percent on revenue up 22.5. Share count went from 155.5 million to 195.7 million, up 25.9 percent, on a **$1,998.6 million equity raise carried as one unexplained cash-flow line**. The narrative never mentions it. The non-standard measures were "refined" and prior periods "recast" — under the exact metric the chief executive headlines. Its filing landed at 16:10:13 on 12 August, after the close, so Wednesday's gain also preceded its own print. Our 12 August call is confirmed correct, and the wire that said the stock "erased an 8 percent rally intraday" is false.

### Four names raised $53.8bn in a week

Cloudflare (NET), the internet-infrastructure company and a holding, sold **$2.50 billion of convertible notes due 2031 at a zero coupon**. Conversion is set at $496.94 — a 60 percent premium — alongside a $259.5 million capped call. Gross convertible debt now stands at $5.625 billion, and a revolving-credit covenant amendment sits in Item 8.01 of the same filing, three days before pricing. That filing was accepted at **17:01:16, after the close, so it cannot explain the 6.22 percent intraday rise to a 52-week high**; capped-call counterparties buying stock to hedge is offered as a labelled hypothesis, not a cause. Alphabet (GOOGL), also a holding, sold $25 billion of notes. Intel raised a $20 billion base offering, **upsized to $23.0 billion when the underwriters exercised their option in full on 11 August** — our 12 August figure was right and is not corrected downward. Charter is swapping 2.25-to-5.375 percent paper into 7.087 and 7.337 percent. Four borrowers, one week, and only one of them got its money for nothing.
