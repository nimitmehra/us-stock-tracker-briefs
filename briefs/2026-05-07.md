## What Happened

### Micron crossed $600. The trim line reset.

Micron Technology (MU), the largest American memory-chip maker, closed Wednesday at $666.59 — up 4.12 percent and at a fresh all-time high. The close is the first session above the $600 super-threshold the trim plan defined — the level at which existing holders are instructed to trim to a one-percent residual; the original $500 trim line now trails the tape by thirty-three percent. For the first day in this rally, Micron underperformed the semiconductor sector ETF by 1.06 percentage points — the marginal AI flow rotated to AMD, Corning and a cluster of compute names with same-day catalysts. The trim instruction was written for a reason; the price now matches it. Cohort rotation is the confirming tell, not the contradicting one.

### Iran-deal optionality firmed. Oil broke. Airlines ran.

President Trump on Tuesday paused Project Freedom, the United States naval guide-out effort, citing progress on a one-page memo with Iran covering end-of-conflict, nuclear demands and Hormuz security. West Texas Intermediate fell to $92.84, off 2.36 percent on the day and 11.6 percent on the week; Brent fell 13.1 percent on the week to $99.06. Pure-play oil producers led energy down — the E&P ETF (XOP) closed -6.24 percent against the broader energy ETF (XLE) at -4.12. Carnival Corporation (CCL) rose 6.79 percent, Royal Caribbean (RCL) 8.75 percent to a fresh record, and Delta Air Lines (DAL) 3.50 percent on jet-fuel relief. The defence ETF (ITA) rejoined the risk-on tape at +3.74 percent — Tuesday's three-session disconnect closed in a single session. Six weeks of war-premium are unwinding from the cohort that wore them most.

### AMD's print reordered the AI cohort.

Advanced Micro Devices (AMD) reported Q1 after Monday's bell — revenue of $10.25 billion against $9.89 billion consensus, data-centre up 57 percent year-on-year, Q2 guide of $11.2 billion. The stock rose 18.61 percent to $421 on Wednesday — the day's largest positive single-stock alpha across the universe. Separately, NVIDIA Corporation (NVDA) announced a $3.2 billion optical-fibre partnership with Corning Incorporated (GLW), with three new AI factories planned in North Carolina and Texas; Corning rose 12.01 percent and NVIDIA 5.77 percent, outperforming the semiconductor ETF for the first session in five. The cohort dispersion was historic — Super Micro Computer (SMCI) ran 24.5 percent, Dell Technologies (DELL) 10.4; Arista Networks (ANET) fell 13.6 percent on a Q1 guide priced for supply constraints, and Skyworks Solutions (SWKS) fell 10.5. The AI regime is no longer broad — it rewards confirmed cash-flow infrastructure and punishes consensus longs that miss on guide.

### The fertilizer cycle printed through.

CF Industries (CF) reported Q1 net earnings of $615 million, up 97 percent year-on-year, on a 19.4 percent revenue gain and a 320-basis-point gross-margin expansion. UAN gross margin moved from 30.2 percent to 42.9 percent in twelve months — the line item that carries the pricing-power claim. The headline included a $170 million one-time litigation settlement gain, disclosed in the lead bullet of the press release; operational EPS ex-settlement was $3.13, up 69 percent. Nutrien (NTR) beat the adjusted-EPS consensus by six percent on record potash volumes but missed the absolute headline; the stock fell 4.44 percent. CF itself fell 6.47 percent on pre-print positioning unwind. Both companies host calls today; the post-print read governs. Mosaic Company (MOS) reports 11 May.
