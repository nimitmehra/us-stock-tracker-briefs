## What Happened

### Bloom landed the AI-power thesis.

Bloom Energy (BE), the fuel-cell maker added to the portfolio on 6 May, posted Q1 revenue of $751m — up 130 percent and 42 percent above consensus. The company also disclosed an expanded Oracle partnership of up to 2.8 gigawatts. Shares closed up 11.58 percent, the largest single-day Tier 1 alpha of the cycle. Constellation Energy (CEG), the nuclear utility most exposed to the same demand, reported adjusted operating EPS up 28 percent that morning and shares fell 3.86 percent. The CyrusOne deal at Freestone is a 2031 cashflow; the Three Mile Island restart has slipped to the same year. Mary Bush, a Bloom director, sold $6.67m of stock on 7 May with no 10b5-1 footnote. The data-center-power thesis just split into a now-cycle leg and a 2031-cycle leg.

### Memory stayed sold out.

Micron Technology (MU) closed Monday at $786, up 5.25 percent — a third session above $700 and 57 percent above the original $500 trim line. SK Hynix, the Korean memory peer, ran 15 percent in its own session to a fresh all-time high; the chief executive told the call that DRAM, NAND and HBM are completely sold out. UBS lifted its price target on the peer from 1.55m to 1.70m won and raised FY26 and FY27 EPS forecasts by 22 and 29 percent. Sandisk (SNDK) and Seagate Technology (STX) — the NAND and HDD legs of the same cohort — held their all-time highs. The trim-to-1-percent sub-action, written for $600, is well past should-already-have-trimmed.

### The AI buildout dollar moved down the supply chain.

Nvidia (NVDA), the GPU leader, closed up 2.94 percent on Monday — modest against the cohort it leads. The harder gains went down the chain: optical names ran 5 to 16 percent; storage and data-center-power names 5 to 11 percent; mobile-pivot peers in the same band. The mega-cap chip names outside Nvidia sat out or sold. The graph absorbed seven new Tier 2 nodes today, covering the optical, custom-silicon, data-center-power and pure-AI infrastructure pockets the wires named with the move. The marginal AI-buildout dollar is no longer paying the GPU king; it is paying the picks and shovels behind him.

### Mosaic withdrew its phosphate guide.

Mosaic Company (MOS) reported Q1 before Monday's open. Net sales were up 14 percent — but GAAP EPS flipped to an 81-cent loss, adjusted EPS missed at five cents against the 20-cent consensus, and adjusted EBITDA fell 24 percent. Management withdrew its FY26 phosphate-production guidance and announced curtailments at the Louisiana, Bartow and Brazil operations; 2026 capex was cut to $1.25bn. Sulfur prices hit a record $1,200 a tonne and remain the structural raw-material chokepoint for phosphate. Shares fell 4.19 percent. Nitrogen-fertilizer names ran 4 to 6 percent on the parallel scarcity case — the fertilizer cohort is now empirically bifurcated, not hypothetically. Phosphate is in active reset; analyst forward-EPS dispersion widens from here.

### Risk sits in vol, not yields.

The S&P closed up 0.24 percent at a fresh all-time high. Underneath, the equity-volatility index (VIX) rose 5.06 percent — a third consecutive session up on a green tape — while the bond-volatility gauge (MOVE) crushed 6.91 percent to a multi-month low. Options dealers are paying to hedge into the highs; the rates desk is paying for the calendar. April CPI lands Tuesday morning. The full-Senate vote on Kevin Warsh, the Federal Reserve chair-designate, falls this week; Jerome Powell, the sitting chair, ends his term on Friday. The first IEEPA-tariff refunds began flowing through US Customs and Border Protection on or about Monday. Stocks held the high; the hedge is in the wing.
