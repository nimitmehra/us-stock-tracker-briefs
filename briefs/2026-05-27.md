## What Happened

### Oil crashed again. The rotation is real.

The morning's dispute is settled: crude fell hard for a second day. Brent settled at $92.86, down 6.8 percent; West Texas crude at $89.41, down 4.8 percent — both extending Tuesday's break after Marco Rubio, the secretary of state, said US–Iran talks had made "some progress," and Iranian state media pledged to restore Strait of Hormuz shipping to pre-war levels within a month. The tape rotated on it. Oil-services fell hardest, the sector fund down 4.0 percent; banks and brokers followed yields lower; airlines, cruise lines, casinos and homebuilders bid on cheaper fuel and a lower cost of money. The index did not move — the two halves netted out. A second straight session of this is a regime forming, not noise. The deal is still unsigned.

### Micron crossed a trillion. The decoupling held.

Micron Technology (MU), the largest American maker of memory chips, closed Wednesday at $928.41, up 3.6 percent, after touching a $1 trillion market value intraday for the first time. What matters is not the milestone but the company it kept. Micron rose while the broad semiconductor complex fell — the sector fund down 1.1 percent, and its most-extended names, the wireless-chip makers, off 6 percent. Seagate Technology (STX), the principal's storage holding, did the same, closing up 2.9 percent at a record. For a second day the makers of memory climbed as the chips around them sold off, and this time the split held into the close. The market is paying only for capacity already sold out on multi-year contracts. Micron's 25 June print is where that gets tested.

### Software split in two after the close.

The cybersecurity derate that hit Cloudflare during the session did not generalise after it. Three results landed after the bell. Snowflake (SNOW), a data-cloud firm, beat and guided higher and rose about 25 percent; Salesforce (CRM) beat on earnings but guided next-quarter sales light and fell about 2.8 percent; Marvell Technology (MRVL) raised its outlook and gained about 3 percent. The market is no longer derating software as a group — it is paying up for AI-data-consumption growth and punishing soft guides. That reframes the read on Cloudflare (NET), the principal's holding, which fell 3.8 percent in the session on the sector move: the risk is the soft-guide camp, not a blanket multiple compression. Tuesday's question now has a more discriminating answer.

### Meta broke its buy trigger.

Meta Platforms (META), the principal's only mega-cap our research rates a buy, closed Wednesday at $635.25, up 3.7 percent — clear of the $616 level our research set as a starter-buy line, the first portfolio holding to trade above its own entry point. The move came on a product launch: Meta began rolling out paid consumer subscriptions across Instagram, Facebook and WhatsApp ($3.99, $3.99 and $2.99 a month) and began testing artificial-intelligence tiers priced up to $19.99. The take-up is unproven and the features are thin, but the strategic signal is real — a first recurring, non-advertising revenue line, and a paid on-ramp that monetises the very AI spending the bears flag. The cheapest of the big tech names just added a reason to own it.

## Cascade Watch

### The disinflation rotation entered its second day

- **What happened:** Falling crude (Brent −6.8 percent) drove a textbook rotation under a flat index — oil-services (−4.0 percent), energy producers, banks, brokers and insurers sold, while airlines, cruises, casinos, autos, transports and homebuilders bid; the graph's oil links to Delta, Carnival and FedEx fired again.
- **Why it happened:** A roughly 15-percent weekly drop in crude on the unsigned Iran framework feeds straight through to fuel-cost-sensitive transport and leisure margins and, via lower long-end yields, into rate-sensitive consumer names — and out of the income banks earn on client cash.
- **How it matters:** A second consecutive session makes this a forming regime, not a one-day reflex — but it rests on a deal that is not signed and a hot inflation print arrives Thursday. Treat the travel and consumer cohort as a momentum trade until the deal or the data confirms it.

### Memory decoupled from the chip selloff and held to the close

- **What happened:** Micron and Seagate rose to fresh records — roughly four points ahead of the semiconductor index — while that index fell 1.1 percent and the wireless-chip names dropped 6 percent; Micron's links to the chip sector and the AI-buildout theme stayed pinned at their ceiling.
- **Why it happened:** The market is paying only for memory capacity already locked into multi-year supply contracts and trimming the chips that ran ahead of demand — memory is sold out, the rest is not.
- **How it matters:** The portfolio owns both sides — the winning makers (Micron, Seagate, Sandisk) and the paying buyers (Microsoft, Alphabet, Nvidia). The makers are now priced for the supercycle and the buyers are not; the winning trade has become the expensive one. Micron's 25 June print is its test.

### Software split into winners and soft guides

- **What happened:** After the close, Snowflake rose about 25 percent on a beat-and-raise while Salesforce fell about 2.8 percent on a light guide and Marvell rose about 3 percent on a raised outlook — fracturing the uniform software-derate read that the morning carried onto the whole group.
- **Why it happened:** The market is now paying up for accelerating AI-data-consumption and punishing decelerating guides, rather than compressing every software multiple together.
- **How it matters:** The blanket compression read no longer applies to Cloudflare — the live risk is the soft-guide camp, not membership in the cohort. Snowflake and Marvell enter the promotion queue on the strength of their prints.
