## Rates & Macro

The ten-year Treasury yield fell nine basis points to 4.41 percent on Wednesday, its second straight session down. The number that matters for the AI book — the inflation-stripped (real) ten-year — fell six basis points to 2.23 percent; the two-year, the market's read on the Fed, eased five to 4.11 percent. Mechanically, a falling real yield is a tailwind for the AI and long-duration trade: when the discount rate drops, far-future profit is worth more today. But the driver mattered more than the direction. Today's drop was an oil crash and a growth scare — crude to pre-war lows, inflation expectations sinking — not a dovish Fed. So the money rotated into rate-sensitive cyclicals and the AI names fell anyway: Seagate dropped 4.3 percent, Microsoft 2.27 percent. The real-yield relief helped AI valuations on paper; the disinflation driver routed the actual buying elsewhere. Separately, Micron's after-hours blowout reaffirmed AI-supplier demand on its own, independent of the rate move. On the exit line: the real ten-year at 2.23 percent sits just under the calibrated danger zone of roughly 2.25 to 2.30 percent. The day before's chip rout was the warning shot — a rate-hike-fear sell-off on a Bank of America note calling for three Fed hikes in 2026 — but today rates came back in. The AI book is just below the exit line, not through it. The backdrop is the same Iran de-escalation that crushed oil: gold fell below $4,000, the whole commodity complex liquidated. Friday's inflation gauge is the test of whether the rate relief was real or a risk-off head-fake.

## What Happened

### Micron's blowout answered the rate scare.

Micron Technology (MU), the largest American maker of memory chips, reported a record quarter after Wednesday's close and the stock jumped about 14 percent in extended trade. Revenue hit a record $41.46 billion, up 346 percent on the year and past the roughly $35.6 billion consensus. Adjusted earnings of $25.11 a share beat by about $4.60, on a record 84.9 percent gross margin. Management guided next quarter to $50 billion and disclosed about $100 billion of contracted revenue across sixteen multi-year customer agreements. The regular-session close was −0.31 percent — that is the pre-print tape; the pop prints Thursday. All three near-term commitments — revenue, margin and earnings — were met or exceeded by wide margins. The quarter converts a contracted demand story from claim to fact: the memory shortage is booked, not spot-froth.

### The day before's chip rout was a rate scare, not a demand crack.

The session the brief skipped — Tuesday, 23 June — was an indiscriminate global sell-off in AI chips, and it matters because Micron's print is its rebuttal. Bank of America reversed its stance and called for three Federal Reserve rate hikes in 2026, lifting the implied odds of a September hike to about 68 percent from 29 percent a week earlier. The crowded long-duration AI trade de-grossed worldwide: NVIDIA fell more than 4 percent, Micron 13 percent off its record, South Korea's KOSPI 9.99 percent, Samsung and SK Hynix each 12 percent. It was a discount-rate shock, not a demand reversal — correlations spiked to one and everything sold together. Micron's blowout a day later supplied the confirmation the fear lacked. The selloff was de-grossing; the print is the fact.

### Great Rotation, phase two: rates down on an oil crash.

The headline indices hid the day's real story. The S&P 500 closed −0.10 percent and the Nasdaq −0.43 percent, but the Dow rose 0.35 percent and the Russell 2000 small-cap index 0.37 percent — the textbook rotation print. The trigger was a crude collapse on Iran de-escalation, which pulled the ten-year yield down nine basis points; that rate move was the master switch. Capital left both legs of the AI trade and the whole commodity complex — gold below $4,000, silver down 8 percent — and crowded into rate-duration cyclicals: homebuilders led (Builders FirstSource up 11.31 percent), alongside travel and retail. The stock-fear gauge (VIX) eased 4.41 percent while the bond-fear gauge (MOVE) rose — a rates event transmitting into equities, not a panic. One event, opposite signs across sectors.
