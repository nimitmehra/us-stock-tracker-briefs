## Rates & Macro

The ten-year Treasury yield, the rate that prices long-dated stocks, closed at 4.75 percent, up seven basis points. The inflation-stripped ten-year — the number that marks down far-future AI profits — rose six to 2.47 percent. The two-year, the market's read on the Fed, rose five to 4.28 percent. On Thursday everything from two months out had moved a basis point or less.

This was a headwind for the AI book, and a clean one. Inflation expectations did not move — the ten-year breakeven sits at 2.28 percent — so the entire long-end rise was real yield. That is the part that hits valuations. The exit line is unchanged: the inflation-stripped ten-year holding above roughly 2.25 to 2.30 percent, or the two-year pushing toward 4.4 to 4.5 percent. Today sits above the first and moved toward the second.

Four things drove it. Second-quarter employment costs rose 0.9 percent against 0.8 expected. Chicago's factory survey printed 57.6 against 55.0. Michigan's consumer survey put one-year inflation expectations at 4.2 percent against a policy rate of 3.50 to 3.75. Three Fed dissenters said publicly that a rate rise is needed now. Crude rose 1.29 percent to $84.67 after Iran's Revolutionary Guard said it disabled two tankers in Hormuz. The thirty-year closed at 5.27 percent, a fresh 52-week high, with the twenty-year still a basis point above it. The Treasury's quarterly refunding lands 08-05.

## What Happened

### Bonds repriced. The index did not notice.

Every Treasury maturity from one year out sold off on Friday. The ten-year closed at 4.75 percent, up seven basis points; the thirty-year rose six to 5.27 percent, a fresh 52-week high. On Thursday nothing from two months out had moved more than a basis point. The cause was data: employment costs beat, Chicago's factory survey beat, and Michigan's one-year inflation expectations printed 4.2 percent. The three Fed officials who dissented for a rate rise this week — Beth Hammack of Cleveland, Neel Kashkari of Minneapolis and Lorie Logan of Dallas — went on the record the same day. Equity coverage led with the S&P up 0.70 percent. The market paid six to seven basis points of term premium for a green day and called it a rally.

### Amazon's profit is a mark, not a result.

Amazon.com (AMZN) rose 15.32 percent to $271.58, closing 1.24 percent below its 52-week high. Cloud revenue of $42.2 billion grew 37 percent at a 39.4 percent margin — the fastest in eighteen quarters, and real. The earnings line is not. Net income of $62.6 billion includes $53.4 billion of pre-tax other income, primarily a fair-value mark on Anthropic. Amazon put $10 billion of equity into Anthropic in the same quarter, lent up to $20 billion more against delivery of cloud capacity, and sold it over $100 billion of ten-year commitments. That round is the price change behind the markup. Trailing free cash flow is an outflow of $7.6 billion, against an inflow of $18.2 billion a year ago. Next quarter's profit guide midpoint sits below the quarter just delivered.

### Apple beat, then guided the margin down.

Apple (AAPL) fell 7.35 percent to $308.91, its worst session in sixteen months, on a quarter that beat. Revenue of $109.4 billion rose 16 percent; earnings came in at $2.02 a share. The guide did the damage. September-quarter growth is put at 9 to 11 percent against a street above 12, and gross margin at 47 to 48 percent against the 50.1 just posted. Management said higher memory costs account for more than the whole decline. Services and Greater China both missed. The balance sheet says it twice. Component inventory rose 3.6 times to $7.6 billion while finished goods stayed flat, and gross intangible assets rose $13.27 billion in nine months with no note explaining why. Tim Cook, chief executive since 2011, reportedly called memory pricing a hundred-year flood.

### The memory de-rate survived good news.

Micron Technology (MU) fell 5.90 percent to $823.03 and Sandisk (SNDK) 5.09 percent, both against a flat semiconductor group, on the day the world's largest hardware buyer confirmed the shortage is worsening. Apple named memory as the reason its own margin guide fell, and is reportedly qualifying Chinese DRAM from CXMT. The tape read both as bad news for the sellers: scarcity now trades as buyer demand destruction plus an invitation to Chinese supply, not as supplier pricing power. Storage split for a second session, hard drives bid and flash sold — Seagate (STX) rose 0.52 percent, Western Digital (WDC) 2.21 percent. Micron left its $850–950 add band to the downside, the third crossing in seven sessions. A level crossed three times in seven sessions is not a decision rule.

### Meta's largest data centre is off the books.

Meta Platforms (META) rose 3.28 percent to $556.71 — mostly sector — and the quarterly report it filed the same day carries a number the press release did not. Meta owns a fifth of a Louisiana data-centre venture and discloses maximum loss exposure of $46.03 billion against a $2.92 billion carrying value — sixteen times. Inside it sits a residual value guarantee with a threshold near $28 billion, carried at zero liability because payment is "not probable". It stays off the balance sheet on the judgment that remarketing the campus drives its economics — while Meta provides the construction, administration and property management. Capital spending for 2026 stands at $130–145 billion. The scenario that calls a data-centre guarantee is the scenario in which "not probable" stops holding.

## Cascade Watch

### Small caps broke; concentration did not deepen

- **What happened:** The Russell 2000 fell 0.50 percent to 2,931.34 on a day the S&P rose 0.70 percent and the Nasdaq 1.00 percent, with 223 companies up and 279 down and the median S&P company down 0.24 percent — but the gap between the cap-weighted and equal-weighted index halved, from 1.66 points on 07-30 to 0.82, and the number of stocks moving more than two percent fell from 245 to 110.
- **Why it happened:** Real long-term rates rose six basis points, which is a direct cost to the small, indebted and unprofitable end of the market; the speculative-innovation fund fell 2.28 percent on a rising Nasdaq, the same signal.
- **How it matters:** What deepened is the size dimension, not the concentration one — the Russell went from up 1.37 percent on 07-30 to down 0.50 percent in a session. Do not write today as "concentration got worse"; write it as "small caps stopped participating," because the two point at different trades.

### Memory scarcity flipped from a supplier signal to a buyer problem

- **What happened:** Apple confirmed on 07-30 that worsening memory costs are why its own margin guide fell, filed a risk factor saying it expects the imbalances to intensify, and disclosed a component hoard 3.6 times larger than at year-end — and Micron fell 5.90 percent and Sandisk 5.09 percent against a flat semiconductor group.
- **Why it happened:** With Apple reportedly qualifying Chinese DRAM and SK hynix lifting next year's capital spending by about half, the market is reading scarcity as a signal that buyers will destroy demand and that Chinese supply has an opening — not that suppliers have pricing power.
- **How it matters:** The de-rating survived the strongest possible good news, which means it is not a valuation story that mean-reverts on a datapoint. Storage is now splitting inside itself for a second session — hard drives bid, flash sold — and that split is thesis-relevant to two holdings at once.

### Cloud spending found its first revenue proof, and two silicon names sat it out

- **What happened:** Amazon's cloud arm grew 37 percent at a 39.4 percent margin, and the read-across lifted Alphabet 6.73 percent, Arista 5.46 percent, Eaton 7.32 percent, Vertiv 6.18 percent, Microsoft 3.02 percent and NVIDIA 2.93 percent — while Broadcom rose 0.37 percent and Taiwan Semiconductor 0.23 percent.
- **Why it happened:** Since 07-23 the tape has punished spending without visible return; this is the first print where the revenue side moved enough to defend it. Broadcom and TSMC make the silicon that spending buys and should have led.
- **How it matters:** Treat one session against a six-session regime as one session. The interesting fact is the non-participation: the two names most directly levered to internal hyperscaler silicon did nothing on the day their own theme rallied.

### Two incumbents were repriced for losing demand to AI, not for missing

- **What happened:** Reddit fell about 23 percent after beating on revenue, profit and forward guidance, because US daily users fell sequentially and management described search referrals as choppy; GoDaddy fell 16.70 percent on soft full-year revenue guidance with brokers naming accelerating AI pressure on web design.
- **Why it happened:** Both are demand-side disintermediation rather than execution failure — and Alphabet, the counterparty accreting the query, rose 6.73 percent the same session.
- **How it matters:** Our existing AI-disruption grouping runs entirely to beneficiaries. It now needs the other side, and GoDaddy has been added as the first anchor for it.

### There was no month-end rebalancing in this tape

- **What happened:** Across 503 S&P constituents the relationship between the month's return and today's return printed positive 0.128, against negative 0.372 on 07-30; sorting by trailing one-month return, the month's best performers were the only group with a positive median.
- **Why it happened:** A pension or target-date rebalance sells the month's winners into the last session, which prints a negative relationship. This is the opposite sign.
- **How it matters:** July was an energy month — energy up 12.76 percent, technology down 5.53, semiconductors down 12.88 and last — so a rebalancing flow would have bought energy and sold technology into the close. The tape did the reverse. The Apple-Amazon dispersion is fundamental, not mechanical.

## Graph Signal

The three largest companies in the book all filed quarterly reports this week, and all three used the same manoeuvre: the cost of the artificial-intelligence buildout was moved somewhere the income statement does not show it. Amazon booked $53.4 billion of pre-tax income as a mark on a private company it is simultaneously funding and selling to, while its own trailing free cash flow ran $7.6 billion negative. Meta kept a $28 billion residual guarantee at zero liability and a $46.03 billion exposure at a $2.92 billion carrying value, on a data-centre venture it builds, administers and manages but does not consolidate. Apple added $13.27 billion of intangible assets in nine months against a matching rise in long-term liabilities, with no acquisition and no note, while cutting its own capital spending 28 percent and telling the SEC for the first time that it may be unable to secure enough third-party computing capacity. Three different structures, one shape: the spending is real, the accounting for it is off to one side, and in each case the disclosure that matters sits in a footnote the press release did not carry. The market paid fifteen percent for one of them on Friday. Read the cash-flow statement before the earnings line on all three.
