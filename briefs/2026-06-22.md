## What Happened

### The AI trade split: buyers sold, suppliers bought.

The first session after the Juneteenth long weekend cut the artificial-intelligence trade in half. The hyperscaler buyers — the companies spending the most on AI with the least proven return — sold off hard. Alphabet (GOOGL), the search and AI group, fell 4.99 percent, its worst day in over a year, after two flagship researchers left Google DeepMind for rivals in three days. Amazon (AMZN) fell 4.75 percent, Microsoft (MSFT) 3.18 percent, Meta Platforms (META) 2.32 percent. The suppliers ran the other way on NVIDIA's unveil of its next-generation Vera Rubin platform: Micron Technology (MU) rose 6.82 percent to a record $1,211.38, Bloom Energy (BE) 5.15 percent to a record. The market sold the spenders and bought what they spend on.

### Moderna fell hard on no bad news.

Moderna (MRNA), the messenger-RNA vaccine maker, fell 7.22 percent to $59.34 — the day's largest single-stock drop in the book, against a healthcare sector that rose. There was no new bad news. Last week the company won its flu-vaccine advisory vote nine to nil, and the shares ran roughly 39 percent into it. Monday gave part of that back: the long weekend handed fast money its first window to take the gains. The 5 August final-approval decision is unchanged and remains the next binary. The tape looks alarming and the thesis does not — a positioning unwind, not a reversal.

### Small-caps led; the Russell 2000 cleared 3,000.

Beneath a barely-red S&P 500 sat a violent rotation. The Russell 2000 small-cap index rose 0.83 percent to 3,004.40, closing above 3,000 for the first time ever, and the Dow held green at plus 0.29 percent while the Nasdaq fell 1.32 percent. Value and cyclical groups — energy, banks, property, industrials — were bought; high-multiple growth was sold. The volatility split confirmed the read: the stock-fear gauge (VIX) rose 5.37 percent to 17.28, but the bond-fear gauge (MOVE) fell 7.46 percent and crash-hedge pricing eased. When stocks wobble but bonds and tail-hedges stay calm, money is rotating, not fleeing.

## Cascade Watch

### Memory makers ran while the megacaps fell.

- **What happened:** Micron rose 6.82 percent, Sandisk 4.07 percent and Seagate 2.22 percent — all to records — on the same down-Nasdaq day the AI-spending megacaps de-rated; Micron's links to the chip sector and the AI-buildout both re-fired at their ceiling.
- **Why it happened:** NVIDIA's Vera Rubin unveil signalled more memory, optical and power content per chip, a supply-shortage trade that runs orthogonal to the spend-versus-return worry hitting the hyperscalers.
- **How it matters:** The supplier side is where the rotating money landed; Micron's Wednesday print is the test of whether the whole memory-supplier thesis holds.

### The AI-spending megacaps de-rated as a bloc.

- **What happened:** Alphabet, Amazon, Microsoft and Meta fell together (down 2.3 to 5.0 percent) on a day Treasury yields were flat and small-caps hit a record — an AI-trade-specific move, not macro beta.
- **Why it happened:** Two top Google AI researchers leaving for rivals crystallised a broader fear that the heaviest AI spenders are over-building while losing the talent to execute it.
- **How it matters:** None of these four carries an authored link to the AI-buildout factor, so the de-rate logged at the macro level, not stock-to-stock — the pattern is a watch item if it extends.

### Iran de-escalation cut oil again.

- **What happened:** A US Treasury 60-day license re-opening Iranian crude plus a roadmap-to-final-deal pushed oil down two to three percent, yet energy stocks rose (the energy ETF up 1.26 percent).
- **Why it happened:** Value money bought cheap energy equity even as the commodity fell — a rotation footprint, not a fundamentals call.
- **How it matters:** Lower crude is a disinflation tailwind and a fuel-cost positive for travel names, against lower realised prices for energy producers.

## Graph Signal

The day's cleanest divergence is what NVIDIA's own stock did. NVIDIA was the catalyst — it unveiled the Vera Rubin platform that sent the chip index to a record — yet its shares fell 0.97 percent while the names that supply it ripped: ON Semiconductor up 8.16 percent, Coherent 9.22 percent, Super Micro 15.66 percent, Micron 6.82 percent. The market priced the new platform as a demand event for everyone downstream of NVIDIA rather than for NVIDIA itself, and folded the stock into the broader megacap-AI de-rate. The picks-and-shovels of the picks-and-shovels got the pop. It is the sharpest evidence yet that the market is now separating who spends on AI from who gets paid for it — worth watching into Wednesday's Micron print.

The bulls point to a calm volatility tape, a record small-cap close and a decoupling chip-supplier rally; the bears point to record capex meeting a retention crisis at the franchise the whole bull case depends on.
