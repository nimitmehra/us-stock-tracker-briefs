## What Happened

### Oil traded the deal. Tehran disputes it.

Brent crude printed $100.21 in thin holiday trading on Monday, down 10.6 percent on the week and toward its first sub-$100 level this month. The drop rode optimism that a Trump-brokered ceasefire will reopen the Strait of Hormuz. The framework is not signed. US officials, briefing the Washington Post, Axios and CNN, describe a 60-day truce with Hormuz reopened toll-free and Iran's enriched uranium surrendered. Iranian state media contradicts the marquee term, insisting the strait stays under Iranian control and that no nuclear commitment exists. The market is trading the press conference, not the strait — and the strait is the term in dispute. Any Iranian contradiction that sticks reprices the premium straight back in.

### Warsh takes the chair. Markets price hikes.

Kevin Warsh was sworn in as the seventeenth chair of the Federal Reserve on Friday, with President Trump publicly instructing him to "be totally independent." Warsh has signalled a regime change — faster balance-sheet run-off, reformed communications, overhauled forecasting. The market is not reading him as dovish. April inflation is still running at 3.8 percent; the University of Michigan's consumer-sentiment gauge sits at a record-low 44.8. Against that, futures lifted the odds of a 2026 rate rise to 40-to-50 percent — for a chair installed on a cut mandate. Thursday's April PCE print is the last inflation read before Warsh's first meeting on 16–17 June. It is the pivot.

### The AI-top selling got cleaner, not bigger.

The SEC record on AI-infrastructure insiders selling into the May rally resolved in two directions this weekend. Sandisk (SNDK), the principal's memory holding, was cleared. The four executive filings tagged on Friday as scheduled sales are in fact tax-withholding on vested stock — routine, not distribution — and drop out of the cross-cohort exit pattern. Palantir Technologies (PLTR) moved the other way. Co-founder and chief executive Alexander Karp's $54.1 million sale on Nvidia Corporation's (NVDA) 20 May print day is now confirmed discretionary, carrying no trading-plan cover. With president Stephen Cohen's tranche, Palantir's two most senior officers sold roughly $97.6 million in a single session. The pattern is smaller but harder. A negative signal earns no promotion, and Palantir stays in the second tier.
