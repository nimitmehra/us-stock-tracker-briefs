A risk-on relief rally — July 1 run backwards. Inflation-stripped real rates eased back out of the danger zone on last Thursday's weak jobs report, and the beaten-down AI complex bounced. ## Rates & Macro

The rate that prices far-future profit relaxed on Monday. The inflation-stripped ten-year yield — the one that moves the AI names — eased two basis points to 2.24 percent, backing out below the 2.25-to-2.30 percent zone it had entered on July 1. The nominal ten-year slipped one point to 4.48 percent; the two-year, the market's read on the Fed, edged one point lower to 4.13 percent. Inflation expectations held at 2.24 percent, so this was a real-yield move — it helped valuations directly, not sentiment.

Today was a tailwind for the AI book, and it is the mirror image of last week's headwind. The exit line — the calibrated danger level — is the real ten-year holding above roughly 2.25 to 2.30 percent, or the two-year pushing toward 4.4 to 4.5 percent on hawkish Fed repricing. We sit just under the lower edge: one hot inflation print re-arms it, so the retreat is a reprieve, not an all-clear.

The driver was the data, not a dovish Fed. Last Thursday's June jobs report came in at 57,000 new positions against about 115,000 expected, with the jobless rate falling to 4.2 percent only because fewer people looked for work. That cooled the hike bets that had pushed real yields into the zone. New Fed chair Kevin Warsh still calls inflation too high, and markets carry odds of a September hike — the tension is a hawkish central bank against a softening labour market. The next inflation print is the swing.

## What Happened

### The AI trade bounced. The debate did not resolve.

The capital-hungry end of the market rebounded on Monday as real rates eased off their danger zone, and the discount-rate relief flowed straight to the highest-duration names. The bounce is narrower than the headline: within the complex, the second-derivative names led — AI-connectivity, AI-storage and merchant-GPU makers up 6 to 13 percent — while the mega-cap front-runners lagged, NVIDIA (NVDA) up just 0.4 percent and Micron Technology (MU) 0.9 percent. And it came after a deeper fall: last Thursday the memory group broke 5 to 14 percent on a report that Anthropic is reportedly building its own chips, a crack in the scarcity story that a one-session rebound on broker upgrades does not seal. The complex is still below its late-June highs. Relief is not resolution.

### Broadcom locked Apple through 2031.

Broadcom (AVGO) disclosed in a securities filing on Monday that it and Apple (AAPL) have agreed to expand their collaboration through 2031, with new multi-year deals for Broadcom to design and supply custom chips across future Apple products. No contract value was given. The win extends the visibility on Broadcom's custom-silicon franchise and closes off the bear tail that Apple designs Broadcom out — a positive read-through for both, one a stress-tested buy candidate, one a portfolio holding. Broadcom rose 3.7 percent, still 22 percent below where it stood a month ago. A rebound inside a drawdown, not a breakout.

### Moderna's flu shot cleared its panel.

Moderna (MRNA), the messenger-RNA vaccine maker, won a unanimous 9-to-0 vote from a Food and Drug Administration advisory committee last Thursday that its flu vaccine has a favourable benefit-risk profile, with a final approval decision set for August 5. The shares rose 2.6 percent to a fresh one-year high, up 67 percent on the month. This converts a sentiment-only re-rate into a dated, data-backed catalyst — a second commercial pillar with a hard approval date. One caution rides underneath: the average analyst price target still sits near $45 against a roughly $80 price. The panel voted on the science; the Street still doubts the sales.

### One red name on a green day.

AeroVironment (AVAV), the drone and counter-drone maker, fell 7.4 percent — the lone large decliner on a broadly green tape, while the rest of the defence group rose. No filing explains it: Monday's insider paperwork was routine equity grants, not selling. The drop is profit-taking after a roughly 35 percent three-day run on a strong quarter and a $500 million Army counter-drone contract, compounded by a soft next-year forecast and pending shareholder lawsuits. Underneath sits the standing driver — a material-weakness-in-controls flag disclosed in last month's audited annual report, still unresolved. Do not read it as a defence-sector break; read it as a name still digesting its own audit.

- **How it matters:** the AI capital-spending-deceleration watch stays merely watching, not fired — do not read one green session as a confirmed reversal.

### The real-rate exit zone stood down

- **What happened:** the real ten-year eased to 2.24 percent, exiting below the 2.25-to-2.30 percent danger zone it had entered on July 1, and the rates signal stepped back from active to armed.
- **Why it happened:** last Thursday's weak jobs report softened the Fed-hike bets that had driven real yields up, relieving the discount-rate pressure on far-future AI profit.
- **How it matters:** the exit line is armed, not fired — spot sits about one basis point under the edge, so the next hot inflation print re-enters it and the whole AI cohort de-rates again.

The day's cleanest read is not the bounce but its shape, and the graph sorts it more finely than the index. Two dispersions line up. Inside the rally, the market paid down the value chain — AI-connectivity, storage and merchant-GPU names up 6 to 13 percent while the front-runners NVIDIA, Micron and KLA lagged or fell — so this is a broadening within the AI trade, not a re-rating of its leaders. And inside memory itself, a split opened: hard-disk names ran with Seagate up 5.9 percent while DRAM and flash sold, Micron off 13 percent on the week and Sandisk 16.6 percent. The tape is buying the picks-and-shovels one layer out and separating storage by media type. Whether the front-runners rejoin, or the laggard signal on NVIDIA and Micron persists, is the read to carry into the next session.

## Source Tone

The two-session window told a "de-rate, deepen, then dip-buy" story rather than a clean turn. Coverage on Thursday was risk-off inside tech — the Anthropic in-house-chip scoop crystallised a "compute scarcity may not be permanent" fear on top of still-elevated real yields, and memory took the brunt. By Monday the outlets converged on "AI optimism revived, chips shake off the dip," with a record Dow close, big-tech leadership, and a wall of memory and AI-connectivity broker upgrades doing the narrative work. The tone is constructive but thin: the rebound is powered by analyst opinion and a macro-relief bid, not fresh demand confirmation, and the overbuild question reopened last week is unresolved. Trump's opening-bell ceremony and a Dell plug added political-tape flavour without moving a thesis.
