## What Happened

### Bonds repriced. Stocks did not retrace.

The ten-year US Treasury closed at 4.595 percent on Friday, up roughly fourteen basis points and at a fresh 52-week high. The thirty-year settled at 5.128 percent, the first close above five percent in many quarters; the five-year hit a 52-week high alongside. The bond-market volatility gauge (MOVE) ripped 14.71 percent to 79.87, leading the day's repricing while the vol-of-vol fell — a positioning re-rating, not a panic. CME FedWatch now prices zero rate cuts in 2026 and roughly a fifty percent chance of a December hike. Powell's chairmanship formally ended Friday; Kevin Warsh takes the chair into the June 16–17 FOMC. The Treasury sold $691 billion of paper this week. The bond market is no longer reading PPI and CPI as energy outliers.

### Beijing fell short. Tariffs didn't even come up.

Three wire services called the Trump–Xi summit "stabilisation, not breakthrough." Donald Trump told Fox News that tariffs "didn't even come up." China agreed to order 200 Boeing jets — well below the 500 figure floated pre-trip. It also pledged to expand US agricultural purchases. US Trade Representative Jamieson Greer told Bloomberg TV that semiconductor export controls were "not a major topic" in formal talks. The market had priced 500 jets, an export-control easing and explicit chip deliverables; none landed. Boeing closed −3.8 percent. The H200 shipment list — Alibaba, Tencent, ByteDance, JD.com — remains reportedly approved, not a single order booked. Nvidia (NVDA) prints Q1 FY27 after the bell on Wednesday. That call has to either guide-in the China line or guide-around it.

### Hormuz re-escalated. Brent broke $109.

Trump told reporters Friday he was "losing patience with Iran"; Foreign Minister Abbas Araqchi said Iran has "no trust" in Washington. A ship was reportedly seized and another sunk near the Strait of Hormuz, which the White House readout still describes as effectively closed. Brent settled at $109.07, up 3.17 percent on the day and 7.72 percent on the week; WTI added 4.20 percent to $105.42. Ukraine's overnight strike on the Ryazan refinery opened the second-order tail. Silver fell 10.61 percent — the largest single-day commodity move on the print. Gold dropped 2.61 percent despite the textbook geopolitical setup; rising real yields and a firmer dollar drained the safe-haven bid faster than war news could feed it. The reflation-trade-as-portfolio-hedge is broken on a second consecutive session.

### The memory cohort split. Microsoft found its marginal buyer.

Micron Technology (MU), the largest American maker of memory chips, fell 6.62 percent to $724.66, off a high of $803.63 earlier in the week. Drivers stacked: hot PPI hostile to high-multiple semis, the Trump-Xi deliverable absence, windfall-tax-on-AI-memory chatter, and a Samsung HBM4 qualification milestone that complicates Micron's Vera Rubin share. Inside the same memory complex, Sandisk (SNDK) closed up 1.80 percent — alpha of +5.60 points against the semiconductor ETF on its worst day of the year. NAND and HDD decoupled cleanly from HBM for a second day. Microsoft (MSFT) closed +3.05 percent — alpha of +4.86 points against the tech ETF — after Pershing Square's Bill Ackman built a new position and fully exited Alphabet to fund it. The MSFT-cohort-isolation pattern of the prior week found its marginal buyer.

## Graph Signal

The clean divergence of the day sits between the bond market and the American consumer.
