## What Happened

### The mega-cap AI complex splits in two

For two years the question with American hyperscalers has been how much capital they could be persuaded to spend on artificial-intelligence infrastructure. On Thursday the question changed. Alphabet (GOOGL), the parent of Google and Google Cloud, reported Q1 revenue of $109.9bn against $107.1bn consensus and lifted its 2026 capital-expenditure plan to $180-190bn from $175-185bn; shares closed +9.97%, the company's best month since 2004. Meta Platforms (META) raised its own 2026 capex range to $125-145bn — the same gesture, +$10bn at both ends — and the tape punished it -8.55%, slicing roughly $130bn from its market value in a single session. Microsoft (MSFT) -3.93% and NVIDIA (NVDA) -4.63% were trapped in the same downdraught; Qualcomm (QCOM) +15.12% on a print that pushed automotive silicon past a $5bn run-rate, and Advanced Micro Devices +5.16%, were not. The rule the market wrote in real time: capex is welcome where Cloud earns it back from external customers, and unwelcome where the spending feeds first-party AI only. For Micron Technology (MU), the largest American memory maker, the read-through is mechanical — hyperscaler capex still buys high-bandwidth memory — yet Micron closed -0.25% against a +1.43% Philadelphia Semiconductor index, the first session it has not participated in the AI-memory bid since its post-print re-rate. Whether that is positioning fatigue or the front edge of an AI-memory-derate is the question into next week. [CONFIRMED — Alphabet, Meta, Microsoft, NVIDIA, Qualcomm Q1 releases, 30 April; market-data.json, 30 April close]

### ConocoPhillips becomes the first oil major to formally guide-down on Iran

ConocoPhillips (COP), one of the largest American independent exploration-and-production companies, reported Q1 2026 on Thursday and used the print to do something none of its larger peers has yet done: it took Qatar entirely out of its second-quarter production guidance, citing "uncertainty surrounding the conflict in the Middle East", and trimmed its 2026 production range to 2.295-2.325 million barrels of oil-equivalent per day. Adjusted earnings per share of $1.89 beat the $1.73 consensus by 9.2%; cash from operations excluding working capital came in at $5.4bn, against which the company returned $2.0bn to shareholders evenly split between buybacks and dividends. The forensic read on the 10-Q is unusually clean — a 181,000-character body with zero hits on the engineered-disclosure phrase set — meaning the Qatar exclusion is being put on the record rather than buried in it. ExxonMobil (XOM) and Chevron (CVX) report this morning and now have a cohort baseline to disclose against; whether they match the Qatar-out language or hedge it will set the oil-major disclosure culture for the rest of the cycle. [CONFIRMED — ConocoPhillips 10-Q + 8-K Item 2.02, 30 April; press release Exhibit 99.1]

### Adobe's CEO takes $18.3m off the table — and not under a 10b5-1 plan

Shantanu Narayen, chief executive of Adobe (ADBE) since 2007 and chair since 2017, sold 75,000 shares from the Narayen Family Trust on 28 April at a weighted-average price of about $243.50, for total proceeds of roughly $18.3m. The Form 4 disclosing the trade was filed on Wednesday with `<aff10b5One>0</aff10b5One>` — meaning Mr Narayen did not transact under a Rule 10b5-1 plan and so did not have its affirmative defence against trading on material non-public information. The footnote frames the sale as "primarily made for the reporting person's tax and estate planning purposes", the standard non-MNPI carve-out. Two facts sharpen the read. First, this is the chief executive's first discretionary open-market sale of 2026; his prior Form 4s have been ordinary RSU-vest-and-tax-withhold cycles. Second, the trade was executed the day after the Adobe-NVIDIA strategic partnership and Firefly AI Assistant public-beta announcements (27 April) — precisely the news flow the bull case rests on. The trust's position fell 17.2% in a single day. The information here is the magnitude and the choice to step outside Rule 10b5-1, not a thesis-break; the principal's existing Adobe weighting should incorporate it before the next position-sizing review. [CONFIRMED — SEC Form 4 accession 0000796343-26-000105, filed 30 April]

### Delta swings to a clean adjusted print and refreshes its C-suite

Delta Air Lines (DAL), one of the largest US carriers by capacity, reported Q1 2026 on Thursday morning with adjusted earnings per share of $0.64 against a year-earlier $0.45 — a 42% rise — alongside a GAAP loss of $-0.44 per share that pulls the special-items gap wider than usual. The same release announced the retirement of operations chief John Laughter and a coordinated three-seat reshuffle: a new president, a new chief operating officer and Erik Snell — appointed to the Wheels Up board last week — promoted to chief financial officer. The shares rose 2.6% to $67.99 and joined Carnival (CCL, +3.64%) in the day's airline-and-cruise relief tape. Delta's adjusted-versus-GAAP gap is the operating-leverage thesis re-stated: jet-fuel relief partly captured at the adjusted line, special-items still working through the GAAP. The 8-K had not landed in EDGAR by Friday's IST cut-off; tomorrow's pipeline picks up the deep-research refresh. [CONFIRMED — Delta IR press release, 1 May; Reuters; market-data.json]
