## What Happened

### The AI trade rotated: software sold, the buildout bought.

The same Jensen Huang keynote that melted software up on Monday reversed it on Tuesday. Investors re-read two Computex lines — "optics where you must, copper where you can" and "Vera Rubin in full production" — and rotated out of the AI application layer into the physical buildout. Optical-interconnect makers ran 10 to 18 percent on NVIDIA's $6.5 billion of photonics stakes; chip-equipment names hit fresh highs; the software complex unwound 5 to 9 percent. The indices closed at records regardless, small-caps leading. Among holdings, Microsoft (MSFT) fell 4.2 percent and Alphabet (GOOGL) 3.9 percent as funding sources, while NVIDIA (NVDA) slipped 0.7 percent — the keynote paid the suppliers, not the platform. Software sold as yields fell; this was a rotation, not a rate move.

### Hewlett Packard Enterprise put a number on the demand.

Hewlett Packard Enterprise (HPE) printed its best quarter on record and closed up 19.5 percent. Fiscal-second-quarter revenue rose 40 percent to $10.7 billion, networking jumped 148 percent, and non-GAAP earnings of $0.79 beat the $0.54 consensus. Management lifted full-year revenue-growth guidance to 29–33 percent from 17–22 percent, and earnings to $3.35–$3.45 from $2.30–$2.50. This is the cleanest confirmation yet of the AI-server demand the rotation is paying for — the server maker is now the cohort's earnings proof, not just its price action. Broadcom reports Wednesday after the close, with the Street looking for $10.7 billion of AI revenue and 25 percent sequential growth. That print is the read-through that tells the cohort whether Tuesday was a re-rating or a reach.

### The rate-cut bet got priced out. The cohort shrugged.

The bond market quietly repriced the year. Futures now imply roughly even odds of a Federal Reserve rate hike in 2026 and under 10 percent odds of a cut — a sharp move from last week's near-certain hold — after the thirty-year Treasury yield touched 5 percent intraday. The trigger was Tuesday's April job-openings report: 7.62 million openings, far above the 6.8 million expected and the highest in two years, with hiring still falling. A frozen-but-resilient labour market hands incoming Federal Reserve Chair Kevin Warsh cover to lean hawkish at his first meeting on 16–17 June. The AI cohort ignored all of it and closed at records. That rate-indifference is itself the signal: the buildout trade is, for now, being priced as if the long end does not exist.

## Cascade Watch

### AI capex rotated from software to the physical buildout

- **What happened:** On one keynote, optical-interconnect makers ran 10 to 18 percent and chip-equipment names hit fresh highs, while the software complex (Snowflake, ServiceNow, Workday, Palantir, Adobe) fell 4 to 7 percent and the indices still closed at records.
- **Why it happened:** The market re-read Huang's "copper-vs-optics" and "Vera Rubin in full production" lines literally and started paying for where the AI capital actually lands — interconnect, equipment, power, copper — rather than for generic "AI exposure."
- **How it matters:** The thesis is broadening down the stack, not breaking. The deployment read favours the buildout sub-cohorts (optics, power, chip-equipment) over application-layer software, whose multiples are now the rotation's funding source.

### The software-disruption bear thesis reasserted

- **What happened:** Adobe's link to the AI-disruption theme hit its weight ceiling on a 4.4 percent drop, as the whole software-as-a-service cohort gave back Monday's melt-up in a single session.
- **Why it happened:** The "agents replace the workflow" fear flipped the cohort back from beneficiary to disruption-target — exactly the read that drove Monday up, run in reverse.
- **How it matters:** Treat Monday's software rally as a one-session positioning event, not a regime change; the application layer is where the rotation sourced its cash, not where it parked it.

### Energy and off-price retail edges fired on their own catalysts

- **What happened:** Cheniere's link to oil prices strengthened on a firmer crude tape (the stock up 3.6 percent), and Ross Stores' link to consumer-discretionary maxed out on a quarter showing revenue up 20.6 percent.
- **Why it happened:** Oil firmed on the live Iran tail while off-price retail printed a genuine acceleration — both independent of the AI rotation that drove the rest of the tape.
- **How it matters:** Both are small confirmations that the cyclical-value leg of Tuesday's rotation — banks, energy, materials, value retail — has fundamental support underneath it, not just flows.
