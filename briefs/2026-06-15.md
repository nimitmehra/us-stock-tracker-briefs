## What Happened

### Hormuz reopens. Oil broke; long-duration ran.

The United States and Iran signed a memorandum of understanding over the weekend to end the war and reopen the Strait of Hormuz, ceasing fighting for sixty days. Crude fell about five percent to a since-March low — West Texas Intermediate settled at $80.60, down 5.04 percent. The deal removed a real, binding supply constraint: Iran's blockade had cut OPEC-and-allies output to roughly 33 million barrels a day. Oil down meant inflation relief, so the ten-year Treasury eased to 4.469 percent, and the highest-duration assets re-rated — the S&P rose 1.65 percent, the NASDAQ 3.07 percent. The formal signing in Geneva is still pending, and reported Israeli strikes in Lebanon threaten the framework. A peace dividend is priced before the ink is dry.

### Memory makers ran on a wall of upgrades.

The day's second engine was idiosyncratic. Micron Technology (MU), the largest American memory-chip maker, closed at $1,087.99, up 10.84 percent to a fresh all-time high, after UBS raised its price target to $1,625 from $535 on sold-out 2026 high-bandwidth-memory capacity. Seagate Technology Holdings (STX) rose 9.43 percent and Sandisk (SNDK) 6.45 percent, both to records, on Mizuho target raises into a structural NAND and hard-drive shortage that the firm sees lasting until 2028. The tape paid the supply-chain bottleneck and underpaid the compute incumbent — NVIDIA (NVDA) rose 3.54 percent but lagged its own semiconductor ETF. Three of the principal's four most-overdue trim signals sit inside this cohort. The shortage is real; the trims are the discipline.

### Seagate's bosses sold into their own record.

As Seagate printed a new all-time high, its top executives sold. Four officers — chief executive William Mosley, chief technology officer J.C. Morris, chief financial officer Gianluca Romano and chief commercial officer Ban Seng Teh — together sold roughly $4.4 million of stock into the up-session, with no pre-planned sale plan named in the filings. The sale is small against the move and may be partly routine grant settlement. But a four-person cluster trimming into a record, on the day analysts raised targets, is a tell worth holding alongside the supercycle story. Management took some chips off a table it is publicly bullish on.

## Cascade Watch

### Oil's break fired the whole energy and travel chain

- **What happened:** The Hormuz-reopening news refreshed nine links from oil prices out to energy producers and travel names — ExxonMobil, Chevron, ConocoPhillips, Diamondback, Petrobras and liquefied-gas exporter Cheniere on the producer side, Carnival, Delta and FedEx on the consumer side.
- **Why it happened:** A five-percent oil drop is bad for producers' revenue and good for fuel-heavy cruise, airline and freight names, so a single catalyst pushed the two cohorts in opposite directions at once.
- **How it matters:** No portfolio holding sits on an oil link, so this is a read on where the cheaper-fuel money is flowing, not a position alert — the energy sector fell 3.5 percent while airlines rose, and the principal owns neither leg.

### Memory's supercycle tightened the AI links again

- **What happened:** Micron's record session refreshed its links to the chip-cycle and the AI-build-out themes, both already at their ceiling, while the semiconductor-sector signal logged the within-group rotation toward memory.
- **Why it happened:** A coordinated analyst-target avalanche across the memory and storage makers confirmed the same shortage story the graph has tracked for weeks — peer capacity is sold out, so the pricing power is corroborated.
- **How it matters:** The corroboration strengthens the held thesis into Micron's 24–25 June report, but it also deepens the overshoot on the trim signals that the same names carry.

### No trigger changed status; three sit deeply fired

- **What happened:** Despite the moves, no Tier 1 trigger transitioned today — Micron's $500 trim, Sandisk's $1,800 legacy-share exit and Meta's $560 add all stayed where they were, refreshed with today's readings.
- **Why it happened:** All three were already in their fired or armed state; new records extend the distance past them rather than crossing a fresh line.
- **How it matters:** Sandisk's sell-into-strength signal is the most overdue, intensifying from +4.5 percent above its line on 11 June to +17.1 percent today; Meta's rally widened its add gap, so the cheapest add no longer sits close.

## Sector Pulse

The AI build-out broke into three distinct cohorts today, none of which maps to a single sector fund. The memory-and-storage makers led — Micron, Seagate, Sandisk, plus watch-list Western Digital and Marvell, all up six to sixteen percent on a wall of analyst raises against sold-out capacity; this is the supply-chain bottleneck being paid ahead of the compute layer, and it strengthens the held storage thesis. A second cohort, AI-power and strategic metals, ran alongside — uranium, copper and the nuclear utilities up on data-center electricity demand. The cross-current the funds miss is in commodities: gold rose 2.92 percent into a risk-on, yields-down tape, and tail-hedge demand ticked up even as the volatility gauge collapsed. The equity rally and the hard-asset bid are being driven by two different things — rate relief in one, structural debasement in the other.

## Source Tone

The weekend rewrote the engine. Friday's wires pinned a calm grind on de-escalation hopes; by Monday those hopes had hardened into a signed memorandum, and every major desk — Reuters, Bloomberg, CNBC, NBC — converged on the same two drivers, Hormuz and the record SpaceX listing that minted the first trillionaire. The framing is unusually one-directional, with the Federal Reserve meeting one day out as the only obvious counterweight. The genuinely fresh tell versus Friday: the oil-down leg now rests on a physical-supply cause rather than sentiment, while Friday's clean metals-up debasement read got muddier, since de-escalation cuts both ways for silver.
