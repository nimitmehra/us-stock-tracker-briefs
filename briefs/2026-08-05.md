## Rates & Macro

The ten-year Treasury yield, the rate that prices long-dated stocks, closed unchanged at 4.63 percent — not one basis point of movement on a day the Dow rose and the Nasdaq fell. The two-year, the market's read on the Federal Reserve, fell two to 4.18 percent. The inflation-stripped ten-year — the number that marks down far-future artificial-intelligence profits — rose one, to 2.41 percent.

The composition is the only informative part. A flat nominal yield swapped a basis point of inflation compensation for a basis point of real yield: expected inflation fell to 2.22 percent from 2.23. Only the real part reaches valuations, so this was a headwind for the AI book — a very small one, and the smallest that still counts as the wrong direction.

The exit line is unchanged: the inflation-stripped ten-year holding above roughly 2.25 to 2.30 percent, or the two-year pushing toward 4.4 to 4.5 percent. Today sits above the first — as it has in every session our file holds back to 29 July, never once dipping into the band — and well clear of the second. Treat it as a calibration, not a promise.

Underneath, the US data was stagflationary in miniature. Private payrolls rose 44,000 in July, the slowest pace of the year and short of expectations; the services activity survey stayed in expansion but missed forecast with strong input costs. On the same day three Fed officials talked about rates — Mary Daly, president of the San Francisco Reserve Bank, Lisa Cook, a governor of the Board, and Neel Kashkari, president of the Minneapolis Reserve Bank — and two of them talked about raising them, Mr Kashkari saying the central bank "should raise rates now." Slowing hiring against sticky services prices and a live hike debate is the least comfortable configuration long-duration equity can be handed. The bond market did not blink. The Treasury's quarterly refunding statement was scheduled for Wednesday and could not be reached on any channel available to us; it is unresolved, not absent.

### The flu approval landed six hours late

The Food and Drug Administration approved Moderna's (MRNA) mFLUSIVA on Wednesday, the first messenger-RNA influenza vaccine cleared in the United States. It was announced at about 22:15 Eastern, six hours after the bell, with nothing on file at the SEC through the close.
