## Rates & Macro

The ten-year Treasury — the rate that prices long-dated stocks — closed at 4.60%, up five basis points, and the inflation-stripped ten-year rose four points to 2.35%. The two-year, the market's read on the Fed, added three points to 4.21%. On the surface this looks like last week. Underneath, the engine reversed. On 07-16 the real-yield rise was cooling-inflation — inflation expectations (the ten-year breakeven) were *falling* to 2.22%, a benign valuation reset. Today the breakeven turned back *up* to 2.25%, because oil is spiking. That makes today a headwind for the AI book, and a worse one than last week: real rates, inflation expectations and the energy cost line now all push the same way.

The exit line for the AI trade is unchanged and now breached on the wrong side — the inflation-stripped ten-year holding above roughly 2.25–2.30%, or the two-year pushing toward 4.4–4.5%. Real yields sit inside that danger zone and are climbing, not backing away. The macro removes the cushion: new Fed chair Kevin Warsh has said prices are too high, the June rate-committee minutes showed roughly half the officials favoring a *hike* before year-end, and the July meeting on the 28th–29th is priced near-certain to hold. Oil is why the Fed can't ease — Brent $88.69, up about 20% on the month — while the jobs market stays tight (jobless claims fell to 208,000) and only housing shows a clear soft patch. Rates, crude and a hawkish chair are pointed one direction, and it is not the one the AI multiple needs.

## What Happened

### The Gulf war is now the oil price

The Iran risk premium stopped being a premium and became a supply story. As of Monday the United States had struck Iran for a tenth consecutive night; Iran's Revolutionary Guard fired fresh missile and drone waves at US bases in Bahrain and Kuwait, three US service members have been killed, and Washington reimposed a naval blockade of Iranian ports while Yemen's Houthis declared a maritime embargo on Saudi Arabia. Crude carried the whole of it — Brent $88.69 and West Texas $82.36, up roughly 20% on the month, with US pump prices back near $4 a gallon. Strait of Hormuz traffic, a fifth of the world's seaborne oil, is thinning as tankers reroute. This is the reinflation engine, and it is escalating, not cooling.

### AI demand bought against a worse tape

The day's real tension: company-specific AI demand fighting a deteriorating macro, and winning the session. Iris Energy (IREN) rose 19.6% after disclosing $2.8 billion of new multi-year AI-cloud contracts — Microsoft, NVIDIA and Perplexity among the signers — and lifting its year-end recurring-revenue target above $4 billion, roughly 85% now contracted. Hut 8 (HUT) signed a $9.8 billion AI data-center lease the same session. Together that is $12.6 billion of fresh committed compute, and the memory and storage cohort re-risked on it — Micron (MU), SanDisk (SNDK) and Seagate (STX) all closed green while the index fell. Read it precisely: this is not a de-rating reversal, because the macro got worse. It is specific demand pushing against a heavier backdrop.

### Alphabet's Wednesday print is the referee

Alphabet (GOOGL), the search and cloud group, reports second-quarter results Wednesday after the close — the first of the seven mega-caps to print, and the market's first read on whether hyperscaler AI capital spending is turning into profit. The Street looks for about $2.89 a share on $116.8 billion of revenue, with Google Cloud eyed near $22.8 billion. The overhangs are a reported delay to the Gemini 3.5 model, a $190 billion capital-spending bill, and suspended buybacks. Days after the neocloud contracts reaffirmed the demand side, Alphabet tests the profit side.

### Bloom Energy broke $200 on no news at all

Bloom Energy (BE), the fuel-cell maker and a portfolio holding, fell 8.3% to $197.06, breaking below $200 for the first time in this drawdown — down about a third in two weeks. There was no SEC filing behind it; a completed search of the EDGAR record found nothing new. The drivers are a scandium-sourcing supply risk newly flagged by TD Cowen, project-execution slippage on the Oracle and AEP data-center buildouts, a short-seller overhang and $44 million of insider selling with no buys. This is positioning and the rate unwind, not fresh fundamental damage — but it now sits just above the level where we start trimming.

## Cascade Watch

### The rate squeeze changed its engine

- **What happened:** The macro-rate factor stayed at its active danger level, but its mechanism flipped — real yields rose 07-16 on falling inflation expectations (cooling inflation), and today rose again on inflation expectations turning *up* to 2.25% as Brent climbed toward $89.
- **Why it happened:** The US–Iran shooting war pushed crude about 20% higher on the month, and oil-driven inflation expectations dragged the whole complex the same way rather than offsetting it.
- **How it matters:** This is strictly worse for the AI book than last week's cooling-inflation reset — there is no Fed-cut cushion when rates, inflation expectations and energy all point up together.

### Oil's supply premium re-lit the producer and shipper links

- **What happened:** The oil factor escalated on the Hormuz shooting war, firing its links to FedEx, Petrobras, Carnival, Delta and the energy producers (Exxon, Chevron, ConocoPhillips, Diamondback, Cheniere).
- **Why it happened:** A live blockade plus a Houthi embargo turned a threat premium into an actual supply disruption, which raises fuel costs for transport names and revenue for producers at once.
- **How it matters:** Watch the split — the same crude move that lifts the energy cohort taxes the shippers and airlines, a clean cross-current if oil holds these levels into the week.

### Memory suppliers and their largest buyer split on one fact

- **What happened:** Micron, SanDisk and Seagate bounced on AI-storage tightness while Apple fell 2.1% on the very same fact — a BofA warning that storage-chip supply constraints will cut roughly 190 basis points from Apple's next-quarter gross margin.
- **Why it happened:** Tight AI-driven storage supply is a tailwind for the firms that sell the chips and a cost headwind for the largest firm that buys them.
- **How it matters:** The memory links to the chip sector and the AI-disruption factor reaffirmed on real demand, but the rotation is intra-tech positioning, not a broad risk-on — small-caps and credit did not confirm.
