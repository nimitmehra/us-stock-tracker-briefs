## Cascade Watch

### The market paid for scarcity and sold its cure

- **What happened:** Across the three sessions, memory makers Micron and Sandisk rose 3.33% while the equipment makers that build memory capacity — Applied Materials, Lam Research, KLA, ASML and Teradyne — fell 3.83%, a **7.16-point gap, of which 6.95 points landed on Monday alone.**
- **Why it happened:** The reading is that the market is paying for the rent that scarcity produces and refusing to pay for the capital spending that would end it.
- **How it matters:** **This is a hypothesis, not a mechanism, and it comes with its test attached.** If the gap stays positive on 1 September it has a second independent session behind it; if it collapses the way the hard-disk gap just did, three-session comparisons in this complex are noise and we stop resting conclusions on them. **The competing explanation is not separable:** Applied Materials' 13 August guidance implied slowing shipments, and an equipment guide is itself a memory-capital-spending datapoint.

### One legislature moved two stocks and no sector

- **What happened:** Utilities fell 2.20% as a group on Monday, but the median utility fell only 0.58% and utilities outside California fell 0.86%; the gap is entirely Edison International and PG&E, with Sempra at −3.60% supplying the gradient.
- **Why it happened:** California amended a wildfire bill over a weekend to remove liability protection investors had priced in; the exposure is to one state's law, not to rates or to the sector.
- **How it matters:** The correct read is a California regulatory exposure, not a utilities signal — and **the template generalises: a legislature reversed an expected corporate backstop at the last minute.** PG&E delivers its response on a webcast on Wednesday 2 September.

### AI capital is now recruiting balance sheets from outside technology

- **What happened:** In one session an oilfield-services company paid about $4.1bn of enterprise value for a German cooling business, and a rocket company said it would cast its own gas-turbine blades because the three firms in the world that make them are booked to 2030.
- **Why it happened:** The constraint on the artificial-intelligence build-out has moved out of silicon and into metal, heat and power, and that constraint is now attracting capital and vertical integration from outside the technology sector.
- **How it matters:** **That is exactly how scarcity rents end.** It cuts against the premium embedded in the power-equipment and castings complex, and it puts a public price on data-centre thermal assets.

### Discovery is blind to material-event filings

- **What happened:** Our discovery scan queries only quarterly and annual reports plus a handful of foreign forms. **Material-event filings are in no query at all**, which was demonstrated twice this window: Boston Scientific's cyber disclosure and PG&E's Senate Bill 492 disclosure both had to be found by other routes.
- **Why it happened:** The scan was built to find reported periods, and a material-event filing has none.
- **How it matters:** **Silence from the broad scan about a name is uninformative — it is not evidence that nothing happened.** PG&E's 20% single-session move was explained by exactly that category of filing, and we cannot see it.
