## What Happened

### Metals rose with stocks. The split is the signal.

Gold rose 3.66 percent to $4,239.90, silver 6.63 percent, copper 3.44 percent and palladium 5.01 percent — all on a day the stock-fear gauge fell 9 percent and oil dropped to an eight-week low. Metals and equities rising together while oil falls is not a haven reflex; havens rise when stocks fall. The cause is structural: China's central bank bought gold for a nineteenth straight month, and the AI build-out needs copper for power. Beneath a quiet index that gained half a percent, the cross-asset tape told a debasement story the headline number hid. The index level was the least interesting thing about the day.

### Seagate called its convertible debt into a record high.

Seagate Technology (STX), the hard-drive maker and a portfolio holding, said it will redeem all of its 3.50 percent convertible notes due 2028 — about $150.7 million of debt that could turn into shares — with a redemption date of 8 September. The stock rose 7.25 percent to $931.04, the day's largest gain in the holdings and a fresh high. Clearing a 2028 dilution overhang off the balance sheet while the stock sits near a record is a clean act of housekeeping, layered on the artificial-intelligence storage-demand cycle already lifting the name. The chief financial officer's small unplanned share sale a day earlier is the only smudge on an otherwise tidy print.

### Bloom Energy proved the AI-power thesis pays its own way.

Bloom Energy (BE), the fuel-cell maker and a holding, rose 4.56 percent to $260.22 on two pieces of deal news: a master agreement with cloud firm Nebius worth up to $2.6 billion, and an expanded Oracle partnership lifting committed fuel-cell capacity to as much as 2.8 gigawatts. The backlog now sits near $20 billion, and the chief executive said no share sale is needed to fund the Oracle ramp. That last line answers the question hanging over the whole sector — who pays for the AI build-out — for this name specifically: the spending is turning into Bloom's revenue, self-funded. The funder of the funder, it turns out, does not need to pass the hat.
