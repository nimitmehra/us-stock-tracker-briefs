## Rates & Macro

The ten-year Treasury yield, the rate that prices long-dated stocks, closed at 4.68 percent, up one basis point. The inflation-stripped ten-year, the number that marks down far-future AI profits, held at 2.41 percent. The two-year, the market's read on the Fed, rose one basis point to 4.23 percent; every maturity from two months out moved as little.

For the AI book this was neither tailwind nor headwind. Real yields were flat, so nothing in the valuation channel changed; the gain came from risk appetite, with the stock-market fear gauge (VIX) collapsing 17 percent to 17.09. The exit line is unchanged: the inflation-stripped ten-year holding above roughly 2.25–2.30 percent, or the two-year pushing toward 4.4–4.5 percent. Today sits above the first line and static — uncomfortable, not deteriorating.

Second-quarter growth came in at 1.5 percent annualised against 2.1 percent expected, and inflation cooled on both measures: the quarterly core reading inside the growth release fell to 3.4 percent from 4.4 percent, and the separate monthly June reading of the Fed's preferred gauge eased to 3.3 percent from 3.4 percent. Crude fell again, WTI to $83.59. The doves got everything and the long end gave nothing back. The thirty-year sits at 5.21 percent, its 52-week high, with the twenty-year above it at 5.22 percent — a small inversion at the very end of the curve. Equity risk premium compressed today; term premium did not. Watch the thirty-year, not the VIX.

## What Happened

### One stock rose. The market fell.

Microsoft (MSFT) closed up 15.51 percent at $451.10 on Thursday, adding about $448.6 billion of market value in one session. Wires reported it as the largest single-day gain by any company; the margin over NVIDIA's April 2025 record is roughly $8 billion, which is inside how precisely such a thing can be measured. The fiscal-fourth-quarter print beat on revenue — $90.01 billion against $87.61 billion expected — with Azure revenue up 43 percent. That one stock carried the index. Beneath it, 197 S&P 500 constituents rose and 304 fell; the median company lost 0.63 percent on a day the index gained 1.66 percent. Homebuilders, banks, transports, property and insurers all closed lower. This was concentration, not a rally.

### The memory glut lasted one day.

Samsung Electronics told its earnings call on Thursday that the memory shortage worsens into 2027 and holds through 2028. It is also moving 60 to 70 percent of its capacity into five-year contracts with floor pricing. That is the opposite of the glut the tape priced twenty-four hours earlier. Micron Technology (MU) rose 18.36 percent to $874.66, back inside the price range our own research sets for adding, one session after breaking below it to $739. Sandisk (SNDK) rose 25.99 percent and Seagate Technology (STX) 11.41 percent. Apple, Amazon and Meta each said they are paying more for memory or components, all inside the same thirty hours. Three buyers with every reason to stay quiet said the same thing.

### Meta got cheaper and worse together.

Meta Platforms (META) fell 7.95 percent to $539.03 — the regular-session verdict on Wednesday's after-hours print — and crossed the level our research set for adding on capital-spending weakness. The quarterly report filed the same day made that caution worse. Free cash flow fell 91 percent to $784 million. The filing discloses $349.31 billion of non-cancelable commitments and $10.80 billion of cash escrowed until 2028–2030. It adds about $68 billion of further data-centre leases signed in July, and roughly $13 billion of guarantees on a Texas venture Meta owns a fifth of. Debt rose 42 percent in six months to $84 billion, buybacks stopped, and the core apps business's operating margin fell from 53 percent to 39 percent. The price came to us; the business went the other way.

### Apple's beat was borrowed; Amazon's was marked.

Apple (AAPL) and Amazon.com (AMZN) both reported after Thursday's close, so the verdict trades Friday. Apple beat on revenue and earnings, but tariff refunds supplied two points of gross margin and eleven cents of the $2.02 per share. Services missed. The September guide of 9 to 11 percent growth sits under the street's roughly 12 percent, on named memory and chip-node shortages. The shares fell 6.65 percent after hours. Amazon's cloud arm grew 37 percent to $42.2 billion, its fastest in eighteen quarters, and the shares rose 7 to 9 percent late. But $53.4 billion of the pre-tax income behind that $5.75 of earnings is a mark on its Anthropic stake. Trailing free cash flow is an outflow of $7.6 billion, against an inflow of $18.2 billion a year ago.

### Bloom's record quarter has one customer.

Bloom Energy (BE) rose 26.49 percent to $207.12 on a reported Mizuho upgrade, finally paying out the blowout quarter that Wednesday's risk-off tape buried. An amended quarterly report, filed to fix a "three months / six months" transposition, disclosed what the original obscured. One customer was about 73 percent of second-quarter revenue; across six months, two customers were 44 and 21 percent, the second of them a related party. That is on a quarter of 165 percent revenue growth. The amendment was clerical; what it corrected is not. A record quarter built on one buyer is an exposure, not a demand curve.

## Cascade Watch

### Positioning was unwound, not conviction

- **What happened:** The biggest gainers were the deepest one-month losers — Sandisk down 43.7 percent on the month, KLA 40.2 percent, Micron 24.2 percent — while the biggest fallers were last week's biggest winners: Workday up 23.7 percent on the week fell 5.9 percent, ServiceNow up 19.7 percent fell 4.9 percent, Adobe up 16.8 percent fell 5.9 percent.
- **Why it happened:** Short positions in the de-rated chip names were covered and crowded software longs were sold to fund it; no company-specific bad news was found for any of the software names.
- **How it matters:** Treat the "software over silicon" trade the 07-29 brief called dominant as a five-session positioning move now reversed in one — and apply the same scepticism to Thursday's reversal.

### Every rate-sensitive corner of the market fell

- **What happened:** Homebuilders fell 0.15 and 1.15 percent, banks 0.24 and 0.37 percent, transports 1.03 percent, property 1.25 percent, retail 0.90 percent and insurance 1.99 percent, on a day the index rose 1.66 percent.
- **Why it happened:** The thirty-year yield stayed at its 52-week high through a growth miss and cooling inflation; the domestic economy's cost of long money did not fall, so the businesses that depend on it did not rally.
- **How it matters:** The index level is being set by six or seven balance sheets that do not borrow. Any read on "the market" from the index is currently a read on those companies.

### A restatement filed after the stock rose 19 percent

- **What happened:** NuAI (NUAI) closed up 18.82 percent, then filed a material-event notice stating that its first-quarter accounts should no longer be relied upon — an expense misclassification of about $1.4 million, a $23.5 million executive share-grant valuation "inappropriately calculated and understated" with unquantifiable and possibly material effect, an acquisition valuation still under outside review, and an acknowledged breakdown in internal controls.
- **Why it happened:** The filing landed after the close; the tape traded the speculative bid of the day and had no access to the disclosure.
- **How it matters:** The stock is excluded from our drawdown discovery screen from here, and it is a reminder that a deep drawdown plus a violent bounce is a pattern the screen finds attractive and the filings often do not.

### Three buyers confirmed the sellers' story

- **What happened:** Apple said on its earnings call that it paid more for memory in June than in March and expects to pay more again in September. Amazon, also on its call, raised annual capital spending to roughly $220 billion citing higher memory costs — a figure carried by call coverage, not written into its filing. Meta raised its floor citing component pricing, in the filed quarterly report. All three inside thirty hours.
- **Why it happened:** These are hostile witnesses. Each has every commercial incentive to downplay input inflation, and each said so anyway — two out loud to investors, one in writing to the SEC.
- **How it matters:** The evidence for memory scarcity is now first-party and from the demand side, which is stronger than any producer's forecast — and it puts our largest hardware holding on the wrong side of the trade that lifted three others.
