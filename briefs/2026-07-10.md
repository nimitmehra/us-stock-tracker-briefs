## What Happened

### The record was narrow. Its breadth was defensive.

The S&P 500 closed at a record 7,575.39 on Friday, up 0.42 percent and a fourth straight gain — and almost nobody paid for protection, the fear gauge near a one-year low. Underneath, the tape told a different story. The most expensive long-duration software de-rated in near-unison, the cybersecurity and high-multiple names off 3 to 6 percent, while the Russell 2000 small-cap index fell 0.49 percent on a record day for the big indices. Money left growth and rotated into materials, staples and value. Two things held the index green: Meta's 6 percent jump, which alone was the communications sector, and the AI-storage and chip bid. Strip those out and the internals read as an orderly, rate-driven de-risking. The bond market's warning showed up in the stocks, not the index.

### Moderna sold the news, not the science

Moderna (MRNA), the messenger-RNA vaccine maker and a portfolio holding, fell 10.8 percent to $68.27 — the day's worst single name in the book. The cause was not the pipeline. Its August 5 flu-vaccine approval decision and the unanimous 9-to-0 advisory-panel vote behind it are both intact, and no adverse regulatory filing hit the tape. The drop stacked three sentiment blows. An EU contract for up to 24 million RSV-vaccine doses carried no minimum-purchase obligation — revenue the market read as it may never arrive. JPMorgan reaffirmed a Sell and named Moderna among stocks to short. And a broad biotech unwind followed the stock's near-50 percent June run. This was a valuation reset, not a thesis break. The two dated catalysts still stand.

### Meta turned seller of compute; NVIDIA got the Gulf

Two mega-cap holdings carried the winning side. Meta Platforms (META) rose 6.0 percent to $669.21, its best week since early 2024. The lift came from Meta Compute — a new business that sells its AI computing power and models to outside customers. Meta paired it with a claim that it now builds AI capacity at roughly half its earlier cost, reframing the spending the market keeps interrogating as revenue. NVIDIA (NVDA) rose 4.0 percent on reports the Trump administration will ease AI-chip export controls to the United Arab Emirates, opening sovereign-AI demand it had been walled out of. Both moved on their own news, not the tape. They were the day's two clean thesis-strengtheners against a rate-pressured backdrop.

### Bloom's rebuttal is not clearing the short

Bloom Energy (BE), the fuel-cell maker and a portfolio holding up nearly 150 percent this year, fell 4.8 percent to $244.61, extending a slide of more than 20 percent since late June. The pressure is the Hunterbrook Media short report from July 8, which alleges misstated results and a scandium-supply chain too thin to feed Bloom's production ramp. Bloom filed a material-event disclosure rebutting the claims, but it points back to existing filings rather than new figures, so the allegation is contested, not cleared; UBS reaffirmed a Buy at $350 the same week. A short thesis is a hypothesis until the numbers arrive. The July 28 earnings print is where it resolves.
