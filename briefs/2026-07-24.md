The AI selloff rotated one seat down the stack — the memory names crushed, the mega-cap payers steady — but this time bonds refused to confirm it. ## Rates & Macro

The ten-year Treasury — the rate that prices long-dated stocks — closed at 4.69%, down two basis points and off the week's fresh 52-week high. The two-year, the market's read on the Fed, fell four points to 4.33%. But the number that actually marks down AI valuations, the inflation-stripped ten-year, held flat at 2.43%.

So today was a mild tailwind for the AI book, and an incomplete one. The relief came at the front of the curve, where the Fed lives, not in the real long yield that prices far-future profit. Falling oil pulled the July rate-hike scare back, and the front end followed; the real ten-year did not budge.

The exit line is unchanged: the inflation-stripped ten-year above roughly 2.25–2.30 percent, or the two-year pushing toward 4.4–4.5 percent on a hawkish Fed. Today the two-year eased away from its marker, but the real ten-year still sits beyond the danger line. The Federal Reserve's rate committee decides Wednesday, 29 July, with consensus now at a hold at 3.75 percent — the barrel having stopped doing the tightening for it.

## What Happened

### The AI selloff moved to the memory names. Bonds did not follow.

The memory complex took Friday's blow. Micron Technology (MU) fell 6.99 percent to $920.95, Sandisk (SNDK) 10.79 percent — the day's worst Tier 1 move — and Seagate (STX) 6.75 percent, on a feared supply glut and profit-taking after historic runs. Michael Burry, the "Big Short" investor, reportedly doubled down on shorts against Micron and Nvidia. But the mega-caps that pay for the AI buildout steadied — Alphabet (GOOGL) up, Apple (AAPL) leading the market, Microsoft (MSFT) flat — and bonds rallied rather than sold. Thursday's systemic funding scare became a sector rotation, and a discriminating one: Nvidia (NVDA) fell less than a percent while the periphery dropped 7 to 15, and money bought the data-center landlords it sold the chipmakers to own. The market is repricing who wins the buildout, not whether it happens.

### Brent lost $100. The oil-and-rate vise loosened.

Brent crude fell 2.29 percent to $98.38, back below $100 for the first time in a week, with West Texas Intermediate down to $90.47. The driver, reportedly, was an emerging diplomacy track: Oman and Tehran are said to be talking over the Strait of Hormuz, and President Trump, while "locked and loaded," said Iran wants a deal. The war premium that had pushed July rate-hike odds to a coin flip started to bleed out. That is the day's hidden hinge — cheaper oil is why front-end yields fell, and why the funding scare could downgrade to a rotation. Strikes continued for a thirteenth night, so the premium is unwinding on hope, not a ceasefire. The barrel, not the Fed, is still setting the discount rate.

### Intel's beat did not hold.

Yesterday's counter-exhibit reversed. Intel (INTC), which jumped 13 percent after Thursday's close on 25-percent revenue growth, closed Friday's regular session down 7.89 percent at $92.32 — the after-hours pop given back in full. The operating story is intact: revenue rose to $16.1 billion, up 25 percent year on year, and the headline loss remains a non-cash mark on government-held shares, not an operating hole. But the tape did not care. On a day the whole chip complex derated, delivered earnings bought Intel one night, not one session. Thursday's read — that the market still pays for results — did not survive contact with Friday's rotation.

## Cascade Watch

### The memory selloff drove the whole chip node

- **What happened:** Micron's 6.99-percent fall pushed its link to the semiconductor group to maximum strength, and the group cascaded to a dozen more names — memory, optical and equipment stocks down 5 to 15 percent.
- **Why it happened:** A feared oversupply and profit-taking hit the highest-beta corners of the AI-hardware trade, with no new fundamental datapoint behind it.
- **How it matters:** Treat the memory drop as positioning, not a thesis break — chip prices are being raised across the industry the same week the equities sold a glut.

### Oil's slip split the cohort it touches

- **What happened:** Brent's 2.29-percent fall activated the oil-price links to both energy producers and fuel-burning consumers — cruise and airline names rose while the energy complex's war-premium bid faded.
- **Why it happened:** A cheaper barrel is a cost cut for airlines and cruises and a revenue cut for producers — the same event, opposite signs.
- **How it matters:** The energy longs, reporting 31 July, face a give-back if the Iran diplomacy track holds; the travel bid is the confirming cross-current.

### The AI landlord was bought as the AI hardware was sold

- **What happened:** Data-center REITs Digital Realty (+11 percent) and Equinix (+4.9 percent) were bought hard on the same day the semiconductor group fell 3.3 percent.
- **Why it happened:** Falling yields lifted the highest-duration equity sector, and capital chose the infrastructure landlord over the equipment vendor inside the same AI theme.
- **How it matters:** The split is the cleanest read that this is rotation within the AI trade, not an exit — and it seeded a new watch-list name, Digital Realty, for research. This is an emerging cross-asset rotation observation, not yet a graph edge: DLR and EQIX were created this run with empty edge sets, so no activated link sits behind the split.

## Source Tone

The wires split on what Friday was. The bearish read: the AI-receiver trade was a crowded momentum long, and a 7-to-11-percent air pocket with no news is what the exit looks like, with Burry early. The bullish read: nothing broke — oil fell, yields eased, chip prices are rising, and the compute shortage is structural, so this was profit-taking. The day's elegant contradiction is that chip prices were being hiked across the stack — Qualcomm by double digits, Taiwan Semiconductor to follow — on the very morning memory equities sold a feared glut. The pricing thesis and the glut fear crossed on the tape; next week's prints adjudicate.
