## What Happened

### Oil traded the deal. Tehran disputes the one term that matters.

Brent crude closed Friday at $100.21, down 10.6 percent on the week, and crude fell another 5 percent in Monday's thin holiday session toward $91. Equity futures traded it as settled — S&P 500 futures up 1 percent, Nasdaq-100 up 1.4 percent — on a Trump-brokered framework. But nothing is signed. US officials, briefing the Washington Post, Axios and CNN, describe a 60-day ceasefire with the Strait of Hormuz reopened toll-free and Iran surrendering its enriched uranium. Iranian state media contradicts the marquee terms: the strait sits in Iranian waters, tolls are on the table, no nuclear commitment has been made. Trump says the blockade stays until a formal deal. The market is pricing the press conference; the strait is the disputed term. Tuesday's reopen is the riskiest session.

### Warsh takes the chair. The market prices hikes, not cuts.

Kevin Warsh was sworn in as the seventeenth chair of the Federal Reserve on Friday, after the most divisive Senate confirmation in the institution's history (54-45), with President Trump telling him to "be totally independent." Warsh was installed on a cut mandate. The market is not reading him that way. Futures now price rising odds of a 2026 rate rise, September the first possible move, against April inflation near 3.8 percent and consumer sentiment at a record-low 44.8. JPMorgan's base case is rates held through year-end. Thursday's April reading of the Fed's preferred inflation gauge is the last major print before Warsh's first meeting on 16–17 June. That print is the pivot.

### Nvidia's drop was the print, not positioning — and China hardened.

Nvidia Corporation (NVDA) closed Friday at $215.33, down 1.9 percent, the cohort's worst mega-cap performance. Friday's brief read it as positioning. The cleaner read is a "sell the news" fade — the fourth straight — after Wednesday's beat-and-raise: record data-centre revenue of $75.2 billion, up 92 percent, an $80 billion buyback, the dividend lifted twenty-five-fold. The $91 billion guide topped the $87 billion average but missed the ~$96 billion whisper. Two China datapoints hardened the week: a US trade official confirmed chip-export controls were not in last week's talks, and Huawei showed a Shanghai roadmap claiming 1.4-nanometre-equivalent capability by 2031. Jensen Huang, Nvidia's co-founder and chief executive, has said the company "largely conceded" China to Huawei — now the base case, not a risk. The refresh fires Tuesday.
