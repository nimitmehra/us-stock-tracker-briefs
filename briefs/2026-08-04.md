## Rates & Macro

The ten-year Treasury yield, the rate that prices long-dated stocks, closed at 4.63 percent, down seven basis points. The two-year, the market's read on the Fed, fell five to 4.20 percent. The inflation-stripped ten-year — the number that marks down far-future AI profits — fell only three, to 2.40 percent.

That split is the point. Of the seven basis points, roughly three came from real yields and four from lower inflation expectations, which fell to 2.23 percent as crude collapsed. Only the real part reaches valuations. So this was a tailwind for the AI book, but a thin one, and mostly an oil story wearing a rates costume.

The exit line is unchanged: the inflation-stripped ten-year holding above roughly 2.25 to 2.30 percent, or the two-year pushing toward 4.4 to 4.5 percent. Today sits above the first and well clear of the second. Treat it as a calibration, not a promise.

Underneath, three US prints landed soft on the same day — June job openings below expectations, factory orders unexpectedly down, and a trade deficit that narrowed 5.6 percent to $73.3 billion on a trend the coverage itself called unsustainable. All three come from one outlet, and two of them carry no exact figure at all; no dated Fed speaker could be sourced either. Crude fell a second straight session, taking about eleven percent out over two days on talks nobody has signed. The Treasury's refunding statement lands 05 August; Friday's jobs report is the test.

## Cascade Watch

### The AI-power trade broke from the AI trade

- **What happened:** NRG fell 15.48 percent, Vistra 8.15 and Constellation 2.36 on the session Palantir (PLTR) rose 29.45 percent and the semiconductor fund rose 5.55 — a group that has traded together for months splitting in one day.

A customer-funded plant transfers the risk off the generator and takes the upside with it.
- **How it matters:** the break is confined to merchant generators — GE Vernova (GEV), Eaton, Vertiv, Quanta (PWR), Argan (AGX) and Generac (GNRC) all rose — so this reprices the option, not the equipment. It reads through to Bloom Energy, which we hold, and the second session decides whether it is a regime or a day.

- **What happened:** five companies raised full-year guidance and all five produced negative alpha — Rockwell down 9.20 points, Grainger 6.93, TransDigm (TDG) 2.68, BWX 2.66 and Diamondback (FANG) 2.12 — against Caterpillar (CAT) up 3.83 and Zebra 21.49 on equally good prints.

- **How it matters:** a raised guide is no longer a reason to expect a positive day, so a name we own that raises and falls is not evidence against the thesis. ### Breadth broadened and narrowed at the same time

- **What happened:** 360 companies rose against 140, the gap between the cap-weighted and equal-weighted index narrowed for a fourth session to 0.32 points, and the median company gained 0.88 percent — but the gap between mean and median more than doubled to 0.586 points and the momentum quintiles turned U-shaped.

- **How it matters:** Monday's read that breadth was healing does not survive the second measurement. More names participated and fewer names carried the return, which is the signature of a chase and not of a broadening bull market.

### Tariff refunds are now inside reported earnings

- **What happened:** Zebra recorded $73 million of emergency-powers tariff recoveries inside a quarter that showed non-GAAP earnings up 75.9 percent, and rose 26.47 percent; Grainger's 120 basis points of margin expansion was explicitly "inclusive of tariff refunds," with management conceding core profitability was merely in line, and it fell 5.16 percent.

## Source Tone

The wires ran one clean, cheerful story: the first record close since June, roughly 61 percent of the index beating expectations, and oil falling because a deal with Iran is close. Every element is defensible and two things were buried. The first is that the same Tuesday produced three soft US data prints, and a market pricing a September rate rise should not be treating weak labour data as unambiguously good. The second is the content of the Iranian terms. Every outlet led with the Treasury Secretary's "today or tomorrow"; the reported Iranian demand for inbound control and outbound oversight of the Strait appeared as a sourced aside. That is the formalisation of a chokepoint veto, not a restoration — and the crude market has priced eleven percent of relief on the assumption that any deal is a good one. It is also worth recording what the coverage could not do at all: three of the day's largest single-stock moves had no explanation anywhere in the press and a full explanation in the companies' own filings.
