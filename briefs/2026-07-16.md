## What Happened

### Strong chips were sold anyway

Taiwan Semiconductor Manufacturing Company (TSM) reported $40.20 billion of second-quarter revenue, a 67.7% gross margin and 60.3% operating margin. It guided third-quarter revenue to $44.6–45.8 billion and raised 2026 capital spending to $60–64 billion from $52–56 billion. Yet its US shares fell 2.3%, and the semiconductor exchange-traded fund lost 3.7%. Micron Technology (MU), Sandisk (SNDK), Seagate Technology (STX) and Astera Labs (ALAB) fell 5.7–12.6%. The results confirm strong leading-edge demand; the close shows that demand no longer excuses every valuation or cycle risk. A good print met a higher bar.

### Bloom fell without a culprit

Bloom Energy (BE) fell 13.6% to $206.73, its worst one-day move among the daily-watched holdings and 13.7 percentage points behind the industrials sector. Power and data-centre infrastructure peers also weakened, which supports a high-beta AI-power unwind after an extreme prior run. No new Bloom release or credible company catalyst was found before the close, so cause is not established at the company level. The shares remain 750% above their 52-week low despite sitting 40% below their high. Second-quarter results on 28 July are the next scheduled operating test. Large moves can be real before their stories are.

### Fuel costs overwhelmed United's earnings beat

United Airlines Holdings (UAL) reported $17.7 billion of second-quarter revenue and raised full-year adjusted earnings guidance to $9–11 a share. The harder number was fuel: expense rose $2.3 billion, or 84%, from a year earlier, and the company now expects a nearly $6 billion full-year shock. United recovered about half the increase in the quarter and expects 80–90% recovery in the third quarter. Reuters separately reported that Iran threatened wider Gulf-infrastructure retaliation, while the underlying strike claims remained unconfirmed. Pricing power is buying time; fuel is spending it.

### The market cut duration, not growth

The S&P 500 fell 0.5% and the Nasdaq Composite 1.5%, but the session was not broad liquidation. Consumer staples gained 2.8%, regional banks 2.8%, transports 2.8%, health care 2.2% and real estate 2.0%. High-yield bonds were flat, bond volatility fell 2.0%, and small caps were nearly unchanged. Meanwhile, semiconductors lost 3.7%, technology 2.2% and speculative-growth shares 3.7%. The evidence points to exposure reduction in expensive AI, storage and power winners, not an economy-wide funding shock. The market sold the longest promises and kept the nearer cash flows.
