## What Happened

### Bloom and Nebius rewrite the revenue model

Bloom Energy (BE) and Nebius Group signed a ten-year agreement on Friday for more than 300 megawatts of solid-oxide fuel-cell capacity at US AI data centres, valued at up to $2.6 billion and structured around recurring service revenue. The shares had risen 9.06 percent on Thursday, the cohort's largest single-day move, and held within 1.75 percent of the 52-week high through Friday's close at $302.49. The structural piece is the revenue shape. A fuel-cell company that books equipment one-time becomes a fuel-cell company that books service for ten years. Bloom's $350 trim trigger, set on 14 May at thirty-two percent offside, now sits 13.6 percent below. One more strong session arms it.

### Hormuz reopens. Energy refused the bid.

The Trump-Iran framework is reportedly 95 percent agreed. Axios and Fox News, citing administration sources over the weekend, reported a 60-day ceasefire with the Strait of Hormuz reopening inside the window, Iran free to sell oil during the truce, and a negotiated path for the country's roughly 2,000 kilograms of enriched uranium. Brent fell 2.31 percent on Friday to $100.21. The energy ETF (XLE) refused the news in the opposite direction — up 0.61 percent on a down-oil day, the third consecutive session of decoupling. Position energy on refiner-crack and producer-hedge math, not on the spot tape. The diplomacy still has to sign.

### Huang conceded China. The cohort broadened anyway.

Nvidia Corporation (NVDA) chief executive Jensen Huang told CNBC on Thursday the company has "largely conceded" China to Huawei and that investors should "expect nothing" on near-term licence approvals. The Q2 guide of $91 billion was already calibrated ex-China; Huang's framing makes ex-China the base case, not optionality. Shares closed Friday at $215.33, down 1.90 percent — the day's worst-alpha mega-cap. The cohort dollars went elsewhere. Legacy enterprise-IT hardware and mobile-RF semiconductors caught the bid, six names between 10 and 17 percent each, all listed in Significant Moves below. Apple (AAPL) and Meta Platforms (META) carved positive alpha within their sectors, even as Microsoft (MSFT), Alphabet (GOOGL), Micron (MU) and Sandisk (SNDK) joined NVDA as the funding source. The AI trade is broadening, not breaking — the vendor list is getting longer.

### Insiders sold the AI top. Palantir led.

Palantir Technologies (PLTR) filed six Form 4s on Friday covering the previous Tuesday's session. Six executives — chief executive Alex Karp, president Stephen Cohen, chief technology officer Shyam Sankar, chief financial officer David Glazer and two others — sold $125.5 million collectively at the all-time-high band of $132 to $137. Mr Cohen's $43.5 million tranche carried no Rule 10b5-1 reference: discretionary. Mr Karp's $54.1 million tranche cites the plan only for the tax-withholding leg; the open-market component's plan-applicability is unresolved. Palantir is the new face of a wider pattern. CoreWeave (CRWV), Sandisk (SNDK) and others printed C-suite clusters on the same Nvidia-print day; the trailing-two-week cohort tally is near $364 million. The Nvidia date was the coordinating event, not a coincidence.
