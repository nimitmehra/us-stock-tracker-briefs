## What Happened

### Trump cancelled the strikes. Oil round-tripped.

President Donald Trump cancelled the scheduled US strikes on Iran in early-afternoon trading and said a war settlement had been "approved by all parties," with documents to sign "in the next few days." Crude reversed hard — Brent fell 4.1 percent to $89.32, West Texas Intermediate 3.8 percent to $86.64, the lowest since April. The cancellation is confirmed; the multi-party deal is one side's claim, unsigned, with no Iranian confirmation. The market bought it anyway: airlines and cruise lines jumped on fuel relief, energy was the only sector to fall, and the whole war premium unwound in a single session. The signed document is the part the tape has not seen.

### Memory and chips melted up. NVIDIA did not lead.

A furious bid ran through the chip complex, with the semiconductor exchange-traded fund (SMH) up 6.75 percent on twin engines — a Bank of America double-upgrade of Intel and a deepening memory-supply upcycle. Micron Technology (MU), the largest American memory maker, closed up 11.66 percent at $995.87; Sandisk (SNDK) rose 14.5 percent to a record. But the leadership was narrow: NVIDIA Corporation (NVDA) gained just 2.22 percent, lagging its own sector by 4.5 points, as Oracle's vast capital-spending plan stoked worry about who funds the buildout. The tape is paying for scarce memory and turnaround stories, not for the incumbent compute leader.

### Adobe beat and raised. Its finance chief left for a rival.

Adobe (ADBE), the creative-software maker and a watch-list name, posted record second-quarter revenue of $6.62 billion after the close, beat on adjusted earnings, and raised its full-year guide; its AI-first recurring revenue tripled past $500 million. The stock fell about 5.5 percent in after-hours trading anyway, because chief financial officer Daniel Durn is leaving on 15 June to take the same role at chipmaker Marvell. The print answered the cohort's open question — generative AI is feeding creative software, not eating it — yet the market read the mid-cycle departure and a merely-good beat as the signal. The quarter was strong; what got buried carried the load.

## Cascade Watch

### Memory makers rose; the funders fell

- **What happened:** Micron strengthened its links to the AI-buildout and the semiconductor sector — and Adobe its link to the AI-buildout — on the memory melt-up and the beat-and-raise, while Sandisk ripped on the same theme, Microsoft took a high-significance negative mark, and NVIDIA lagged its sector on the same day.
- **Why it happened:** Oracle's vast spending plan is revenue for the chip and storage makers upstream but a cost for the cloud platforms and software names that must fund it.
- **How it matters:** The durable cross-current is bullish AI demand, bearish AI funding — favour the suppliers over the spenders until the financing question resolves.

### The war premium ran in reverse

- **What happened:** The oil-price node took a high-significance negative mark as Brent fell 4.1 percent on the strike cancellation, flipping the nine-name energy-and-fuel cascade — airlines and cruise lines up, energy the only sector down.
- **Why it happened:** Removing the threat of a strike on the world's key oil chokepoint drained the geopolitical premium that had bid crude all week.
- **How it matters:** With a seventh straight weekly inventory draw underneath, the physical tightness persists even as the front-month gave back its spike — the unwind is the war premium, not the supply story.
